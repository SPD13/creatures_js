# Organ #3 — Pancreas (β-cells / Insulin)

## Overview

Organ #3 is the creature's **long-term energy-storage regulator**. Where the stomach, intestines and salivary glands break food down into simple sugars, this organ is the one that decides to **put the leftovers away for later**. It plays the role of the real-life pancreatic β-cells — the insulin-secreting cells that tell the body "there is more glucose than we need right now, store it as glycogen".

It is a small, single-purpose organ, but it sits at the heart of how a creature survives between meals.

---

## In-Game Role

### Turning sugar into reserves

After a creature eats and its food is digested, its bloodstream ends up carrying a lot of **Glucose** — immediate fuel. If that glucose is not used right away, it would simply decay and be wasted. Organ #3 captures it and packs it into **Glycogen**, the creature's long-term energy reserve:

- 6 × Glucose → 1 × Glycogen

This is the reaction that allows a Norn to **survive a period without food**. A well-fed creature builds up a store of glycogen; when it later gets hungry and has no food nearby, other organs can draw on that store to keep it alive and moving.

A creature whose Organ #3 is damaged or starved of energy *looks* healthy for as long as food is plentiful, but will **crash much faster the moment feeding stops**, because its reserves never got built up.

### Self-regulation from body fat

Organ #3 listens to the creature's current **Adipose Tissue** level (its stored body fat). This is the in-game equivalent of the feedback loop that regulates insulin in real animals:

- When fat reserves are already large, the organ moderates its output — no point packing away more energy when there is plenty.
- When fat reserves are low, the organ runs more eagerly, making sure every spare glucose is banked.

The effect on the creature is subtle: a leaner Norn is much more aggressive about converting food into lasting reserves than an already well-padded one.

### Social awareness — the loneliness signal

Somewhat unexpectedly for a metabolic organ, Organ #3 is also a small **social-sensor**. It watches the creature's *crowdedness* — how many other Norns are nearby — and emits the **Loneliness** chemical when the creature is on its own for long enough.

In game this is one of several little mechanisms that nudge solitary Norns to start moving, exploring or seeking company. It is also why a creature left completely alone in a world will, over time, visibly become unhappy even if it is otherwise perfectly fed and healthy.

---

## In-Game Effects Summary

- Converts excess blood sugar into a long-term energy store that keeps a creature alive between meals.
- Regulates itself based on how much fat the creature already carries — lean Norns store more aggressively.
- Is the reason a healthy, well-fed Norn can skip a meal without immediate consequences, while a creature with no reserves collapses quickly.
- Nudges the creature toward social behaviour by producing a Loneliness signal when no one is nearby.

In short: Organ #3 is the quiet "savings account" of the creature's biochemistry — it is almost invisible when full, but a Norn without a working one has no cushion against hunger.

---

## Reactions, Receptors and Emitters

On the standard Starter Parent 1 genome, Organ #3 contains:

| Subsystem   | Count | Main role |
|-------------|-------|-----------|
| Reactions   | 1     | Glucose → Glycogen (long-term energy storage). |
| Receptors   | 1     | Reads the creature's Adipose Tissue level, which modulates the organ's behaviour. |
| Emitters    | 1     | Emits Loneliness based on how crowded the creature is — triggering social drive when alone. |
