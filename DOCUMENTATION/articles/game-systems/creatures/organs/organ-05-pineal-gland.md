# Organ #5 — Pineal Gland

## Overview

Organ #5 is the creature's **sleep-maintenance organ** — the quiet partner to Organ #4. Where the pancreatic glucagon organ is responsible for *starting* a sleep cycle (by producing Sleepase in response to a brain signal), Organ #5 is responsible for *keeping the creature asleep* once it has dozed off, and for slowly letting the loneliness drive settle back down over time.

In real biology this maps best to the **pineal gland**, the small endocrine gland that secretes melatonin during the sleep phase of the day/night cycle.

---

## In-Game Role

### Keeping the creature asleep

When a Norn falls asleep, it is not enough to simply stop being tired for a single moment — the creature has to actually **stay asleep** long enough to recover. Organ #5 is what makes that possible.

Whenever the creature is in the asleep state, Organ #5 releases a steady flow of **Sleepase** into the bloodstream:

- While asleep → Sleepase emitted

Sleepase is the sleep-maintaining chemical that the liver (Organ #2) uses to keep the sleepiness drive topped up. Because this organ only emits while the creature is already asleep, it acts as a **feedback loop**: "if I am asleep, stay asleep a little longer". That is what turns a brief nod into a proper rest.

Working together:

- **Organ #4** (pancreas α-cells) produces Sleepase when the brain *decides* it is time to sleep.
- **Organ #5** (pineal gland) produces more Sleepase as long as the creature *is* sleeping.

Without Organ #5, a Norn may fall asleep but **snap awake almost immediately**, never getting the rest it needs — which cascades into permanent exhaustion, hunger and decline.

### Letting loneliness pass

Organ #5 also acts as a **slow drain on the loneliness drive**. Felt loneliness is continuously moved back into its backup reservoir here:

- Loneliness → Loneliness backup

In practice this means a creature's loneliness does not stay pinned at its peak forever — even if the Norn is still alone, the sharp edge of the feeling softens over time, giving it space to do other things. Without this organ, loneliness would keep accumulating and drown out every other drive.

It is also why a lonely Norn that finds company, or falls asleep, eventually stops *feeling* lonely rather than carrying the emotion indefinitely.

### Responding to body state

Organ #5 listens to a circulatory signal tied to the **Hunger for fat** drive. This gives it a subtle link to the creature's nutritional state — a hungry creature's sleep maintenance behaves slightly differently from a well-fed one's. It is a small effect, but it is part of what makes a starving Norn's sleep feel shallower and more fragile in-game than that of a well-fed one.

---

## In-Game Effects Summary

- Keeps the creature sleeping once it has drifted off — without this organ a Norn falls asleep and immediately wakes back up.
- Maintains the chemical side of the sleep/wake cycle together with Organ #4.
- Slowly drains the loneliness drive so the feeling does not pin forever at maximum.
- Is subtly linked to the creature's fat-hunger state, letting malnutrition colour how well it rests.

In short: Organ #5 is the organ that makes rest actually *count*. It is almost invisible in a healthy creature, but a Norn without a working one never truly recovers from a hard day.

---

## Reactions, Receptors and Emitters

On the standard Starter Parent 1 genome, Organ #5 contains:

| Subsystem   | Count | Main role |
|-------------|-------|-----------|
| Reactions   | 1     | Loneliness → Loneliness backup (slow drain on the loneliness drive). |
| Receptors   | 1     | Reads a circulatory signal tied to Hunger for fat, linking the organ to nutritional state. |
| Emitters    | 1     | Emits Sleepase while the creature is asleep, keeping it asleep. |
