# Organ #2 — Liver (with Immune, Kidney and Detox Functions)

## Overview

Organ #2 is by far the **largest and busiest** organ in a creature. While every other organ specialises in one or two biochemical jobs, Organ #2 single-handedly takes on what, in a real mammal, is split across the **liver, kidneys, immune system and pharmacological detox pathways**. If a Norn is surviving a disease, burning off a toxin, building muscle, sweating in the heat, staying in the mood to mate, or cycling itself into sleep, it is because Organ #2 is quietly doing its job.

It is also, for the same reason, the organ a keeper is most likely to notice when something goes wrong: a sick, poisoned, or malnourished creature is almost always one whose Organ #2 is overloaded.

---

## In-Game Role

### Master metaboliser

Organ #2 is the creature's **protein and energy workshop**. It:

- Breaks proteins down into **amino acids** that the body can reuse.
- Uses amino acids and pyruvate to synthesise **cholesterol**, or reverses that reaction to release them again when needed.
- Spends amino acids to build **muscle tissue**, and tears muscle tissue back down into amino acids when food runs short (this is why a starving Norn visibly weakens — the liver is recycling its own muscle for fuel).
- Uses **anabolic steroid** from the muscles themselves to speed up muscle building during exertion.

This is the reason a well-fed creature with lots of protein in its diet *looks* healthier and more energetic — the liver is turning that food into body.

### Waste management (kidney role)

Whenever amino acids are consumed for energy, they leave behind ammonia — a toxic waste. Organ #2 runs the creature's **urea cycle**:

- 2 × Ammonia + CO₂ → **Urea** + Water

Urea is then combined with **Pistle** (the creature's circulatory-waste chemical) and neutralised. Together these two reactions keep a Norn from poisoning itself with its own metabolic by-products, exactly like a kidney.

### Thermoregulation (sweating)

Hotness and coldness drives interact directly in this organ:

- When both are present, they cancel each other (the creature returns to a comfortable temperature).
- **Water + Hotness → neutralised** — the in-game analogue of sweating. A hot, hydrated creature cools itself faster than a dry one.
- **Pistle + Water → Coldness** — a small cooling effect when the circulatory system is active.

This is why a Norn in a hot part of the world that has recently drunk will recover faster than one that has not.

### Breathing aftermath

The organ processes the gaseous side-products of respiration:

- Excess **dissolved CO₂** is combined into water.
- Any **Water + Air** on hand is recombined into fresh **Oxygen**.

The lungs (Organ #20) supply the oxygen for energy; the liver cleans up the chemistry afterwards.

### Sex drive and libido

Although the gonads (Organ #15) produce the raw hormones, the **feelings** of attraction are computed here:

- **Arousal Potential + Opposite-sex Pheromone → Sex drive** — when an attractive mate is nearby and the creature is fertile, sex drive actually goes up.
- **Libido lowerer** damps sex drive once mating has happened, so the creature is not permanently aroused.
- **Arousal Potential + Libido lowerer → neutralised** — keeps libido from running away.
- **Oestrogen + Progesterone** interactions stabilise female reproductive cycles during pregnancy.

This is the organ responsible for Norn courtship behaviour actually making sense: they only chase partners when both chemistry *and* physiology allow it.

### Sleep and dreaming

Organ #2 is the creature's **sleep gate**:

- The sleep-inducer chemical **Sleepase** (produced elsewhere) combines with a sleepiness reservoir to flip the creature's felt sleepiness on.
- Sleepiness slowly returns to its backup form when the creature rests.

Without this organ, a Norn would never feel tired enough to sleep, or never wake up cleanly.

### Drive buffering

Several drives are **pumped from their felt level back into a backup reservoir** here — Crowded, Boredom, Sex drive, Hunger for protein / carbohydrate / fat, Coldness, Hotness, Tiredness. This is the other side of the valve managed by Organ #1 (the Hypothalamus): where Organ #1 slowly *fills* the active drives, Organ #2 slowly *drains* them once the need has been addressed. Together they keep a creature's emotional and physical state in motion rather than stuck on maximum forever.

### Immune system

Organ #2 contains the creature's entire **innate and adaptive immune response**:

- When an **antigen** (disease marker) enters the bloodstream, this organ reacts to it by producing the matching **antibody**. Every one of the eight antigen types has its own antibody reaction here.
- Each antibody reaction also has a side-effect that a keeper can observe: antigen 0 releases histamine (inflammation), antigen 2 and 3 cause chills, antigens 4 and 6 cause a fever, and antigen 7 is painful. This is why different Norn illnesses *feel* different in-game.
- **Histamine A/B + Antihistamine → neutralised** — allergy response can be chemically suppressed.
- **Prostaglandin** drives inflammation-repair coupling: under stress it is consumed to make fatty acid, and it can be regenerated from amino acid + fatty acid.

### Detoxification and medicine

This is where **drug interactions** happen. Organ #2 is responsible for every common in-game toxin and remedy:

- **Alcohol** is broken down by **Dehydrogenase** — into glucose plus a pain signal (alcohol tolerance has a cost).
- **Anti-oxidant** neutralises **Carbon Monoxide**.
- **Carbon Monoxide + Oxygen** are mutually neutralised.
- **Cyanide** damages energy reserves, and is itself destroyed by **Sodium thiosulphite**.
- **Heavy Metals** are flushed by **EDTA**.
- **ATP Decoupler** drains the creature's ATP directly, and is blocked by **Medicine one**.
- **Geddonase** dissolves adipose tissue into glucose (emergency fuel).
- **Glycotoxin** corrupts glycogen into glucose + cold damage, and is neutralised by **Arnica**.
- Environmental toxins each have their own twist — **Fever toxin** heats the body, **Muscle toxin** floods the system with lactate (soreness), **Sleep toxin** forces sleepiness, and **Fear toxin** produces a direct fear response.

In practice this means Organ #2 is where most of the in-game medicine system actually *happens*. When a keeper applies a remedy to a sick Norn, it is this organ that consumes the toxin and neutralises it.

## In-Game Effects Summary

- Keeps the creature's body built from its food — muscle growth, protein recycling, fat & cholesterol balance.
- Cleans up the metabolic waste that would otherwise poison the creature.
- Cools the body in the heat and stabilises temperature.
- Turns pheromones and hormones into actual sexual attraction, and manages reproductive stability.
- Lets the creature fall asleep, and fade out of sleepiness on waking.
- Drains felt drives once a need is satisfied, keeping mood and motivation dynamic.
- Produces antibodies against the eight disease types — each with its own felt side-effect (fever, chills, pain, itchiness…).
- Breaks down every standard poison, and is the target of every standard medicine.

In short: this is the organ the keeper does not think about until it starts to fail. A creature with a damaged Organ #2 quickly loses its ability to handle food, fight disease, sleep properly or survive poisoning — and usually declines rapidly.

## Reactions, Receptors and Emitters

On the standard Starter Parent 1 genome, Organ #2 contains:

| Subsystem   | Count | Main role |
|-------------|-------|-----------|
| Reactions   | 63    | Metabolic backbone of the creature — protein / amino acid / cholesterol / muscle turnover, urea cycle, thermoregulation, sex-drive chemistry, sleep gating, immune antigen → antibody conversion, and every standard toxin / medicine interaction. |
| Receptors   | 155   | The largest receptor set of any organ: reads drives, organ-level repair and injury signals, reaction-rate modulators across the somatic tissue, and monitors virtually every chemical the creature can produce. |
| Emitters    | 0     | The organ does not emit signals outward — it is purely a *consumer and transformer* of chemistry, letting the results propagate via the circulatory chemical pool. |

That asymmetry (huge intake, no outbound signalling) is a perfect summary of the organ's role: it is the creature's **central chemical workshop**, not a broadcaster.
