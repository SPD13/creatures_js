# 075 - Alcohol

Alcohol is chemical slot 75 in the Creatures 3 chemistry and is the sixth entry in the canonical **bacterial-toxin block** (chemicals 70-81), the range of chemicals that infectious bacteria can be rolled to inject into their host while actively infecting it. Unlike most toxins in the block, Alcohol is also a **generic "intoxicant" chemical** that the stock game exposes to the player through ordinary food and pharmaceutical channels — eating carrot seeds, drinking a potion from the Euro Medicine Maker, or blowing into a raw `CHEM` command will all dose a creature with chemical 75 and make it act drunk. Its mechanical signature is a single **digital receptor** (gene 106) wired onto `LOC_GAIT7`, which flips the creature's walk-animation selector to the "drunken stagger" gait whenever Alcohol in the bloodstream rises above a very low threshold. At the same time Alcohol is slowly metabolised by a single enzymatic-style reaction (gene 81) that consumes two units of Alcohol together with one unit of Dehydrogenase (chemical 116) and yields back one unit of Glucose and one unit of Pain — so drinking gives the creature a small caloric bonus together with a hangover.

The design intent is distinctly **dual-use**: Alcohol behaves like a toxin when a bacterium pumps it into a creature continuously, but it behaves like a recreational / pharmaceutical ingredient when the player hands out a single dose. The effect is deliberately visible and entertaining — a drunk creature staggers around the world with the GAIT7 animation, bumping into scenery and misbehaving, while a hand-fed Strong Alcohol potion (0.45 units, from Medicine Maker script `2 25 16`) will hold that stagger for roughly two minutes of real play before decay and the Dehydrogenase reaction between them clear the chemical. The Pain by-product of the metabolism acts as the in-game "hangover": after the intoxication fades the creature has fresh Pain in its bloodstream, which in turn activates the Pain receptor on `LOC_GAIT1` (gene 100, receptor 192) — so a heavy drinker transitions from a drunken stagger into an injured limp as the body finishes processing the dose.

Because Alcohol has **no antidote reaction** in the standard genome (no Arnica-style clearance, not included in the General Cure potion's documented toxin coverage, and not specifically targeted by any immune pathway), recovery from a large dose depends on two things: the Medium passive half-life of 836 ticks (decay rate 0.99917, ~28 seconds of real play per halving at 30 tps) and the Dehydrogenase-coupled metabolic reaction itself, which runs fast (Short, 21 ticks) but self-limits because Dehydrogenase is a low-pool maintenance chemical with a Long half-life of 6,045 ticks. In effect the Dehydrogenase pool acts as a soft rate-limiter on the creature's ability to metabolise alcohol — small doses clear briskly, but a very large or chronic dose will saturate the available Dehydrogenase and thereafter clear only by passive decay, extending the drunken gait window.

## Role in Game Mechanics

### The GAIT7 receptor: a single digital flip that rewrites the walk animation

Alcohol's entire *behavioural* effect on the creature is mediated through a single digital receptor (gene 106, receptor 184) wired to `LOC_GAIT7` in the Sensorimotor tissue. The mechanics are simple:

- When Alcohol in the bloodstream exceeds 16/255 (≈ 0.063 on the normalised 0-1 chemistry scale), GAIT7 is driven to its gain of 239 (essentially fully saturated).
- The Sensorimotor tissue exposes 16 gait loci (`LOC_GAIT0` through `LOC_GAIT15`). A dedicated "gait decider" in the creature's movement chain picks the gait whose locus has the highest current strength, and uses that gait number to select the animation sequence defined in the creature's skeletal data (see `DOCUMENTATION/articles/game-systems/creature-movement-chain.md`).
- Gait 7 in the stock skeletal data is the **drunken stagger** — a sequence of poses that visibly sways side-to-side with impaired timing.
- Because the receptor is flagged `DIGITAL` (flag 2), there is no gradient: GAIT7 is either fully on (creature fully staggering) or fully off (creature walking normally). A creature is never "a little drunk"; it is either sober-gait or visibly drunk.

The threshold value of 16 is deliberately very low — roughly one-sixteenth of the scale — so that even a single carrot seed (0.1 units injected) can comfortably trip the receptor. This is the intended experience: eating a fermented seed, drinking a mild potion, or being nibbled on by an Alcohol-carrying bacterium should *visibly* affect the creature's walk within a second or two.

### The Dehydrogenase reaction: metabolism with a soft rate-limiter

The one consumption reaction in the standard genome is structured to imitate real-world alcohol metabolism: the enzyme Dehydrogenase (chemical 116) oxidises two units of Alcohol, yielding one unit of Glucose (energy recovered from the fermentation substrate) and one unit of Pain (the metabolic cost / hangover):

```
2× Alcohol [75] + 1× Dehydrogenase [116] → 1× Glucose [3] + 1× Pain [148]
```

The reaction's half-life is **Short (21 ticks, decay rate 0.968)**, making it one of the fastest reactions in the genome. Per activation the reaction consumes two units of Alcohol, so the toxin clears briskly *if Dehydrogenase is available*.

Dehydrogenase itself, however, has:

- **No synthesis reaction** in the standard genome (no emitter and no reaction that produces it).
- **A Long passive half-life** of 6,045 ticks (decay rate 0.99988) — effectively a slowly-replenishing fixed pool that the creature is born with.
- **No receptor** of its own — it has no direct effect on the creature's behaviour, organs or animations; it exists solely as a catalyst-like input to reaction 90.

The consequence is a soft rate-limit on alcohol metabolism. For small doses the Dehydrogenase pool is always larger than the Alcohol concentration, so the reaction runs at Alcohol-limited rate and the creature sobers up within 20-40 seconds of real play. For very large doses (heavy potions, chronic bacterial infection) Alcohol concentration exceeds Dehydrogenase concentration, the reaction becomes Dehydrogenase-limited, and the unreacted Alcohol then has to wait out its Medium passive half-life of 836 ticks (~28 seconds per halving) before falling below the GAIT7 threshold. A heavily-drunk creature therefore stays drunk noticeably longer than a lightly-drunk creature — a pleasing dose-dependent behaviour that emerges naturally from the two-chemical reaction without any special-case code.

### The hangover: Pain by-product and the GAIT1 switch

Every activation of reaction 90 injects one unit of Pain into the bloodstream for every two units of Alcohol consumed. Pain is itself wired to a digital receptor on `LOC_GAIT1` (gene 100, receptor 192, threshold 33, gain 239, DIGITAL) — the "injured limp" gait.

For a mild dose (e.g. 0.1 units of Alcohol from a carrot seed) the total Pain produced is at most ~0.05, which does not cross the Pain→GAIT1 threshold of 33/255 (≈ 0.13), so the creature simply staggers briefly and then returns to a normal walk. For a moderate dose (0.15-0.3 units) the accumulated Pain may cross the GAIT1 threshold briefly as the Alcohol is being metabolised. For a heavy dose (0.45 units from a Strong potion, or a chronic infection) Pain will reliably cross threshold and the creature visibly transitions from a drunken stagger into an injured limp as the Alcohol finishes clearing.

The emergent clinical profile is therefore a three-phase sequence:

1. **Drunken stagger** (GAIT7 active, Alcohol > 16/255): creature sways and weaves.
2. **Mixed / transition** (both GAIT7 and GAIT1 active, or flicking between them as Alcohol drops below threshold and Pain rises above threshold): the gait decider picks whichever locus is higher at the moment.
3. **Injured limp** (GAIT1 active, Pain > 33/255, Alcohol back below threshold): the "hangover" phase as Pain itself decays.

Players who dose a creature heavily are thus "rewarded" with a drawn-out comedic animation sequence, which is clearly part of the design intent for Alcohol as a player-facing chemical.

### Alcohol as a bacterial-toxin-block entry

Despite its player-facing recreational role, Alcohol is structurally a member of the 70-81 bacterial toxin block and can be rolled as the injected chemical for any bacterium in `bacteria.cos`. An Alcohol-carrying chronic infection keeps the creature continuously above the GAIT7 threshold for as long as the bacterium is active — the host is kept permanently staggering, which makes ordinary behaviours (walking to food, navigating to water, interacting with the vendor dispensers) materially harder. Unlike Sleep toxin (which produces unconsciousness) or Glycotoxin (which produces chill), Alcohol does not *prevent* action — the creature can still eat and drink — but its impaired movement makes goal-directed navigation unreliable, and the accumulated Pain by-product of sustained metabolism can eventually push Pain drive high enough to trigger distress signals and negative reinforcement.

The bacterial clearance pathway is the usual one: each bacterium injects a matching antigen (chemicals 82-89) which triggers antibody production (chemicals 102-109). Once antibody concentration exceeds the bacterium's dormancy threshold, the bacterium stops injecting and any residual Alcohol clears through reaction 90 plus passive decay.

### Diagnostic visibility

The Medical Scanner surfaces Alcohol as a named toxin warning: the scanner flags an Alcohol concentration > 0.1 as a "toxin" anomaly (see `DOCUMENTATION/caos_scripts/medical scanner.md`). The Medical Pod's chemistry panel shows chemical 75 by name. Together these diagnostics let the player recognise both acute intoxication (a one-shot carrot-seed or potion dose) and chronic intoxication (an Alcohol-carrying bacterium) before the clinical signs alone become ambiguous. The visible clinical signs that differentiate Alcohol from the other GAIT-modifying chemicals are:

- The creature is walking with the specific GAIT7 stagger animation (not GAIT1 limp, not GAIT2/3 tiredness plod).
- Alcohol > 0 on the chemistry panel or Medical Scanner output.
- Often (after a heavy dose or chronic infection) elevated Pain following the drunken phase.
- No associated drive saturation (hunger, thirst, tiredness all normal) — distinguishing Alcohol from Sleep toxin and Tiredness-mediated gait changes.

### Strategic / gameplay implications

- **Recreational chemistry with real consequences**: Alcohol is explicitly designed to be dispensable to creatures by the player as a gag or party mechanic — the Euro Medicine Maker's Light and Strong potions are branded as "Alcohol" and the Strong potion is clearly a "get drunk" item. The emergent Pain / GAIT1 hangover is a dose-dependent *cost* that players discover by experimenting with high doses.
- **No permanent harm**: unlike the rest of the toxin block, Alcohol causes no organ injury and no drive-saturation attack. Even a very large dose is fully reversible given time, which is why the stock game is willing to package it as a food-seed side effect (carrot seeds) and as a consumer pharmaceutical.
- **Bacterial-infection pain compounding**: an Alcohol-carrying chronic infection is arguably *nastier* than a mild-toxin infection because of the compounding Pain by-product. The creature accumulates Pain continuously as reaction 90 metabolises the incoming dose, and sustained Pain above its own thresholds triggers distress, negative learning reinforcement, and the GAIT1 limp overlay. The creature can end up with chronic low-grade Pain long after any specific drinking episode.
- **Dehydrogenase as a hidden lever**: a community modder who re-tunes the Dehydrogenase pool or adds a synthesis reaction for chemical 116 can make creatures either "lightweights" (very slow to metabolise) or "heavy drinkers" (fast metabolism, minimal stagger window). The stock game's fixed Dehydrogenase pool is what gives Alcohol its characteristic *dose-dependent* clearance curve.
- **Interaction with the skeletal data**: because Alcohol's effect is funnelled entirely through Gait 7, a creature with a custom skeleton that has no Gait 7 animation (or a different animation at slot 7) will not visibly stagger when drunk — the chemistry still runs, but the visible cue is lost. Stock Norns, Grendels and Ettins all ship with a drunken-stagger animation in slot 7.

## Summary

Alcohol is the sixth entry in the bacterial-toxin block (70-81) and the block's most player-facing chemical. It is defined by two wires in the standard genome: a single digital receptor (gene 106) on `LOC_GAIT7` with a very low threshold (16/255, gain 239) that flips the creature's walk animation to a drunken stagger whenever Alcohol is above trace levels, and a single metabolic reaction (gene 81) that consumes two units of Alcohol with one unit of the maintenance enzyme Dehydrogenase (116) to yield Glucose (caloric bonus) and Pain (hangover). The Dehydrogenase pool is a slowly-replenishing, non-synthesised reservoir, so Alcohol clearance is Dehydrogenase-limited for large doses — heavy drinkers stay drunk longer — and once the reaction saturates, residual Alcohol clears through its Medium passive half-life of 836 ticks. Players encounter chemical 75 through four stock-game channels (carrot seeds, Light and Strong Alcohol potions from the Euro Medicine Maker, and bacterial infections with OV16 = 75) and ubiquitously through community-authored food, drink and recreational agents; the characteristic gameplay signature is a drunken-stagger walk transitioning into an injured-limp hangover as the Pain by-product of metabolism accumulates.
