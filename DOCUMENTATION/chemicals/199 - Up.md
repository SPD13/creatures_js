# 199 - Up

**Up** is the first of the five **navigation drives** that occupy chemical slots 199–203 in the C3 / Docking Station genome — the cluster `Up [199]`, `Down [200]`, `Exit [201]`, `Enter [202]`, `Wait [203]` that the chemical-names catalogue explicitly groups under the comment `# navigation drives` at the very top of the placeholder block (`Rebuild/Assets/Catalogue/ChemicalNames.catalogue:27-32`). Functionally, Up is a **drive chemical** — bound to a single drive-bar receptor at locus 15 of the Drives tissue — but it sits *outside* the contiguous main drive bank at slots 148–162. The genome architects placed the four physical-direction drives plus one stillness drive in their own band, immediately above the brain-chemistry slots (Brain chemical 1 [198], Reward [204], Punishment [205], Brain chemical 9 [206]), to keep the locomotion-related signals visually adjacent to the brain bus they share with.

The drive bar that Up feeds is, perversely, **not labelled "Up" in the brain catalogue**: `Rebuild/Assets/Catalogue/Brain.catalogue:95` calls drive neuron 15 `"low down"`. This is the genome's standard convention for naming drives by the *feeling state* the chemical creates rather than by the *action that relieves it*. A creature with a high Up chemical level **feels low down** in its drive lobe, which biases its decision-making toward whatever concept neurons it has learned to associate with ascent — climbing, riding lifts up, stepping onto staircases, reaching upward. The chemical is the urge; the drive bar is the discomfort; the action is whatever the comb→decn associative network has been Hebbian-trained to deliver.

Up's position in the chemical-decay table (`DOCUMENTATION/CreaturesData/biochemistry.json:9112-9119`) marks it as a **Short**-speed chemical: genomeValue 38, half-life 43 ticks (~1.4 seconds at 30 tps), decay rate 0.984 per tick. This is the same decay profile shared by all five navigation drives, and it places them squarely in the "fast-fading urge" regime — much shorter than the main drive bank's typical long half-lives, much longer than the instant-decay brain chemistries at slots 204/205. A pulse of Up that is not topped up will fall by half in ~1.4 seconds and be effectively gone in under 5 seconds. This makes the Up drive a **moment-to-moment pull** rather than a slowly-accumulating need: agents that want a creature to *keep* wanting to go up have to keep pulsing the chemical.

The most architecturally surprising fact about Up — and the entire navigation-drive cluster — is that **the stock genome contains zero biochemistry for it**. There are no emitters writing into chemical 199 (the genome's emitters table at `biochemistry.json:7076` lists 43 emitters and none of them target slots 199–203). There are no reactions producing it (101 reactions, none with chem 199 as a product). There are no reactions consuming it. There are no neuroemitters into it (the single stock neuroemitter at gene 1 writes Adrenalin / Fear / Crowded — not navigation drives). There is no initial-concentration entry, so a hatched Norn is born with Up = 0. The chemical reaches the bloodstream **only via CAOS** — through `CHEM 199 <amount>` from agent scripts (lifts, elevators, climbable objects, the hand-of-help, debug toys), through `ALTR` adjustments, or through modder-added stimulus genes that pulse it on success/failure events. The receptor at the other end is fully wired and functional from Baby; the producer side is left entirely to the agent layer of the game.

This producer/consumer asymmetry is the defining structural fact about Up. It means the drive is, in effect, **a hook the agents use to talk to the creature's decision lobe**. A lift script that wants the creature to learn "this lift is what I use when I want to go up" pulses chem 199 just before or during the ride; the brain's `driv→comb` tract picks up the now-elevated Up drive co-activating with whatever concept the creature is currently attending to (the lift agent itself), and the resulting Reward/Punishment signals from successful arrival reinforce a drive→concept association. Over many lift rides, the creature learns: "when I feel low down (Up drive high), the lift is what I want."

## Role in Game Mechanics

### The "drive name vs chemical name" inversion

The most important interpretive fact about Up is the inversion between the chemical name and the drive-bar name. The genome's two catalogues disagree on what to call the same axis:

- `ChemicalNames.catalogue:275`: chemical 199 = `"Up"` — named for the **direction the creature wants to travel**.
- `Brain.catalogue:95`: drive 15 = `"low down"` — named for the **feeling the creature has** when the chemical is high.

These are two faces of the same need. A creature with `chem[199] = 200` has its Drive 15 receptor produce ~200 (gain 255 amplifies any level), which the `driv` lobe interprets as the feeling "I am low down — I should not be here". The directional verb in the chemical name describes the *resolution*: get up, ascend, climb. This convention runs through the navigation cluster:

| Chem | Chemical name | Drive 15-19 brain name | Meaning |
|------|--------------|--------------------------|---------|
| 199  | Up           | low down                 | "I am too low — I should ascend" |
| 200  | Down         | high up                  | "I am too high — I should descend" |
| 201  | Exit         | trapped                  | "I feel enclosed — I should leave" |
| 202  | Enter        | trapped                  | "I feel exposed — I should enter shelter" |
| 203  | Wait         | patient                  | "I feel restless — I should remain still" |

(The catalogue duplicates `"trapped"` for both Exit and Enter because both drives produce a similar-feeling restlessness; the chemical names disambiguate.)

This naming pattern is identical to the main drive bank: chemical 158 is named `"Fear"` (the chemical-side cause) but drive 10 is named `"scared"` (the felt state). The genome consistently names chemicals as causes and drive bars as feelings.

### Why navigation drives have no biochemistry

The choice to leave Up (and all five navigation drives) without biochemistry-side producers is a deliberate architectural decision that surfaces several constraints:

1. **Altitude is not a chemical fact.** Pain, hunger, temperature, fatigue, loneliness, sex drive, and pregnancy all map onto biological signals a real organism would generate from its body — pain receptors firing, blood-glucose dropping, skin temperature changing, glycogen depleting, social-isolation cortisol rising, hormones cycling. *Altitude relative to a goal* is not a body fact; it is a navigational fact about the world. The genome's biochemistry can express the former but not the latter.
2. **The world is the source of truth.** The C3 / DS world has objects with explicit positions, rooms with explicit elevations, and agents with explicit "this lift goes up" semantics. The natural place to express "the creature is in a world where ascending is rewarded" is in the agent layer that owns those objects, not in the genome that owns the body.
3. **Agents differ across worlds.** A Norn living in the C3 ship has the lift system; a Norn living in a fan-made underwater world might have the SeaLift; a Norn in a Mendel mod might have completely different vertical mechanics. By leaving the producer side of Up to the agents, the genome avoids hard-coding any one world's elevation semantics.
4. **The receptor side is universal.** Every Norn, Grendel, and Ettin in the stock genome has the Drive 15 receptor wired identically from Baby. Whichever world a creature finds itself in, *if* an agent in that world pulses chem 199, the creature will respond in the same way — feel "low down", learn the association with the agent, and seek the agent again next time. The decoupling of producer (world-specific) and consumer (genome-universal) is what makes Up portable.

### How Up gets pulsed in the stock world

Concretely, in the C3 ship and the DS subnet, the Up drive is pulsed by a combination of CAOS script paths:

1. **Lift call buttons.** The `Lifts.cos` family of agents (`Rebuild/Assets/Bootstrap/001 World/Lifts.cos`) provides physical lifts with call buttons. When a Norn uses the call button on a lower floor, the lift script can pulse chem 199 on the Norn (or on the user the lift is responding to) so that arriving at the higher floor feels rewarding to the creature's Up drive — the drive falls on arrival because the script stops pulsing, and the Reward chemistry that fires on successful goal completion reinforces "lift = how to go up".
2. **Stairs / staircase agents.** Climbable staircases pulse chem 199 while the creature is at the bottom and let it fall as the creature climbs.
3. **The hand-of-help (player gesture).** When the player picks up a creature and lifts it visually, hand-of-help scripts can pulse Up so the experience associates with the urge that just got resolved.
4. **Bird / hawk agents.** Carrying agents that lift a creature aerially pulse chem 199 during the carry.
5. **Modder-added vertical agents** — balloons, jetpacks, vines, geysers, lift platforms — all use the same `CHEM 199` injection pattern.

In every case, the script *raises* the chemical and lets the receptor's gain-255 amplification produce a strong Drive 15 reading. The drive bar in the brain rises. The decision-lobe's combination layer is currently attending to the lift / stairs / hand / agent. The `driv→comb` Hebbian pass strengthens the dendrite from drive 15's neuron to the concept neuron representing that agent. The next time the creature is in a low room and feels Up rise (perhaps because a different agent pulses the chemical, or because it has learned to predict the feeling), the trained dendrite fires and biases the decision lobe toward the same concept again — now without needing the original pulse.

### Wiring detail of the receptor

The exact receptor entry from `biochemistry.json:3636-3654`:

```json
{
  "id": 16,
  "geneId": 80,
  "switchOnAge": 0,
  "switchOnStage": "Baby",
  "organ": 1,
  "organName": "Creature",
  "tissue": 5,
  "tissueName": "Drives",
  "locus": 15,
  "locusName": "Drive 15",
  "chemical": 199,
  "chemicalName": "Up",
  "threshold": 0,
  "nominal": 0,
  "gain": 255,
  "flags": 0,
  "flagsDescription": "none"
}
```

Three fields matter for behaviour:

- **`threshold = 0`**: the receptor fires for any chem 199 > 0. There is no minimum activation level. Even a small leftover decay tail still drives Drive 15 a little.
- **`nominal = 0`**: the drive's "satisfied" baseline is zero chemical. The drive is satisfied only when chem 199 is fully gone. There is no homeostatic setpoint above zero — Up is purely a "presence-of-need" signal, not a "deviation-from-target" signal.
- **`gain = 255`**: the maximum possible gain in the receptor system. Together with `flags = 0` (analogue), this means Drive 15's reading is `clamp(gain × (chem - threshold), 0, 255)` = `clamp(255 × chem, 0, 255)`, which saturates at chem 199 ≈ 1 (out of a 0–255 range). Practically: a `CHEM 199 1` write is enough to fully saturate the drive bar; `CHEM 199 200` does not produce any more drive than `CHEM 199 1` would — the receptor is at ceiling for any non-trivial chemical level.

The implication is that **Up is a binary-feeling drive** at the receptor side: it is either silent (chem 199 = 0) or fully on (chem 199 ≥ 1). The chemical's *quantitative* level only affects how long the drive stays active before decay drops it back to zero. A bigger pulse = longer-lasting urge; a smaller pulse = shorter urge.

### Why "Short" half-life matters

The 43-tick half-life (genomeValue 38, ~1.4 seconds at 30 tps) is the second key parameter. With this decay:

- A `CHEM 199 100` injection is at ~50 after 1.4 s, ~25 after 3 s, ~12 after 4.5 s, ~6 after 6 s, ~3 after 7 s, < 1 after ~10 seconds.
- Throughout the first ~7 seconds of decay, the chemical is still > 1 and therefore the receptor is still saturated at gain 255 — Drive 15 stays at ~255.
- After roughly 10 seconds, the chemical falls below the receptor's effective saturation level and Drive 15 begins to decline meaningfully.
- The "felt" duration of one Up pulse is therefore on the order of 10 seconds — long enough for a typical lift ride to play out, short enough that the drive does not linger after arrival.

This timing is what makes the navigation drives feel *responsive*: they spike on the relevant trigger, hold long enough for the brain to do its association work, and fade quickly once the situation is over. Compare to the main drive bank's much longer half-lives (Loneliness, Boredom, etc., which are tuned to last many seconds to minutes between satisfactions): those drives describe persistent biological needs, while Up describes a momentary navigational pull.

### Reinforcement learning and Up

Because Up has no biochemistry-side feedback, the drive **does not learn to satisfy itself the way the metabolic drives do**. Hunger gets reinforced when food consumption raises blood glucose and lowers Hunger via reactions in the glucose pathway. Up gets reinforced only when the agent that pulsed it *also* stops pulsing it after the goal is reached and (typically) writes a Reward chemical pulse. Concretely:

1. Lift script writes `CHEM 199 100` when the creature is at ground level near the lift — Up drive rises.
2. Creature approaches lift; `driv→comb` Hebbian pass strengthens dendrite from drive 15 to lift concept.
3. Lift carries creature up; lift script stops pulsing chem 199.
4. Lift script writes `CHEM 204 50` (Reward) on arrival — the brain's `driv→comb` tract picks up the Reward pulse and applies a positive STW change to the just-strengthened dendrite, locking in the lesson.
5. Chemical 199 decays from its leftover level; within ~10 seconds the drive bar is back to zero.

The agent author is responsible for the timing of all four chemical pulses (Up, off-Up, Reward, off-Reward). A lift that forgets to pulse Reward on arrival will still teach the creature to associate "lift" with "feeling low down", but the association will be weaker; a lift that forgets to *stop* pulsing Up on arrival will leave the creature in a permanently-saturated drive state, which the brain interprets as "this lift did not satisfy my urge to go up" — exactly the wrong lesson. This is one of the subtler bugs that can appear in fan-made vertical-transport agents.

### What Up is *not*

A few clarifications, because the slot's position next to the brain chemistry block (Brain chemical 1, Reward, Punishment) invites confusion:

- **Up is not a brain chemical.** Despite sitting at slot 199 — the second-to-first position in the 198–206 brain-chemistry band — Up is a *drive* chemical. It is read by a Drives-tissue receptor, not by an SVRule operand in the brain. No tract init rule reads `CHEMICAL_CODE[199]` (verified in `brain-architecture.json`: every appearance of arrayIndex 199 is in `VALUE_CODE` or `NEGATIVE_VALUE_CODE` operands, where the index is being used as a *numeric constant* equal to 199, not as a chemical lookup). The chemical's effect on the brain is entirely indirect, through the drive-lobe input.
- **Up is not a verb.** The decision-lobe action catalogue has 14 verbs (look, push, pull, deactivate, approach, retreat, get, drop, express, rest, left, right, eat, hit). There is no "go up" verb. The creature cannot decide "I will travel up"; it can only decide to approach a learned upward-travel object.
- **Up is not a left-right pair with Down.** Although Up [199] and Down [200] are conceptual opposites, the genome does **not** contain an annihilation reaction (the way Coldness ↔ Hotness cancel via reaction 23). High Up and high Down can co-exist in the bloodstream simultaneously — both drive bars will be saturated, and the brain will simultaneously feel "low down" and "high up". The behavioural disambiguation happens entirely in the trained `driv→comb` associations, not in chemistry. (In practice, agents do not pulse both at once because no real-world situation calls for it.)
- **Up is not used by the per-tract reward system.** The opcode-59 / opcode-62 mechanism (`SET_REWARD_CHEMICAL_INDEX` / `SET_PUNISHMENT_CHEMICAL_INDEX`) reads chemicals 204 and 205 — Reward and Punishment. Chemical 199 plays no role in the generic reinforcement plumbing; it is a drive input, not a learning signal.
- **Up is not pulsed by any stock stimulus gene.** Unlike Brain chemical 1 [198] (pulsed by `STIM_DISAPPOINT`), Reward [204] and Punishment [205] (pulsed by all the success / failure stimuli), Up has *no stock stimulus producer*. The genome leaves the producer side entirely to the agent layer.

### Modding affordances

Up is one of the cleanest extension points in the genome for behavioural modders, precisely because the producer side is empty:

- **Add a "homing-altitude" emitter.** A modded `G_EMITTER` reading the creature's current altitude (via a custom CAOS-managed locus) and writing chem 199 when altitude < home_altitude turns the agent-layer-only Up drive into a true endogenous homing drive. Combined with a Down emitter that fires when altitude > home_altitude, the creature would have a real two-axis altitude homing chemistry.
- **Add a "stuck below ceiling" stimulus.** A `STIM_BELOW_CEILING` gene that pulses chem 199 every few ticks while the creature is in a low-permeability ceiling room would give the creature a slow-rising Up urge in confined ground areas.
- **Wire navigation drives into the main reaction graph.** Modded reactions like `Up + Reward → Reward` (catalytic destruction of Up by successful arrival) or `Coldness → Up` (cold rooms below produce upward urge) fold the navigation drives into the main chemistry network. None exist in stock C3.
- **Add navigation backup chemicals.** Genetic engineers reserving five free slots can replicate the active/backup sweep+drip pattern from the main drive bank, giving the navigation drives chemical memory across stretches of agent inactivity.
- **Repurpose the slot.** Because the chemical has no stock biochemistry, modders can repurpose chem 199 entirely (e.g. as a "magic mana" axis, a "psionic energy" axis, a "homesickness" supplementary axis) without colliding with any stock receptor / emitter / reaction. Only the agent-layer scripts that write `CHEM 199` need to be aware of the repurpose.

### Practical consequences for gameplay

- **Norns in barren worlds never feel "low down".** A Norn loaded into a custom world that has no agents pulsing chem 199 will have Drive 15 = 0 forever, regardless of how low or trapped it is in physical reality. The drive is not a sense organ; it is a script-driven cue. Builders of new worlds need to add agents that pulse the navigation drives if they want the creatures' brains to engage with vertical navigation.
- **Creatures learn lifts, not altitude.** The Hebbian association is from drive-15 neuron to *concept* neurons (lift, stair, hand, etc.), not to *positions*. A Norn that has learned to ride lift A in room R will still need to learn lift B in room S separately, unless the two lifts are categorised as the same concept in the noun lobe (which depends on their classifier and stimulus profiles).
- **Disabled lift scripts silently disable the drive.** If a world's lift agents are removed or fail to install (e.g. due to a CAOS error in their install script), the Up drive stops being pulsed. There is no biochemistry redundancy to mask the loss — the creatures simply never feel low down again. This is the inverse of the disappointment-pipeline failure mode at chem 198: a broken agent silently mutes the input rather than producing wrong learning.
- **Tools / debug toys can prime the drive directly.** A Science Kit or debug tool that writes `CHEM 199 100` to the selected creature provides a one-click way to test that the Drive 15 receptor and `driv→comb` learning are wired correctly. The drive bar should saturate within one tick and decay over ~10 seconds; the brain should accept the drive as input and bias decision-making toward whatever concepts the creature is currently attending to.
- **Watching chem 199 in Kits diagnoses the navigation pipeline.** A creature in an active world should show occasional chem 199 spikes whenever a vertical-navigation agent pulses it. A flat-zero trace in a world that *should* be pulsing the chemical (e.g. the C3 ship, where lifts are everywhere) is a red-flag that the lift agents have failed to install or that the creature's targeting mechanism is broken.

### JS port notes

The Rebuild port treats chemical 199 as an ordinary bloodstream chemical with no special-case handling:

- **No `CHEM_UP` constant.** The chemical is referenced numerically by genome data only. There is no engine-level enum entry for chemical 199, no special path, no built-in producer.
- **The drive locus must be writable from the receptor.** `Rebuild/Main_Game/src/engine/creature/Creature.js:119` allocates `myDriveLoci` as a `Float32Array(NUM_DRIVES)` of size 20. The Drives-tissue receptor for chem 199 writes to `myDriveLoci[15]` via the standard receptor evaluator. `getDriveLevel(15)` returns this float, and `SensoryFaculty.updateDriveLobe()` propagates it to brain input `('driv', 15)`. Any port-side bug that mis-indexes drive locus 15 (e.g. an off-by-one in the LOC_DRIVE0 base address, see `Creature.js:247`) silently breaks the Up drive.
- **The decay must implement the "Short" half-life correctly.** Half-life 43 ticks, decay rate 0.98399, must be applied every biochemistry tick. Because the receptor saturates at extremely low chemical levels (gain 255), small numeric errors in the decay loop accumulate visibly in the drive bar — a port that uses 0.984 instead of 0.98399 will produce a drive that decays slightly too fast, with visible mis-timing on lift-ride teaching events.
- **`CHEM 199` and `ALTR 199` must reach the same `myChemicalConcs[199]` slot the receptor reads.** This is the standard `getChemicalConcs()` live-reference invariant (see Brain chemical 1 docs) — the receptor's `pointerToChemicals[199]` must be the same memory the CAOS commands modify.
- **The drive 15 brain neuron's "low down" name is purely informational.** The brain catalogue's `"low down"` label is loaded from `Brain.catalogue` and used only for debug displays and Kits — the brain itself addresses the neuron by integer index 15. A port that fails to load the catalogue still has a fully-functional drive system; it just shows generic neuron numbers in the debug UI.

The most likely class of port bug specific to the navigation drives is **agent-side**: if the lift / elevator / climbable-object scripts in the bootstrap COS files fail to install correctly, the chemical never gets pulsed and the drive never engages. The receptor side is straightforward and shares its evaluator with all other drive receptors, so receptor-side bugs would manifest in *all* drives, not just Up.

                       ▼ (after ~10 seconds without re-pulse)

       creature no longer feels "low down" — the urge has subsided

     - Agent-layer hook for teaching creatures to seek vertical-travel
