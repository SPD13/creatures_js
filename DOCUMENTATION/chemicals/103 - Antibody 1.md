# 103 - Antibody 1

Antibody 1 is chemical slot 103 in the Creatures 3 chemistry and the second entry in the canonical **antibody block** (chemicals 102-109, Antibody 0 through Antibody 7). Each antibody is paired one-to-one with an antigen in the 82-89 block — Antibody 1 is specifically the immune response to [Antigen 1 (083)](083%20-%20Antigen%201.md), the **"cough antigen"** carried by one of the two most statistically common classes of bacterial invader. While antigens represent bacterial "fingerprints" and histamines the symptomatic by-products of immune reactions, antibodies are the **cumulative memory** of the creature's adaptive immune system: a rising Antibody 1 level records that the host has been fighting an Antigen-1 infection, and — crucially — is the chemical signal that the attacking bacterium itself reads to decide whether to keep injecting antigen or to go dormant.

Antibody 1 is produced endogenously by **reaction 93** (gene 86), the core Antigen-1 immune response:

```
2× Antigen 1 [83] → 12× Antibody 1 [103] + 1× Histamine A [73]
```

Each firing of this reaction amplifies two units of antigen into twelve units of antibody — a 6× amplification that lets the creature build up a decisive antibody concentration against a chronic source of Antigen 1. The reaction runs at a **Short half-life (58 ticks, decay rate 0.988)**, so antibody production kicks in within a second or two of a bacterium beginning to inject antigen into the host. The Histamine A by-product is what wires this reaction into the player's perception of the infection: every firing produces one unit of [Histamine A (073)](073%20-%20Histamine%20A.md), which crosses the LOC_INVOLUNTARY2 (Cough) receptor threshold and drives the creature's audible, visually-distinctive **cough reflex**. Antibody 1 is therefore the "cough antibody" — the quiet, receptor-less counterpart to the noisy, visible Histamine A cough symptom it is always produced alongside.

Antibody 1 also has a **baseline initial concentration of 52 units** set by gene 17 (concentration 0.2039), so every newborn norn, grendel or ettin starts life with a pre-seeded antibody pool against the cough antigen. This starter dose is smaller than Antibody 0's 96-unit baseline — reflecting a design choice to give Antigen 0 (the sneeze antigen) a slightly larger head start than Antigen 1, even though the two are statistically equally likely to be encountered — but it still meaningfully raises the bar against a creature's first cough-carrying bacterium.

The chemical has **no receptors anywhere in the creature**: no organ locus reads chemical 103, no drive is modulated by its concentration, no metabolic process depends on it, no injury wire is triggered. (The single entry in the receptor table with `"id": 103` is a LOC_AGE2 Adolescent→Youth receptor on *Life* (chemical 125), completely unrelated to Antibody 1 — the numeric collision is an index artefact.) This is the same "pure signalling" design used for every antibody in the block (102-109) and for the histamine antidote (Antihistamine, 100): the antibody does nothing to the creature itself. Its entire game-mechanical footprint is **external**: the bacterium agent (`bacteria.cos`, family/genus/species `2 32 23`) reads the host's chemical slot 103 directly via the CAOS `chem` primitive (polling `chem host (ov15 + 20)`, which resolves to slot 103 whenever the bacterium's rolled antigen is Antigen 1), and uses that value to decide whether it is being successfully suppressed by the immune response. When the antibody level crosses the bacterium's randomly-rolled dormancy threshold (`ov13`), the bacterium goes dormant and stops injecting Antigen 1 and its secondary toxin; when antibody later decays below the bacterium's wake threshold (`ov14`), the bacterium may resume active infection. Antibody 1 is therefore the **decisive lever** that determines whether an Antigen-1 infection is winning or losing — from the *bacterium's* point of view, not the creature's — which in Creatures 3's economy of adaptive immunity is the same thing.

Antibody 1 clears from the bloodstream exclusively through passive decay, at a **Long half-life of 2,481 ticks** (decay rate 0.99972, ~1 min 23 s of real play per halving at 30 tps) — the same decay profile shared by every antibody in the block. There is no reaction in the standard genome that consumes Antibody 1 and no antidote that cancels it: it can only fade over time. This Long-but-not-indefinite decay is a carefully tuned design choice: it keeps antibody levels elevated long enough to **suppress a bacterium for minutes after the antigen has been cleared**, providing durable immunity against a reinfection, but not so long that a creature remains permanently immune to every Antigen-1 exposure it has ever experienced. A creature that fought off a cough bacterium five minutes ago will still be carrying measurable Antibody 1 and will therefore win any new exposure much faster than a creature fighting its first infection; a creature that fought one off an hour ago has decayed back to near-baseline and fights the next one essentially from scratch (plus its 52-unit genomic starter dose). This is the biochemical engine behind the player-observable pattern that "older norns cough less often than young ones".

## Role in Game Mechanics

### The antibody-bacterium dialog: external chemistry as game state

The most interesting design pattern in Antibody 1's mechanics is that its primary consumer is not a receptor on the creature itself but **the bacterium agent as an external reader**. The `bacteria.cos` script (documented in `caos_scripts/bacteria.md`) polls the host's chemical 103 on every timer tick (via the `chem host (ov15 + 20)` pattern, which resolves to slot 103 when `ov15 = 83`) and uses the value to steer the bacterium's state machine:

| Antibody 1 level | Bacterium behaviour | Creature experience |
|------------------|---------------------|---------------------|
| Low (baseline 52 units, or rapidly decaying) | Active infection, injects Antigen 1 + toxin | Cough reflex firing, slow organ injury on five somatic receptors |
| Rising (reaction 93 fires repeatedly) | Still actively infecting, but taking lifespan drain from antibody | Cough continues; bacterium's life is shortening |
| Crosses dormancy threshold (`ov13`) | Transition: bacterium goes dormant, stops injecting | Cough fades over ~seconds as Histamine A decays; antigen fades over ~minutes |
| High and sustained | Bacterium stays dormant; lifespan drain continues each tick | "Recovered": no cough, no organ damage. Bacterium may eventually die from lifespan drain |
| Falls below wake threshold (`ov14`) after decay | Bacterium re-activates, resumes injecting | Cough returns, cycle restarts |

This creates a dynamic feedback loop: the creature's immune response (reaction 93) produces antibody, which suppresses the bacterium, which stops producing antigen, which stops reaction 93, which allows antibody to passively decay, which eventually lets the bacterium wake up again. In practice, most bacterial infections either win decisively (the bacterium dies of lifespan drain before antibody decays) or lose decisively (the player kills the bacterium with anti-bacterial spray), so the oscillation is usually damped rather than periodic — but the underlying chemistry permits recurrent infections from a single persistent bacterium, which is the biochemistry behind a chronic, recurring "cough" in a creature with slow antibody production.

### The cough signature: why Antibody 1 is "the quiet half" of the immune response

Antibody 1 is always produced alongside Histamine A (reaction 93's by-product), but the two chemicals have dramatically different player visibility. Histamine A drives the LOC_INVOLUNTARY2 cough reflex (receptor 70, threshold 16, gain 255), is named in the Medical Pod's toxin panel, and has a dedicated antidote reaction (reaction 74, `Histamine A + Antihistamine → nothing`, consumed by the Cough Syrup and General Cure potions). Antibody 1, in contrast, has no receptors, no symptom, no UI surfacing, and no antidote. The two chemicals are produced by the same reaction in the same stoichiometry — but one of them is the noisy public face of the immune response (a coughing creature), and the other is the silent workhorse that actually causes the infection to resolve.

This design pattern — **noisy by-product + silent effective agent** — is intentional. If Antibody 1 itself drove a receptor, players would have two competing symptoms during every cough infection, making it harder to read the creature's state. Instead, the histamine is the symptom (short half-life, visible, treatable) and the antibody is the memory (long half-life, invisible, self-clearing). A player treating a coughing creature with Cough Syrup burns down the Histamine A in seconds and the cough reflex stops — but the Antibody 1 pool that actually suppresses the bacterium is untouched by the syrup, because reaction 74 only neutralises Histamine A. So the cough disappears, the bacterium is still being suppressed by the accumulated antibody, and the creature remains "immune" for the next few minutes. This is exactly the real-world relationship between symptoms (what antihistamines treat) and adaptive immunity (what they don't).

### The 6× amplification: decisive antibody build-up

Reaction 93's stoichiometry — two units of Antigen 1 in, twelve units of Antibody 1 out — embeds a **6× amplification factor** in the antibody-manufacturing pathway, shared only with reaction 92 (the Antigen-0 response). Consider a bacterium injecting Antigen 1 at 0.02 units per tick: assuming a steady-state where reaction 93 consumes the antigen as fast as it is injected, the reaction fires once every 100 ticks (to accumulate the 2-unit reactant requirement), producing 12 units of antibody per firing, or 0.12 antibody units per tick. That sounds slow, but Antibody 1 has a Long passive decay (2,481 ticks, decay rate 0.99972), so the antibody accumulates rather than rapidly clearing. Over 10 minutes of sustained infection (~18,000 ticks), a creature can build hundreds of units of antibody above its baseline — more than enough to suppress even a high-dormancy-threshold bacterium.

This amplification is why chronic infections eventually become self-limiting in Creatures 3: the bacterium continuously injects small doses of antigen that would, on a 1:1 conversion basis, be quickly cleared — but the 6× amplification accumulates a decisive antibody pool faster than the bacterium can escape it. The asymmetry (antigen decays Long at 1,670 ticks, antibody decays Long at 2,481 ticks) further favours the creature: antibody persists about 50% longer than antigen, so even when the bacterium has gone dormant and stopped producing antigen, the antibody pool it provoked lingers for a proportionally longer time, keeping the bacterium suppressed.

Antibody 1 and Antibody 0 are the only two antibodies in the block with this 6× amplification. The other antibody reactions have lower or even sub-unity amplifications (reaction 94 for Antibody 2 is 16:12, a 0.75× *attenuation*), because their associated symptoms are less player-visible and the design does not need to rush them into suppression. Antibodies 0 and 1 together form the "respiratory pair" — the two most aggressively-amplified immune reactions in the genome, reflecting the design priority of quickly suppressing the sneeze and cough symptoms that dominate player perception of bacterial disease.

### Long decay and the memory of infection

The 2,481-tick passive half-life (~1 min 23 s at 30 tps) is carefully tuned to balance two competing design goals:

- **Memory of exposure**: antibody levels should persist long enough that a creature suppressing a bacterium actually "remembers" the exposure and can fight off a reinfection more easily. A half-life of 2,481 ticks means that one hour (108,000 ticks, ~43 half-lives) after the original exposure, antibody has decayed by a factor of ~2⁴³ ≈ 10¹³ — effectively zero. Five minutes (9,000 ticks, ~3.6 half-lives) after exposure, antibody has only decayed by a factor of ~12, so a creature with a peak of 500 units is still carrying ~40 units of residual antibody, meaningfully above the 52-unit baseline. This creates a "short-term acquired immunity" window of a few minutes to a quarter-hour after a successful infection suppression.
- **Long-term reinfection possibility**: on time scales of a full in-game day or longer, antibody must decay back to baseline so that a creature can realistically be reinfected. The Long half-life achieves this: even peak antibody levels fade completely within an hour or two, so a creature's long-term immunity is not permanent.

The Long-but-finite decay therefore implements a realistic adaptive-immunity model: exposure confers meaningful short-term protection that slowly fades, allowing reinfection over long spans of creature life. Applied to the cough infection specifically, this means a creature recovering from a cough bacterium will resist the next cough bacterium for several minutes of strong immunity and a quarter-hour of weakening immunity — enough to keep a healthy population from being repeatedly hammered by the same class of infection while the immune system recovers.

### No creature-side receptors: why the creature doesn't "feel" its own antibodies

Antibody 1 has zero receptors on any creature locus — the creature cannot sense its own antibody level, and no drive, reflex or metabolic process depends on it. This may seem surprising (real-world antibodies trigger measurable physiological responses) but it is intentional for two reasons:

1. **Separation of concerns**: the chemistry handles the biochemical bookkeeping (antibody accumulation and decay), and the bacterium agent handles the game-state effect (dormancy and lifespan drain). Adding a creature-side receptor would duplicate functionality and risk interference between the two systems.
2. **Prevents overdose side effects**: because there is no receptor, a player can safely dose a creature with arbitrary amounts of Antibody 1 (via a community "immune booster" agent or via CAOS injection) without side effects. The excess simply suppresses whatever bacteria are present more decisively and then decays passively. This makes community pharma content safe to compose.

The contrast with Histamine A (the co-product of reaction 93) is instructive: Histamine A has a LOC_INVOLUNTARY2 reflex receptor because it is a *symptomatic* by-product that needs to drive a visible reflex; Antibody 1 is an *effective* chemical that needs to drive the bacterium's state machine, so it bypasses the receptor system entirely and talks directly to the external agent.

### The antibody block as a whole

Antibody 1 shares the antibody block's uniform design characteristics — Long 2,481-tick decay, no receptors, no emitters, a genomic starter dose — with all seven of its siblings. The block's structure mirrors the antigen block 82-89 one-to-one:

Antibody 1 sits in the "elite" tier of the block alongside Antibody 0: both are manufactured via 6×-amplification reactions, both produce an audible respiratory reflex as a by-product, and both have meaningfully large starter doses in the genome. Antibody 1 is effectively the "co-lead" of the immune response — slightly less amplified (58-tick vs 52-tick reaction half-life) and with a smaller starter baseline (52 vs 96), but otherwise structurally identical to Antibody 0.

### Strategic / gameplay implications

- **Starter dose matters for newborn survival**: the 52-unit baseline Antibody 1 means newborn creatures have moderate first-bacterium resistance against cough infections. The smaller baseline (compared to Antibody 0's 96) means a baby norn is more vulnerable to its first cough than to its first sneeze, but still noticeably more resistant than to any of the non-respiratory infections (Antibodies 2-7 have smaller or no starter doses).
- **The cough-antibody dissociation**: because Histamine A (cough) and Antibody 1 (immune memory) are produced by the same reaction in the same stoichiometry, the chemistry panel shows *both* rising during a cough infection — but they clear at completely different rates. Cough Syrup / Antihistamine collapses Histamine A in seconds while leaving Antibody 1 untouched. Players who understand this can strategically treat *symptoms* with Cough Syrup while *letting the immune response build* undisturbed, which is the optimal treatment pattern: symptomatic relief without losing the acquired-immunity benefit.
- **"Immune memory" is real for coughs too**: a creature that fought a cough bacterium five minutes ago carries residual Antibody 1 that will immediately suppress any new Antigen-1 bacterium it encounters. Player folklore around "my old norn doesn't catch colds as often" is genuinely grounded in the Long passive decay of Antibody 1 preserving post-exposure immunity across the short term.
- **Anti-bacterial spray kills the bacterium, but antibodies linger**: after a player uses the anti-bacterial spray to kill an Antigen-1 bacterium, Antigen 1 levels drop, reaction 93 stops firing, and Histamine A decays within seconds. But the accumulated Antibody 1 persists for ~1.5 minutes per half-life, providing durable post-infection coverage that makes subsequent cough exposures much easier to fight.
- **Community cough-vaccine agents are viable**: because Antibody 1 has no receptors and no side effects, modders can ship "cough vaccine" or "throat lozenge" agents that dose a target creature with chemical 103 directly. This confers immediate immunity against Antigen-1 bacteria without triggering the cough reflex at all. The community "maternal care" and "baby immune-booster" agents typically rely on this pattern alongside chemical 102 dosing.
- **Slightly higher somatic cost per unit infection**: because Antigen 1 carries five somatic injury receptors (versus Antigen 0's four), an unsuppressed cough infection is marginally more damaging to the creature's organs than an unsuppressed sneeze infection. This raises the stakes of building Antibody 1 decisively — a failed Antigen-1 immune response costs slightly more somatic integrity than a failed Antigen-0 response.

### Diagnostic visibility

Antibody 1 is not named in the Medical Pod's toxin panel (it is not a toxin), but it is visible via:

- **Chemistry panel (slot 103)**: direct numeric readout, showing the current antibody level.
- **Debug console**: `CHEM TARG 103` returns the current concentration on demand.
- **Indirect inference via Histamine A**: because Histamine A is produced 1:1 with Antibody 1 in reaction 93, a rising Histamine A reading on the Medical Pod is also a reliable indicator that Antibody 1 is rising in parallel. Player diagnostic intuition ("the cough is getting worse") is therefore actually a sign of *successful* immune activation, not of failing health.
- **Bacterium behaviour inference**: if a bacterium on a creature suddenly stops producing cough symptoms (coughing stops, antigen fades), the player can infer that Antibody 1 has crossed the bacterium's dormancy threshold, even without looking at the raw chemistry.

The clinical signature of a creature mid-cough-infection is: rising Antibody 1, elevated Antigen 1, elevated Histamine A, cough reflex firing, mild tissue damage on five somatic organs. The signature of a creature post-infection is: high Antibody 1, near-baseline Antigen 1, near-baseline Histamine A, no cough — and the residual high Antibody 1 is what gives the creature its short-term acquired immunity against the next Antigen-1 bacterium.

## Summary

Antibody 1 is chemical 103 and the dedicated immune response product for [Antigen 1 (083)](083%20-%20Antigen%201.md), the "cough antigen" carried by one of the two most commonly encountered classes of bacterial invader in Creatures 3. It is produced by **reaction 93** (gene 86, `2× Antigen 1 → 12× Antibody 1 + 1× Histamine A`, Short half-life of 58 ticks), starts every creature's life at a **baseline 52-unit concentration** set by gene 17 (smaller than Antibody 0's 96-unit baseline but still meaningful), and clears only via a **Long passive decay** of 2,481 ticks (~1 min 23 s per halving at 30 tps). It has **no receptors** on the creature — no locus reads its concentration, it affects no drive or reflex or metabolic process. Its entire game-mechanical effect is to be read by the external `bacteria.cos` agent: when the host's Antibody 1 rises above the bacterium's randomly-rolled dormancy threshold (`ov13`), the bacterium goes dormant and stops injecting antigen and toxin; and each tick of elevated antibody additionally drains the bacterium's remaining lifespan by `antibody × 10`, so sustained high antibody eventually kills the bacterium outright. The **6× amplification** of reaction 93 (shared only with reaction 92 for Antibody 0) makes this one of the two most aggressively-ramping antibody responses in the stock genome, reflecting Antigen 1's status as "the cough antigen" — one of the two most commonly encountered and visibly symptomatic bacterial fingerprints. The reaction's co-product, Histamine A, is the noisy public face of the immune response (driving the cough reflex via LOC_INVOLUNTARY2), while Antibody 1 itself is the silent, receptor-less workhorse that actually causes the infection to resolve. The Long-but-finite passive decay implements a realistic adaptive-immunity model: creatures retain meaningful post-exposure cough immunity for minutes-to-quarter-hours and fade to baseline over an hour or two, so short-term reinfection is hard and long-term reinfection is possible. With no creature-side receptors, no antidote reactions and no direct physiological effect, Antibody 1 is a textbook example of Creatures 3's modular chemistry design: a pure "signalling" chemical whose meaning lives entirely in the communication between the biology genome (reaction 93, gene 17) and the external agent ecosystem (`bacteria.cos` reading `chem 103`). Player-side, it is the truest indicator of whether a cough infection is being won or lost — the numeric heartbeat of the creature's adaptive immune response to its second-most-common respiratory pathogen.
