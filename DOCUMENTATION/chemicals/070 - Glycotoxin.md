# 070 - Glycotoxin

Glycotoxin is chemical slot 70 in the Creatures 3 chemistry and occupies the first position in the canonical **bacterial-toxin block** (chemicals 70-81), the range of chemicals that infectious bacteria can be rolled to inject into their host while actively infecting it. Its in-game role is a dual-purpose poison: its name ("glyco-toxin") advertises its primary effect — a fast, offensive reaction that **raids the creature's Glycogen store** and converts it into a reduced amount of Glucose plus Coldness as a side-effect — while a second, slower effect **directly injures a somatic organ** through a receptor wired to the `RLOCUS_INJURY` locus. The combination makes Glycotoxin a "metabolic thief plus organ poison" rather than a pure sedative or a pure organ destroyer: it robs the creature of its carbohydrate reserves while also causing creeping tissue damage, and it advertises its presence through a chill (Coldness is a drive chemical) that both the creature and any nearby diagnostic agents can sense.

Unlike the classic "environmental toxin" block (Heavy Metals, Cyanide, Belladonna, Geddonase at chemicals 66-69), Glycotoxin does have a canonical antidote pathway in the standard genome: reaction 89 consumes 1× Glycotoxin with 1× **Arnica [97]** and produces nothing, giving the General Cure potion (and Arnica-rich home remedies) a working biochemical handle on the poison. Glycotoxin is also explicitly named in the General Cure's documented toxin list — *"Histamine A & B, cyanide, carbon monoxide, ATP decoupler, heavy metals and glycotoxin"* — so a creature dosed with Glycotoxin can be treated pharmacologically, in contrast to Belladonna, which has no cure at all. Passive decay is **Long** at 3,686 ticks (decay rate 0.99981, ~2 minutes of real play at 30 tps), so without treatment an untreated creature will carry a meaningful Glycotoxin load for several minutes while the raid-reaction eats its Glycogen and the injury receptor slowly damages a somatic organ.

The primary stock-game delivery vector is the **bacterium agent family** (family/genus/species 2 32 23 in `bacteria.cos`), which rolls a random toxin from the 70-81 range for each bacterium and then injects it every tick while the bacterium is actively infecting a host (see `DOCUMENTATION/caos_scripts/bacteria.md`). When the infection's toxin ID lands on 70 the bacterium becomes a Glycotoxin carrier, and a chronic infection will therefore drain Glycogen and injure a somatic organ until the immune system suppresses the bacterium (antibodies 102-109) or the player administers an Arnica-bearing remedy.

## Role in Game Mechanics

### The Glycogen-raid reaction: a sugar robber, not a sugar burner

Reaction 88 is the only hostile reaction in the standard genome that directly attacks Glycogen (see `DOCUMENTATION/chemicals/004 - Glycogen.md`). The formula `1× Glycotoxin + 1× Glycogen → 4× Glucose + 4× Coldness` is designed to *look* like normal glycogenolysis (reaction 9, which produces 6× Glucose per Glycogen) but with two deliberate penalties:

- **Reduced yield**: only 4× Glucose instead of 6×, a ~33 % efficiency loss. The creature's blood sugar rises, but less than it would from a legitimate metabolic demand — the toxin is *stealing* carbohydrates rather than *burning* them efficiently.
- **Coldness side-product**: 4× Coldness (chem 152) is a drive-class chemical that drives the creature to seek warmth. A Glycotoxin-poisoned creature therefore feels a chill on top of the sugar drain, which provides a diagnostic signal (the creature visibly shivers / seeks heaters) and an actionable drive that nudges the behaviour system to find a warm spot.

The Short half-life (24 ticks, decay 0.971) means the reaction is *fast*: once Glycotoxin and Glycogen are both present in the bloodstream, the reactants are consumed within seconds. In a chronic infection this pathway acts as a carbohydrate sink — the bacterium injects 0.005-0.050 Glycotoxin per tick, the raid reaction consumes it 1:1 with Glycogen, and the creature's Glycogen store bleeds away tick by tick. A Glycotoxin infection is essentially a slow, asymmetric starvation imposed on a well-fed creature: the more Glycogen it has stored, the more of it the toxin can destroy before decaying or being cleared.

### The injury receptor: slow organ damage via `RLOCUS_INJURY`

Receptor 30 (gene 146) writes into the somatic organ locus `RLOCUS_INJURY` — the "injury to apply" locus. Each time the organ processes its tick, the engine reads this locus and, if non-zero, calls `Injure(LOC_TO_LF(loc_InjuryToApply) / 10)`, reducing the organ's short-term life force by a fraction of the locus value. The comment in the engine source explicitly states: *"If full injury applied, organ can fail in a second (clock-rate depending). 10 = activations necessary to remove all life-force."*

With gene 146's profile (threshold 0, nominal 0, gain 29, flags 0), the arithmetic is:

```
injury_locus ≈ max(0, 0 + (Glycotoxin − 0) × 29 / 255)
             = Glycotoxin × 29 / 255
```

At Glycotoxin = 255 the locus sits at 29/255 (~11 % of maximum injury). Feeding that through the engine's injury formula means the organ loses roughly `29/255 / 10 ≈ 1.1 %` of its life force per activation cycle. Because `RLOCUS_INJURY` is itself rate-limited by the organ's clock rate, the actual damage per real-time second depends on the organ's `RLOCUS_CLOCKRATE` — a fast-clocked organ will take damage quickly, a slow-clocked one will be more resilient.

Gain 29 is deliberately **much lower** than the maximum of 255. By contrast, a hypothetical max-gain injury receptor would let a single saturating dose of toxin destroy an organ in ~10 organ activations (well under a second of real time at default clock rates). Glycotoxin's injury is therefore a *slow burn*, not a fast kill: at saturation it takes on the order of 90 activations (~3 seconds of organ time, possibly much more depending on clock rate) to remove the entire short-term life force, and a more realistic chronic-infection concentration of ~64/255 would take ~4× longer still. This gives the immune system room to catch up before the organ fails and gives the player time to administer Arnica.

### The Arnica antidote: why Glycotoxin is cureable

Reaction 89 (`1× Glycotoxin + 1× Arnica → nothing`) is the standard-genome clearance pathway and the reason Glycotoxin appears in the General Cure potion's documented toxin list. Arnica (chemical 97) is a plant-themed clearance chemical shipped by the game's herbal agents and is a recognisable lever in community pharma. The stoichiometry is clean 1:1 with no by-products, so a player who can get Arnica into a poisoned creature's bloodstream can neutralise Glycotoxin exactly as fast as they can deliver the Arnica.

The reaction's Short half-life (21 ticks, decay 0.968) matters because it makes the antidote *responsive*: an injected Arnica load burns down Glycotoxin within seconds rather than queuing up as a slow drip. This is the pharmacological counterpart to Cyanide's Sodium-thiosulphite reaction — both are fast, dedicated antidote reactions with no products, and both are the explanation for why the General Cure can claim to treat those specific toxins.

### Interaction with the bacterial infection system

Glycotoxin's primary stock-game vector is the bacterium agent (`bacteria.cos`), documented in detail at `DOCUMENTATION/caos_scripts/bacteria.md`. Each bacterium, when spawned, rolls a random toxin ID into OV16 from the range 70-81. When a bacterium lands on OV16 = 70 (or is explicitly configured that way) it becomes a Glycotoxin carrier and, while actively infecting a host, injects `ov17` (0.005–0.050) units of Glycotoxin per tick into the host's bloodstream. Because the raid reaction's half-life is fast and the injury receptor is slow, the clinical profile of a Glycotoxin infection evolves in two phases:

1. **Early phase** — the injected Glycotoxin immediately partners with the creature's Glycogen store via reaction 88. Glycogen drops rapidly, Glucose rises in short bursts, and Coldness accumulates as a drive chemical. The creature feels cold and may visibly seek warmth.
2. **Late phase** — once Glycogen is depleted the raid reaction stalls for lack of reactant, but the injury receptor continues to fire on whatever Glycotoxin remains (and whatever the bacterium keeps pumping in), progressively damaging a somatic organ. The passive half-life is 3,686 ticks so the chemical does not clear quickly on its own.

The immune system responds in parallel: each bacterium also injects a matching antigen (chemicals 82-89) which triggers antibody production (chemicals 102-109). Once antibody concentration exceeds the bacterium's dormancy threshold, the bacterium goes dormant and stops injecting both antigen and toxin. The creature's remaining Glycotoxin then decays passively (Long, ~2 min halving) or is actively neutralised if the player administers Arnica.

### Diagnostic visibility

The Medical Scanner agent (documented at `DOCUMENTATION/caos_scripts/medical scanner.md`) explicitly tests `chem 70 > 0.1` as one of its canonical toxin checks, so Glycotoxin is a *named, detectable* toxin in the stock diagnostics. The Medical Pod similarly surfaces Glycotoxin in the toxins panel when the player docks a creature for examination. This puts Glycotoxin in the same diagnostic tier as the classic 66-69 environmental toxins — named, scanner-visible, and with a clearly documented cure (General Cure / Arnica).

### Strategic / gameplay implications

- **Feast-and-famine vulnerability**: a well-fed creature with a full Glycogen store is *more* vulnerable to Glycotoxin's raid reaction than a hungry one. Starving a creature is a (very bad) defence against the carbohydrate-drain half of the toxin — but does nothing to block the organ-injury half.
- **Arnica supply is the key**: unlike Belladonna, Glycotoxin has a specific clearance reaction that consumes Arnica 1:1. Players maintaining Arnica stocks (via herbal agents or the General Cure potion) can neutralise Glycotoxin infections decisively.
- **Immune vs pharmacological cure**: a creature's immune system (antibody 102-109 for the corresponding antigen 82-89) can suppress the *bacterium* and stop new Glycotoxin injections, but the Glycotoxin already in the bloodstream must still be cleared by Arnica or passive decay before the organ-injury effect stops.
- **Chill as a behavioural flag**: the Coldness side-product of reaction 88 is a drive the creature can feel and the behaviour system responds to. A cold, recently-fed creature in the presence of the infection sprite is a strong diagnostic signal of a Glycotoxin infection specifically, and teaches attentive players to recognise the toxin from behaviour alone.

## Summary

Glycotoxin is the first and thematically central toxin in the bacterial-toxin block (70-81). It is a dual-mode poison: a fast, Glycogen-raiding reaction (gene 78) that halves the creature's carbohydrate efficiency and chills it, plus a slow, additive organ-injury receptor (gene 146) that damages a somatic organ for as long as the chemical is present. Unlike the environmental toxin block at chemicals 66-69, Glycotoxin is pharmacologically curable through a dedicated Arnica-consuming clearance reaction (gene 79), which is the biochemistry underneath the General Cure potion's claim to treat "glycotoxin". Its stock-game delivery vector is the bacterium agent family in `bacteria.cos`, which rolls it as one of twelve possible infection toxins and injects it tick by tick into the host while actively infecting — making Glycotoxin the in-chemistry signature of a specific kind of infectious disease that a prepared player can treat with herbal remedies and that an unprepared player's creature can survive if its immune system mounts a successful antibody response before the injury receptor destroys a critical organ.
