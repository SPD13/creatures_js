# 029 - Air

Air is the creature's **internal reserve of inhaled breathable gas** — the chemical proxy for "how much usable air the lungs are currently holding". It is the upstream raw material of the respiratory chain: reaction 27 (`1× Water [33] + 1× Air [29] → 3× Oxygen [30]`) combines it with Water to manufacture the Oxygen that powers the rest of metabolism (glucose combustion via reaction 19, `1× Pyruvate [2] + 1× Oxygen [30] → 1× CO₂ [24]`). Unlike most chemicals in the C3 biochemistry, Air is not synthesised from other chemicals — it is **emitted directly by the creature's environment** via the sensorimotor emitter on the `LOC_AIRQUALITY` locus, which reads whether the creature's head is in a breathable room or submerged in water. Air is therefore the chemical bridge between the world's room physics and the creature's oxygen-dependent metabolism.

Air has a **medium half-life** (~343 ticks, decay rate 0.998) — it does not linger indefinitely the way the "infinite half-life" metabolic pool chemicals do, but it does not evaporate so quickly that a brief dip underwater immediately starves the creature. A creature that stops breathing still has several hundred ticks of residual Air available to burn before the reserve runs out. The newborn creature starts with an initial concentration of 64/255 ≈ 25.1 %, roughly equivalent to "a single lungful already in reserve", enough to run respiration for the first few seconds of life without needing to consciously inhale.

The second, and deadlier, consumer of Air is **receptor 76** — a digital, inverted trigger on the sensorimotor `LOC_INVOLUNTARY7` locus, which is the creature's **Drowning** reflex. The receptor fires the Drowning involuntary action whenever Air falls below threshold 77 / 256 (~30 %). Air is therefore both the **fuel** for respiration and the **sensor signal** for asphyxiation: when the bloodstream's Air reserve is healthy, the creature breathes normally and generates Oxygen; when it drops below 30 %, the creature panics and triggers its drowning reflex script.

| 2 | Initial concentration | Gene 24, Baby onwards | Standard (genome-wide) | Bloodstream starts at 64/255 ≈ 25.1 % at birth | One-off at creature instantiation |

Air has **no chemical-synthesis route** — it is only ever deposited by the breathing emitter, loaded as an initial concentration at birth, or injected externally via CAOS (`CHEM 29 <amount>`) / food / drug agents. Because `LOC_AIRQUALITY` is driven entirely by the creature's spatial position (head in an air room vs. a water room — see "Environmental input" below), the Air supply is fundamentally a **locational** resource: the creature must keep its head out of water to stay topped up.

## Role in Game Mechanics

### Position in the respiratory pipeline

Air sits at the very top of the creature's oxygen-delivery chain:

```
   (world room type: air vs. water)
                │
                ▼
       LOC_AIRQUALITY locus          ← environmental input (1.0 = air, 0.0 = water)
                │
                ▼
        Emitter 6 (Gene 5)           ← "inhale"
                │
                ▼
          Air [29]                   ← short-lived inhaled reserve
                │
     ┌──────────┴──────────┐
     ▼                     ▼
 Reaction 27           Receptor 76
 (Gene 47)             (Gene 201)
 +Water [33]           threshold 77
     │                 REDUCE+DIGITAL
     ▼                     │
 Oxygen [30] ×3            ▼
     │              LOC_INVOLUNTARY7
     ▼               (Drowning reflex)
 Reaction 19
 +Pyruvate [2]
     │
     ▼
 CO₂ [24]
     │
     ▼
 (Glucose combustion completes; ATP/Energy generated)
```

Air is the **point of entry** for the entire oxidative-metabolism chain. Without it, reaction 27 has no substrate; without reaction 27, Oxygen levels drop and reaction 19 stops turning Pyruvate into CO₂; without reaction 19, Pyruvate accumulates and Glucose cannot be usefully burned; and without glucose combustion, ATP / Energy cannot be regenerated. A creature starved of Air suffocates from the top of the pipeline downward.

### Environmental input — the `LOC_AIRQUALITY` locus

The Air pipeline is the one place in C3 biochemistry where room physics reaches directly into creature chemistry. In the creature's per-tick update the engine sets each tick:

```text
myAirQualityLocus = 1.0                   # default: breathable
if GetRoomIDForPoint(head.CentrePoint()) gives roomId:
    if GetRoomType(roomId) gives roomType:
        if roomType is WATER_ROOM_TYPE_1 or WATER_ROOM_TYPE_2:
            myAirQualityLocus = 0.0       # head submerged → nothing to breathe
```

So the locus is a simple two-state sensor: 1.0 while the creature's head is in open air (or no room at all) and 0.0 while its head is inside a water-type room. Emitter 6 then reads this locus every 20 ticks with gain 56, depositing a proportional amount of Air into the bloodstream. Keep the head above water and the emitter steadily tops up the Air reserve; push it under and the emitter stops producing, while reaction 27 and the passive half-life both continue to drain the reserve.

Note that this is a **head-only** check — not a body-in-water check. A Norn can stand chest-deep in water and keep breathing as long as the head sprite's centre is still in an air room. This is deliberate: it makes swimming and wading behave sensibly without drowning a creature the moment a foot touches water.

### The breathing balance

Under normal conditions (head in air, respiration running, genome intact), the Air pool is in steady state:

- **Inflow**: emitter 6 fires every organ-clock, producing a proportional amount of Air based on `LOC_AIRQUALITY = 1.0` and gain 56.
- **Outflow**: reaction 27 consumes 1 Air per 1 Water to produce 3 Oxygen (medium half-life, ~105 ticks); passive decay removes ~0.2 % of the reserve per tick (~343 tick half-life).

The stoichiometry is generous — every unit of Air becomes three units of Oxygen — so even a modest steady inflow of Air keeps Oxygen stocked comfortably. Initial concentrations (Air 64, Oxygen 191, Water 255, Pyruvate 64) are balanced so that a newborn can immediately run both reaction 27 (Water is abundant) and reaction 19 (Pyruvate + Oxygen → CO₂) and start producing CO₂ (and hence ATP) from its first breath.

### The drowning reflex — receptor 76 in detail

What makes Air unique among respiratory chemicals is that it *is itself monitored* as an asphyxiation sensor. Receptor 76 is a **digital, inverted** receptor wired to the Drowning involuntary action locus (`LOC_INVOLUNTARY7`, defined as "Involuntary 7 / Drowning"). With flags `REDUCE | DIGITAL` the organ receptor kernel reduces to:

- **Excess signal** = `max(0, Air − 77)`. If Air ≥ 77, the signal is non-zero and — because DIGITAL — is clamped to `gain = 255`. If Air < 77, the signal is zero.
- Because REDUCE is set, that fixed gain is **subtracted** from `nominal = 255`.
- Net output = `255 − 255 = 0` when Air is *above* threshold; `255 − 0 = 255` when Air is *below* threshold.

In other words: **when the Air reserve drops below ~30 %, the Drowning involuntary-action locus is driven to maximum strength**. The MotorFaculty scanner then picks it up:

```text
for i in 0 .. NUMINVOL-1:
    a = myInvoluntaryActions[i]
    if a.latency == 0 and a.locus > RndFloat() and a.locus > strongestSoFar:
        strongestSoFar = myInvoluntaryActions[i].locus
        bestInvoluntaryActionId = i
if strongestSoFar > 0 and Life.GetWhetherAlert() and bestInvoluntaryActionId != myCurrentInvoluntaryAction:
    scriptClassifier.SetEvent(SCRIPTINVOLUNTARY0 + bestInvoluntaryActionId)
    # ... dispatch SCRIPTINVOLUNTARY7 (Drowning)
```

With `locus = 1.0` (full-strength output), the reflex wins the probability check almost every scan and the creature's brain fires the `SCRIPTINVOLUNTARY7` (Drowning) script. Exactly what that script does is genome-dependent — it typically plays a coughing animation, emits Pain, and in the canonical Norn genome drains the creature's life force over time — but the triggering mechanism is entirely chemical-driven.

The safety gate `c.Life()->GetWhetherAlert()` is important: **drowning does not fire while the creature is unconscious, asleep, dreaming, or dead**. An unconscious Norn submerged in water will continue to lose Air, but will not play the drowning reflex. Their metabolism will, however, be unable to produce Oxygen, Pyruvate will back up, CO₂ will fall, and ATP production will collapse. Unconscious drowning is therefore a silent, slower death through metabolic starvation rather than the noisy reflex trigger.

### Why Air decays even while breathing

A subtle design point: Air has a *medium* passive half-life (343 ticks), not the "effectively infinite" half-life of most metabolic pool chemicals. This deliberate decay creates a small but important property: **the Air reserve is always being refreshed**. Without the decay, a creature that swam briefly then returned to air could indefinitely preserve an old, stale Air reserve. With the decay, each lungful the creature holds is slowly replaced by freshly-emitted Air. Mechanically this also means that gene mutations which reduce the gain of emitter 6 cannot be compensated by simply "not using" the Air — old Air is constantly bleeding off, so the creature must keep its head in air to keep the reserve above the 30 % drowning threshold.

### The asymmetry with Oxygen

It is informative to compare Air and Oxygen as sibling respiratory chemicals:

| Property | Air [29] | Oxygen [30] |
|----------|----------|-------------|
| Role | Inhaled reserve | Blood-borne oxidant |
| Source | Emitter 6 (environmental) | Reaction 27 (metabolic, from Air + Water) |
| Consumer | Reaction 27 (converts to Oxygen); receptor 76 (drowning sensor) | Reaction 19 (glucose combustion); receptor 78 (low-oxygen compensation) |
| Passive half-life | Medium (~343 ticks, decay 0.998) | Effectively infinite (decay 1.0) |
| Initial concentration | 64 / 255 ≈ 25 % | 191 / 255 ≈ 75 % |
| Environmental coupling | **Yes** — directly tied to `LOC_AIRQUALITY` | No — purely internal chemistry |
| Reflex trigger | **Yes** — Drowning reflex (threshold 30 %) | No direct reflex; drives other compensation receptors |

Air is a **short-lived, environmentally-dependent gateway chemical**; Oxygen is a **long-lived, internally-regenerated working fluid**. The split is important because it separates the *availability of breath* (something that depends on where the creature is) from the *availability of oxidant* (something that depends on the internal metabolic machinery still working). A creature can run out of Air very quickly by submerging; once Air runs out, Oxygen stocks last much longer and continue to power metabolism for a while before the creature fully asphyxiates.

### Progression of asphyxiation

Because Air is a gateway chemical, asphyxiation (e.g. prolonged submersion) unfolds in distinct stages:

1. **Tick 0–300 (head enters water).** Emitter 6 stops producing. Air begins to drop from its steady-state value; half-life and reaction 27 together strip roughly 0.5 % per tick.
2. **Tick ~300–500 (Air crosses threshold 77).** Receptor 76 fires. `LOC_INVOLUNTARY7` spikes to 1.0. The MotorFaculty picks it up and triggers `SCRIPTINVOLUNTARY7` — the creature coughs, thrashes, plays its drowning animation. Air continues to fall.
3. **Tick ~500–1500 (Air approaches zero).** Reaction 27 starves. Oxygen production collapses. Existing Oxygen (initially 75 %) begins to be consumed by reaction 19 without being replenished. Pyruvate starts to accumulate; CO₂ and ATP production tail off.
4. **Tick ~1500+ (Oxygen depleted, ATP collapse).** Glucose combustion cannot complete. Energy reserves are no longer regenerated by the respiratory chain. Organ clock-rate receptors that depend on ATP begin to misbehave; organ damage accumulates. The creature dies of metabolic collapse, typically through life-force depletion driven by the drowning script itself or by cascade receptor failures (low ATP → low Energy → low life force).

Pulling the creature out of water at any point before stage 4 resets the process: `LOC_AIRQUALITY` jumps to 1.0, emitter 6 resumes, Air recovers in a few hundred ticks, reaction 27 resumes, Oxygen restocks, and the drowning reflex clears as soon as Air rises back above 77. In practice this makes "underwater for a few seconds" a survivable hazard and "trapped underwater for tens of seconds" a fatal one — precisely the gameplay feel the designers seem to have been aiming at.

### Mutations and scripter notes

- **Mutations on gene 5 (emitter 6).** Reducing the gain weakens the creature's ability to inhale: the steady-state Air level drops, and prolonged swimming or even brief dips underwater can push it below threshold 77. Removing the emitter entirely produces a creature that cannot breathe at all and will die shortly after its initial Air reserve (64) is consumed — typically within a couple of minutes of birth.
- **Mutations on gene 47 (reaction 27).** Disabling the reaction is a metabolic catastrophe: Air will accumulate freely (with no consumer except decay) but will never become Oxygen. Existing Oxygen stocks are consumed by reaction 19 and never replenished, and the creature slowly suffocates while *visibly still breathing*. This is an important distinction from drowning — receptor 76 never fires because Air is fine, so the creature does not trigger `SCRIPTINVOLUNTARY7`. It dies quietly of metabolic starvation.
- **Mutations on gene 201 (receptor 76).** If the receptor threshold is raised, the creature becomes more tolerant of low Air — it can hold its breath longer before panicking, but risks waiting too long before returning to air. If the threshold is lowered, it panics immediately on the smallest dip and effectively cannot swim. If the receptor is disabled entirely, the creature has no drowning reflex: it will wade serenely into water and die of asphyxiation without protest.
- **Mutations on gene 59 (Air half-life).** Shortening the half-life accelerates asphyxiation dramatically — even momentary submersion becomes dangerous. Lengthening it turns the Air reserve into a "breath-holding capacity" that persists for thousands of ticks; the creature can effectively swim unbothered. This is the closest C3 has to a "gill" mutation.
- **Water-room topology.** Because the Air sensor is strictly head-position-driven, designers can create "caves with air pockets" simply by arranging water rooms with air rooms in between — a creature that can navigate the geometry correctly will survive through long stretches of otherwise-fatal terrain. Conversely, a room whose type is accidentally set to water will silently suffocate any creature that enters it.
- **CAOS-injected Air (`CHEM 29 <amount>`)** is the usual way to manually "top up" a creature's breathing reserve; conversely `CHEM 29 -64` will immediately strip the reserve and fire the drowning reflex. This is useful for testing the reflex trigger or for scripting toxic-gas agents.

### Summary of the Air pathway

```
          ┌─────────────────────────────────────┐
          │        World: room.type             │
          │  (air / water → LOC_AIRQUALITY)     │
          └─────────────────┬───────────────────┘
                            │
                   (head position)
                            │
                            ▼
                 Emitter 6 (Gene 5)
                 "inhale from ambient air"
                            │
                            ▼
                       Air [29]    (half-life 343 ticks)
                            │
          ┌─────────────────┼───────────────────┐
          │                                     │
          ▼                                     ▼
   Reaction 27 (Gene 47)              Receptor 76 (Gene 201)
   +Water [33] → 3× Oxygen [30]       threshold 77, REDUCE+DIGITAL
          │                                     │
          ▼                                     ▼
   Metabolic oxidation chain           LOC_INVOLUNTARY7 (Drowning)
   (Pyruvate + Oxygen → CO₂ + ATP)            │
                                               ▼
                                        SCRIPTINVOLUNTARY7
                                        (drowning reflex script)
```

Air therefore plays the role of the creature's **atmospheric interface** — the one chemical whose supply is controlled by the world rather than by the genome, and whose depletion is the game's canonical mechanism for representing "can't breathe". Every other respiratory chemical (Oxygen, CO₂, Pyruvate, ATP) lives purely inside the creature's metabolic simulation; Air is the thin membrane through which that simulation is coupled to the map.
