# 102 - Antibody 0

Antibody 0 is chemical slot 102 in the Creatures 3 chemistry and the first entry in the canonical **antibody block** (chemicals 102-109, Antibody 0 through Antibody 7). Each antibody is paired one-to-one with an antigen in the 82-89 block — Antibody 0 is specifically the immune response to [Antigen 0 (082)](082%20-%20Antigen%200.md), the "sneeze antigen" carried by the most common class of bacterial invader. While antigens represent bacterial "fingerprints" and histamines the symptomatic by-products of immune reactions, antibodies are the **cumulative memory** of the creature's adaptive immune system: a rising Antibody 0 level records that the host has been fighting an Antigen-0 infection, and — crucially — is the chemical signal that the attacking bacterium itself reads to decide whether to keep injecting antigen or to go dormant.

Antibody 0 is produced endogenously by **reaction 92** (gene 85), the core Antigen-0 immune response:

```
2× Antigen 0 [82] → 12× Antibody 0 [102] + 1× Histamine B [74]
```

Each firing of this reaction amplifies two units of antigen into twelve units of antibody — a 6× amplification that lets the creature build up a decisive antibody concentration against a chronic source of Antigen 0. The reaction runs at a **Short half-life (52 ticks, decay rate 0.987)**, so antibody production kicks in within a second or two of a bacterium beginning to inject antigen into the host. Uniquely among the immune-system chemicals, every Creature 3 norn, grendel or ettin starts life with a **baseline initial concentration of 96 units** of Antibody 0 (gene 25, concentration 0.3765), so a newborn creature already has a "pre-seeded" antibody pool against the sneeze antigen. This starter dose is not present for the other seven antibodies — Antibody 0 is the only antibody in the block with a non-zero initial concentration — reflecting its role as the genome's default first-line defence against the most statistically likely bacterial encounter.

The chemical has **no receptors anywhere in the creature**: no organ locus reads chemical 102, no drive is modulated by its concentration, no metabolic process depends on it, no injury wire is triggered. This is the same "pure signalling" design used for the histamine antidote (Antihistamine, 100) — the antibody does nothing to the creature itself. Its entire game-mechanical footprint is **external**: the bacterium agent (`bacteria.cos`, family/genus/species `2 32 23`) reads the host's chemical slot 102 directly via the CAOS `chem` primitive, using its value to decide whether it is being successfully suppressed by the immune response. When the antibody level crosses the bacterium's randomly-rolled "sleep threshold" (`ov14`), the bacterium goes dormant and stops injecting Antigen 0 and its secondary toxin; when antibody later decays below the bacterium's "wake threshold" (`ov13`), the bacterium may resume active infection. Antibody 0 is therefore the **decisive lever** that determines whether a bacterial infection is winning or losing — from the *bacterium's* point of view, not the creature's — which in Creatures 3's economy of adaptive immunity is the same thing.

Antibody 0 clears from the bloodstream exclusively through passive decay, at a **Long half-life of 2,481 ticks** (decay rate 0.99972, ~1 minute 23 seconds of real play per halving at 30 tps). There is no reaction in the standard genome that consumes Antibody 0 and no antidote that cancels it — it can only fade over time. This Long-but-not-indefinite decay is a carefully tuned design choice: it keeps antibody levels elevated long enough to **suppress a bacterium for minutes after the antigen has been cleared**, providing durable immunity against a reinfection, but not so long that a creature remains permanently immune to every Antigen-0 exposure it has ever experienced. A creature that fought off an Antigen-0 bacterium five minutes ago will still be carrying measurable Antibody 0 and will therefore win any new exposure much faster than a creature fighting its first infection; a creature that fought one off an hour ago has decayed back to near-baseline and fights the next one from scratch (plus its genomic starter dose of 96 units). This is the biochemical engine behind the player-observable narrative that "my norns slowly build up immunity as they grow older".

The usage profile is therefore a study in **asymmetric design**: Antibody 0 has dramatic, decisive effects on bacteria, and no direct effects on the creature at all. Its entire game-mechanical value comes from being *readable* by the bacterium agent — an elegant example of Creatures 3's modular architecture, where chemistry is the shared communication layer between the biology genome and the external agent ecosystem.

## Role in Game Mechanics

### The antibody-bacterium dialog: external chemistry as game state

The most interesting design pattern in Antibody 0's mechanics is that its primary consumer is not a receptor on the creature itself but **the bacterium agent as an external reader**. The `bacteria.cos` script (documented in detail in `caos_scripts/bacteria.md`) polls the host's chemical 102 on every timer tick and uses the value to steer the bacterium's state machine:

| Antibody 0 level | Bacterium behaviour | Creature experience |
|------------------|---------------------|---------------------|
| Low (baseline 96 units, or rapidly decaying) | Active infection, injects Antigen 0 + toxin | Sneeze reflex firing, slow organ injury |
| Rising (reaction 92 fires repeatedly) | Still actively infecting, but taking lifespan drain from antibody | Sneeze continues; bacterium's life is shortening |
| Crosses sleep threshold (`ov14`) | Transition: bacterium goes dormant, stops injecting | Sneeze fades over ~seconds as Histamine B decays; antigen fades over ~minutes |
| High and sustained | Bacterium stays dormant; lifespan drain continues each tick | "Recovered": no symptoms, no organ damage. Bacterium may eventually die from lifespan drain |
| Falls below wake threshold (`ov13`) after decay | Bacterium re-activates, resumes injecting | Sneeze returns, cycle restarts |

This creates a dynamic feedback loop: the creature's immune response (reaction 92) produces antibody, which suppresses the bacterium, which stops producing antigen, which stops reaction 92, which allows antibody to passively decay, which eventually lets the bacterium wake up again. In practice, most bacterial infections either win decisively (the bacterium dies of lifespan drain before antibody decays) or lose decisively (the player kills the bacterium with anti-bacterial spray), so the oscillation is usually damped rather than periodic — but the underlying chemistry permits recurrent infections from a single persistent bacterium, which is the biochemistry behind a chronic, recurring "cold" in a creature with slow antibody production.

### Why Antibody 0 is the only antibody with a starter dose

Gene 25 of the standard genome sets the initial concentration of chemical 102 to 96 units (0.3765 of the 0-255 scale), making Antibody 0 the only antibody in the block 102-109 with a non-zero birth baseline. This asymmetry reflects three design intentions:

1. **Statistical priority**: Antigen 0 is one of eight possible antigens (each with equal 12.5% roll probability on bacterium spawn), so statistically every creature is likely to encounter an Antigen-0 infection at some point. Pre-seeding the response gives newborns at least a 25-40% head start on the sleep threshold of most bacteria, meaning a baby norn is not wholly defenceless against its first sneeze-antigen bacterium.
2. **Symptom visibility**: Antigen 0 is the "sneeze antigen" — it produces the most audible and recognisable infection symptom in the game. The designers wanted newborn creatures to not spend their first minutes of life in constant sneezing; giving them a starter antibody pool ensures that mild exposures are handled silently.
3. **Narrative framing**: the starter dose implements a "maternal immunity" or "placental antibody transfer" analogy, a real-world phenomenon where newborns inherit some maternal antibodies. Although Creatures 3 does not model literal maternal transfer (the initial concentration is hard-coded, not inherited from a parent genome), the *effect* is equivalent: creatures begin life with some pre-existing immunity against the most common threat.

Crucially, the 96-unit starter dose is not large enough to make newborns immune to a heavy infection — a bacterium with a high sleep threshold (`ov14` can roll up to 255) will still be able to infect aggressively. But it is enough to significantly raise the bar against mild bacteria and to give the immune-response loop a running start.

### The 6× amplification: decisive antibody build-up

Reaction 92's stoichiometry — two units of Antigen 0 in, twelve units of Antibody 0 out — embeds a **6× amplification factor** in the antibody-manufacturing pathway. Consider a bacterium injecting Antigen 0 at 0.02 units per tick: assuming a steady-state where reaction 92 consumes the antigen as fast as it is injected, the reaction fires once every 100 ticks (to accumulate the 2-unit reactant requirement), producing 12 units of antibody per firing, or 0.12 antibody units per tick. That sounds slow, but Antibody 0 has a Long passive decay (2,481 ticks, decay rate 0.99972), so the antibody accumulates rather than rapidly clearing. Over 10 minutes of sustained infection (~18,000 ticks), a creature can build hundreds of units of antibody above its baseline — more than enough to suppress even a high-sleep-threshold bacterium.

This amplification is why chronic infections eventually become self-limiting in Creatures 3: the bacterium continuously injects small doses of antigen that would, on a 1:1 conversion basis, be quickly cleared — but the 6× amplification accumulates a decisive antibody pool faster than the bacterium can escape it. The asymmetry (antigen decays Long at 1,670 ticks, antibody decays Long at 2,481 ticks) further favours the creature: antibody persists about 50% longer than antigen, so even when the bacterium has gone dormant and stopped producing antigen, the antibody pool it provoked lingers for a proportionally longer time, keeping the bacterium suppressed.

### Long decay and the memory of infection

The 2,481-tick passive half-life (~1 min 23 s at 30 tps) is carefully tuned to balance two competing design goals:

- **Memory of exposure**: antibody levels should persist long enough that a creature suppressing a bacterium actually "remembers" the exposure and can fight off a reinfection more easily. A half-life of 2,481 ticks means that one hour (108,000 ticks, ~43 half-lives) after the original exposure, antibody has decayed by a factor of ~2^43 ≈ 10¹³ — effectively zero. Five minutes (9,000 ticks, ~3.6 half-lives) after exposure, antibody has only decayed by a factor of ~12, so a creature with a peak of 500 units is still carrying ~40 units of residual antibody, meaningfully above baseline. This creates a "short-term acquired immunity" window of a few minutes to a quarter-hour after a successful infection suppression.
- **Long-term reinfection possibility**: on time scales of a full in-game day or longer, antibody must decay back to baseline so that a creature can realistically be reinfected. The Long half-life achieves this: even peak antibody levels fade completely within an hour or two, so a creature's long-term immunity is not permanent.

The Long-but-finite decay therefore implements a realistic adaptive-immunity model: exposure confers meaningful short-term protection that slowly fades, allowing reinfection over long spans of creature life. This is a more interesting gameplay pattern than either permanent immunity (which would trivialise late-game infections) or instant forgetting (which would make the infection loop feel stateless and unrewarding).

### No creature-side receptors: why the creature doesn't "feel" its own antibodies

Antibody 0 has zero receptors on any creature locus — the creature cannot sense its own antibody level, and no drive, reflex or metabolic process depends on it. This may seem surprising (real-world antibodies trigger measurable physiological responses) but it is intentional for two reasons:

1. **Separation of concerns**: the chemistry handles the biochemical bookkeeping (antibody accumulation and decay), and the bacterium agent handles the game-state effect (dormancy and lifespan drain). Adding a creature-side receptor would duplicate functionality and risk interference between the two systems.
2. **Prevents overdose side effects**: because there is no receptor, a player can safely dose a creature with arbitrary amounts of Antibody 0 (via a community "immune booster" agent or via CAOS injection) without side effects. The excess simply suppresses whatever bacteria are present more decisively and then decays passively. This makes community pharma content safe to compose.

The contrast with histamines (which have LOC_INVOLUNTARY reflex receptors) is instructive: histamines are *symptomatic* by-products that need to drive a visible reflex, so they have receptors; antibodies are *effective* chemicals that need to drive the bacterium's state machine, so they bypass the receptor system entirely and talk directly to the external agent.

### The antibody block as a whole

Antibody 0 is the template for the full antibody block (102-109), all of which share the same passive decay (2,481 ticks, Long) and all of which have no receptors. The block's structure mirrors the antigen block 82-89 one-to-one:

| Antigen | Paired Antibody | Production Reaction | Amplification | Symptom By-product |
|---------|------------------|---------------------|---------------|---------------------|
| Antigen 0 (82) | **Antibody 0 (102)** | Reaction 92 (Short, 52-tick half-life) | 2:12 (6×) | Histamine B (74) |
| Antigen 1 (83) | Antibody 1 (103) | Reaction 93 (Short) | 2:12 (6×) | Histamine A (73) |
| Antigen 2 (84) | Antibody 2 (104) | Reaction 94 (Short) | 16:12 (0.75×) | Coldness (152, ×2) |
| Antigen 3 (85) | Antibody 3 (105) | Reaction 96 | 1:1 | Coldness (152, ×2) |
| Antigen 4 (86) | Antibody 4 (106) | Reaction 95 | 2:3 | Hotness (153) |
| Antigen 5 (87) | Antibody 5 (107) | Reaction 97 | 1:3 | Chemical 90 |
| Antigen 6 (88) | Antibody 6 (108) | Reaction 98 | 1:3 | Hotness (153) |
| Antigen 7 (89) | Antibody 7 (109) | Reaction 99 | 1:3 | Pain (148) |

Antibody 0 shares its 6× amplification with Antibody 1 (the cough antibody), which is not coincidence: antigens 0 and 1 produce the two most visible symptoms (sneeze and cough), so the genome favours a decisive, fast-ramping immune response to bring these noisy infections under control quickly. The other antibody reactions have lower amplification (or in the case of reaction 94, sub-unity) because their associated symptoms are less player-noticeable and the design does not need to rush them into suppression. Antibody 0 is therefore the single most aggressively-amplified antibody reaction in the genome — reflecting Antigen 0's status as the "common cold" of Creatures 3.

### Strategic / gameplay implications

- **Starter dose matters for newborn survival**: the 96-unit baseline Antibody 0 means newborn creatures have meaningful first-bacterium resistance against sneeze infections. Players who notice that their baby norns handle their first cold without much drama are experiencing gene-25's starter dose in action.
- **Antibody levels are the real "health indicator" for bacterial infections**: while players typically watch Histamine A/B (sneeze/cough) or the toxin panel, the underlying state that matters is the antibody level — it is what the bacterium reads. A creature with rising Antibody 0 is winning; a creature with falling Antibody 0 and persistent antigen is losing. The chemistry panel's slot 102 is the truest diagnostic.
- **"Immune memory" is real**: a creature that fought an Antigen-0 bacterium five minutes ago carries residual antibody that will immediately suppress any new Antigen-0 bacterium it encounters. Player folklore around "my old norn doesn't get sick as often" is genuinely grounded in the Long passive decay of Antibody 0 preserving post-exposure immunity across the short term.
- **Anti-bacterial spray kills the bacterium, but antibodies linger**: after a player uses the anti-bacterial spray to kill an Antigen-0 bacterium, Antigen 0 levels drop and reaction 92 stops firing. But the accumulated Antibody 0 persists for ~1.5 minutes per half-life, providing durable post-infection coverage that makes subsequent exposures much easier to fight.
- **Community vaccine agents are viable**: because Antibody 0 has no receptors and no side effects, modders can ship "vaccine" agents that dose a target creature with chemical 102 directly. This confers immediate immunity against Antigen-0 bacteria without triggering the infection loop at all. The community "maternal care" and "baby immune-booster" agents typically rely on this pattern.

### Diagnostic visibility

Antibody 0 is not named in the Medical Pod's toxin panel (it is not a toxin), but it is visible via:

- **Chemistry panel (slot 102)**: direct numeric readout, showing the current antibody level.
- **Debug console**: `CHEM TARG 102` returns the current concentration on demand.
- **Bacterium behaviour inference**: if a bacterium on a creature suddenly stops producing symptoms (sneezing stops, antigen fades), the player can infer that Antibody 0 has crossed the bacterium's sleep threshold, even without looking at the raw chemistry.

The clinical signature of a creature mid-infection is: rising Antibody 0, elevated Antigen 0, elevated Histamine B, sneeze reflex firing. The signature of a creature post-infection is: high Antibody 0, near-baseline Antigen 0, near-baseline Histamine B, no sneeze — and the residual high Antibody 0 is what gives the creature its short-term acquired immunity.

## Summary

Antibody 0 is chemical 102 and the dedicated immune response product for [Antigen 0 (082)](082%20-%20Antigen%200.md), the "sneeze antigen" carried by the most common class of bacterial invader in Creatures 3. It is produced by **reaction 92** (gene 85, `2× Antigen 0 → 12× Antibody 0 + 1× Histamine B`, Short half-life of 52 ticks), uniquely among antibodies starts every creature's life at a **baseline 96-unit concentration** set by gene 25, and clears only via a **Long passive decay** of 2,481 ticks (~1 min 23 s per halving at 30 tps). It has **no receptors** on the creature — no locus reads its concentration, it affects no drive or reflex or metabolic process. Its entire game-mechanical effect is to be read by the external `bacteria.cos` agent: when the host's Antibody 0 rises above the bacterium's randomly-rolled sleep threshold (`ov14`), the bacterium goes dormant and stops injecting antigen and toxin; and each tick of elevated antibody additionally drains the bacterium's remaining lifespan by `antibody × 10`, so sustained high antibody eventually kills the bacterium outright. The **6× amplification** of reaction 92 (shared only with Antibody 1) makes this the single most aggressively-ramping antibody response in the stock genome, reflecting Antigen 0's status as the "common cold" of Creatures 3 — the most commonly encountered and the most visibly symptomatic bacterial fingerprint. The Long-but-finite passive decay implements a realistic adaptive-immunity model: creatures retain meaningful post-exposure immunity for minutes-to-quarter-hours and fade to baseline over an hour or two, so short-term reinfection is hard and long-term reinfection is possible. With no creature-side receptors, no antidote reactions and no direct physiological effect, Antibody 0 is a textbook example of Creatures 3's modular chemistry design: a pure "signalling" chemical whose meaning lives entirely in the communication between the biology genome (reaction 92, gene 25) and the external agent ecosystem (`bacteria.cos` reading `chem 102`). Player-side, it is the truest indicator of whether an Antigen-0 infection is being won or lost — the numeric heartbeat of the creature's adaptive immune system.
