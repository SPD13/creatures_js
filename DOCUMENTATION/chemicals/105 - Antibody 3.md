# 105 - Antibody 3

Antibody 3 is chemical slot 105 in the Creatures 3 chemistry and the fourth entry in the canonical **antibody block** (chemicals 102-109, Antibody 0 through Antibody 7). Each antibody is paired one-to-one with an antigen in the 82-89 block — Antibody 3 is specifically the immune response to [Antigen 3 (085)](085%20-%20Antigen%203.md), the second of the two **Coldness-producing** antigens whose infections manifest as elevated Coldness drive and, at high concentration, involuntary sleep. Like all antibodies, Antibody 3 is **cumulative immune memory**: a rising chemical-105 level records that the host has been fighting an Antigen-3 infection, and it is the chemical signal that the attacking bacterium agent reads to decide whether to keep injecting antigen and toxin or to go dormant.

Antibody 3 is produced endogenously by **reaction 96** (gene 88), the Antigen-3 immune response — a **perfectly symmetric, non-amplifying** reaction:

```
1× Antigen 3 [85] → 1× Antibody 3 [105] + 2× Coldness [152]
```

Of the eight antibody reactions in the stock genome, reaction 96 is the only one that is **exactly 1:1** — it consumes one unit of Antigen 3 to manufacture one unit of Antibody 3. It is the mid-point of the antibody-block amplification spectrum: below the 6× amplification of Antibody 0/1 (reactions 92/93), above the 0.75× de-amplification of Antibody 2 (reaction 94), and distinct from the 1:3 expansions of Antibodies 5/6/7 (reactions 97/98/99). The reaction runs at a **Short half-life (64 ticks, decay rate 0.989, rate genome value 42)** — identical to reaction 94's speed, so once Antigen 3 is present the antibody pool is drained into production rapidly. Crucially, reaction 96 has a **1-unit reactant threshold** — any trace of Antigen 3 will cause it to start firing immediately, unlike reaction 94's 16-unit gate. Every firing also **dumps two units of Coldness (152)** as a by-product, matching the 2× Coldness signature of reaction 94 and tying Antibody 3 production directly to the chills/involuntary-sleep symptom pattern. Every Creatures 3 norn, grendel or ettin starts life with a **baseline initial concentration of 50 units** of Antibody 3 (gene 19, concentration 0.1961), just above Antibody 2's 46-unit starter dose and in line with the modest-to-moderate starter doses assigned to the middle of the antibody block.

The chemical has **no receptors anywhere on the creature** for its own effect: no organ locus reads chemical 105, no drive is modulated by its concentration, no metabolic reaction consumes it, no injury wire is triggered. (Receptor 104 in the receptor table uses `"chemical": 125` (Life) and only happens to have the id-field 105 — that is the LOC_AGE4 Adult→Old life-stage receptor, completely unrelated to Antibody 3.) This is the standard "pure signalling" design for the entire antibody block — the antibody does nothing to the creature itself. Its entire game-mechanical footprint is **external**: the bacterium agent (`bacteria.cos`, family/genus/species `2 32 23`) reads the host's chemical slot 105 directly via the CAOS `chem` primitive whenever its rolled `ov15` is 85, using the value to decide whether it is being successfully suppressed by the immune response. When antibody rises above the bacterium's dormancy threshold the bacterium goes dormant and stops injecting Antigen 3 and its paired toxin; when antibody later decays below its wake threshold, the bacterium may resume active infection. Antibody 3 is therefore the **decisive lever** that determines whether an Antigen-3 bacterial infection is winning or losing — from the bacterium's point of view, which in Creatures 3's economy of adaptive immunity is the same thing.

Antibody 3 clears from the bloodstream exclusively through passive decay, at a **Long half-life of 2,481 ticks** (decay rate 0.99972, ~1 minute 23 seconds of real play per halving at 30 tps) — the decay profile shared by every antibody in the block. There is no reaction in the standard genome that consumes Antibody 3 and no antidote that cancels it. Together with reaction 96's immediate, trace-triggered firing, this produces the characteristic Antigen-3 infection arc: even a brief, mild infection immediately starts building antibody unit-for-unit as the antigen arrives; heavier infections pile antibody up continuously (rather than in threshold-gated bursts as for Antibody 2), with each antibody unit produced paired with 2 units of Coldness bleeding into the bloodstream. Once Antibody 3 rises far enough to exceed the bacterium's dormancy threshold, the bacterium goes dormant and the elevated antibody decays slowly over ~minutes, conferring durable short-term immunity against reinfection.

## Role in Game Mechanics

### The 1:1 symmetric reaction: why Antibody 3 tracks exposure linearly

Reaction 96 is the **amplification mid-point** of the antibody block. Every other antibody reaction in the stock genome either amplifies (2→12 for reactions 92/93, 1→3 for reactions 97/98/99) or de-amplifies (16→12 for reaction 94). Reaction 96 is the single perfectly symmetric exchange: one antigen molecule becomes one antibody molecule, no more, no less. This balanced stoichiometry shapes how Antigen-3 infections play out:

| Property | Reaction 92 (Antibody 0) | Reaction 93 (Antibody 1) | Reaction 94 (Antibody 2) | **Reaction 96 (Antibody 3)** | Reactions 95/97-99 |
|----------|--------------------------|--------------------------|--------------------------|------------------------------|--------------------|
| Reactant threshold | 2× antigen | 2× antigen | 16× antigen | **1× antigen** | 1-2× antigen |
| Antibody output | 12 | 12 | 12 | **1** | 3 |
| Amplification ratio | 6× | 6× | 0.75× | **1×** (symmetric) | 1.5× to 3× |
| Symptom by-product | 1× Histamine B | 1× Histamine A | 2× Coldness | **2× Coldness** | Hotness / Pain / Chem 90 |
| Half-life | Short (52) | Short (52) | Short (64) | **Short (64)** | Varies |

The practical consequence for Antigen-3 infections is that the immune response is **continuous and proportional**. A bacterium injects Antigen 3 at 0.02 units/tick, so antibody accumulates at essentially the same rate minus whatever is lost to reaction 96 producing it and to passive antigen decay (Long, 1,370 ticks, ~0.051%/tick — slightly faster than Antigen 2's decay). There is no accumulation threshold: any trace of antigen immediately starts producing antibody, so there is no "free ride" stage where a mild infection can damage the creature without engaging the immune system. This makes Antigen 3 the **most engaging** of the two Coldness antigens from an immune-response standpoint — even the briefest contact begins the antibody build-up process.

The linearity of the 1:1 ratio also means that, in aggregate over a long infection, the cumulative Antibody 3 produced is bounded by (roughly) the cumulative Antigen 3 that ever entered the bloodstream — it cannot ramp up disproportionately the way Antibody 0/1 can (where 2 antigen yields 12 antibody, a 6× bonus). In design terms, Antibody 3 represents "honest" immunity: what you produce is exactly what you were exposed to, with no amplification bonus. The system trades the fast-ramp advantage of amplifying reactions for a smoother, more faithful exposure record.

### The Coldness by-product: twice the symptom load, continuous delivery

Every time reaction 96 fires, it dumps **2 units of Coldness (chemical 152)** into the bloodstream — twice the per-firing symptom load of the histamine-producing antibodies 0 and 1 (which each emit 1 unit of their paired histamine). Coldness has three downstream chemistry effects:

1. **Coldness drive**: receptor on chemical 152 with gain 204 feeds the creature's Coldness drive. Elevated Coldness makes the creature feel cold and motivates it to seek heat sources.
2. **Involuntary Sleep reflex**: at Coldness ≥128, receptor 72 (LOC_INVOLUNTARY4, DIGITAL flag, gain 255) fires the involuntary Sleep reflex — the creature falls asleep regardless of what they were doing.
3. **Thermoregulatory counterpart to Hotness**: Coldness and Hotness (153) participate in the creature's ambient temperature regulation.

The critical difference from Antigen 2 is the **delivery profile** of Coldness. Reaction 94 (Antibody 2) burns its 16-unit reservoir in bursts, producing a spiky "suddenly cold" symptom pattern separated by long quiet intervals. Reaction 96 (Antibody 3) fires continuously on trace Antigen 3, so Coldness is delivered as a **smooth, steady trickle** — 2 units per firing, but firings happen as fast as antigen arrives. The subjective player experience of an Antigen-3 infection is therefore **persistent mild cold** rather than the episodic chills of Antigen 2: the creature's Coldness drive ramps gradually upward through a sustained infection, and the involuntary Sleep threshold (Coldness ≥128) takes longer to cross but, once crossed, is crossed more durably. The two Coldness antigens consequently feel quite different in play even though they share the same symptom chemicals.

### The 50-unit starter dose: slightly-above-average pre-immunity

Gene 19 sets the initial concentration of chemical 105 to 50 units (0.1961 of the 0-255 scale). This is a modest-to-moderate starter dose — about half of Antibody 0's 96 units, just above Antibody 2's 46 units, and close to the median of the antibody-block range (23 to 96). The design logic is:

1. **Every antibody block chemical has a starter dose.** The antibody block is unique in the stock genome for having genomic initial concentrations on every slot: Antibody 0 = 96, Antibody 1 = 52, Antibody 2 = 46, Antibody 3 = 50, Antibody 4 = 48, Antibody 5 = 56, Antibody 6 = 23, Antibody 7 = 46. The stock creature is *pre-armed* against every antigen from birth.
2. **The starter dose raises the bacterium's dormancy bar on day one.** The bacterium compares the host's antibody level against its own randomly-rolled dormancy threshold. A 50-unit starter dose is enough to trigger dormancy in any bacterium whose roll landed on the low end of the threshold distribution, meaning newborn creatures have non-trivial odds of silencing a mild Antigen-3 bacterium immediately on contact — without ever firing reaction 96 and therefore without ever experiencing the Coldness symptom.
3. **Antibody 3's starter dose is proportionate to Antigen 3's threat profile.** Slightly higher than Antibody 2 (46) because reaction 96's 1:1 ratio means the creature builds organic Antibody 3 more *reliably* but not more *quickly* per antigen unit delivered than reaction 94 does in its bursts — the extra starter units compensate by front-loading a bit more protection at birth.

Because passive decay runs constantly from birth (Long 2,481 ticks), the starter 50 units halves roughly every 82 seconds of play. After about 5 minutes of newborn life (no Antigen 3 exposure), Antibody 3 has decayed to ~10 units — no longer meaningfully above zero. This is why the starter dose matters specifically for the **first minutes** of a creature's life: it protects against early bacterial exposures that a newborn would otherwise fight from a cold start, but fades away by adolescence unless reinforced by actual exposure events.

### Long decay and the memory of infection

The 2,481-tick passive half-life (~1 min 23 s at 30 tps) is the standard antibody decay profile and balances two competing design goals:

- **Memory of exposure**: antibody levels should persist long enough that a creature suppressing a bacterium actually "remembers" the exposure and can fight off a reinfection more easily. A creature that fought off an Antigen-3 bacterium five minutes ago still carries a meaningful fraction of its peak antibody (~12%) — enough to halt most fresh bacterium dormancy rolls.
- **Long-term reinfection possibility**: on time scales of a full in-game day, antibody must decay back toward baseline so that a creature can realistically be reinfected. The Long half-life achieves this: peak antibody from a heavy infection fades to the starter baseline within an hour or so, so long-term immunity is not permanent.

For Antigen 3 specifically, the interplay between reaction 96's threshold-free firing and the Long passive decay produces a **smooth rise-and-fall curve** in Antibody 3 during and after an infection: antibody climbs continuously while the bacterium is active, plateaus when the bacterium goes dormant, and then decays exponentially once antigen input ceases. Plotting Antibody 3 over time during a chronic infection shows an integral-like shape — a clean ramp upward followed by a clean exponential tail — in contrast to the staircase pattern of Antibody 2.

### No creature-side receptors: why the creature doesn't "feel" its own antibodies

Antibody 3 has zero receptors on any creature locus — the creature cannot sense its own antibody level, and no drive, reflex or metabolic process depends on it. This is the standard antibody-block design and is intentional for two reasons:

1. **Separation of concerns**: the chemistry handles the biochemical bookkeeping (antibody accumulation and decay), and the bacterium agent handles the game-state effect (dormancy and lifespan drain). Adding a creature-side receptor would duplicate functionality and risk interference between the two systems.
2. **Prevents overdose side effects**: because there is no receptor, a player can safely dose a creature with arbitrary amounts of Antibody 3 (via a community "immune booster" agent or via CAOS injection) without side effects. The excess simply suppresses Antigen-3 bacteria more decisively and then decays passively. This makes community pharma content safe to compose.

The contrast with Coldness (which has LOC_INVOLUNTARY4 Sleep reflex receptor and Coldness drive receptor) is instructive: Coldness is a *symptomatic* by-product that needs to drive visible creature behaviours, so it has receptors; Antibody 3 is an *effective* chemical that needs to drive the bacterium's state machine, so it bypasses the creature-side receptor system entirely and talks directly to the external agent.

### Strategic / gameplay implications

- **Reaction 96 has no "free ride" regime**: unlike Antigen 2, whose 16-unit reactant threshold means short infections never produce antibody and never trigger the Coldness by-product, any Antigen 3 exposure — however brief — immediately starts producing Antibody 3 and dumping Coldness into the bloodstream. Even trace Antigen-3 exposures cost the creature some Coldness.
- **Continuous Coldness delivery produces a smoother symptom profile**: rather than the bursty "suddenly cold / suddenly sleep" pattern of Antigen 2, Antigen 3 produces a rising continuous Coldness trickle. The creature's Coldness drive ramps steadily and, at sustained high infection rates, eventually crosses the Sleep threshold more durably than Antigen 2's brief spikes ever do.
- **Antibody 3 is a proportional infection severity indicator**: because reaction 96 is 1:1 and trace-triggered, any Antibody 3 level significantly above the 50-unit baseline is proportional evidence of Antigen-3 exposure magnitude. Doubling the antigen roughly doubles the antibody pool (modulo decay timing). This gives the chemistry panel a cleanly-readable exposure gauge — more directly interpretable than Antibody 2's threshold-gated binary indicator.
- **Immune memory against Antigen 3 builds predictably**: because there is no threshold gate, antibody accumulation is linear in exposure, so any Antigen-3 infection contributes to long-term immunity. A creature that has weathered several mild Antigen-3 exposures over its lifetime accumulates durable protection even if no single exposure was severe — something Antigen 2 cannot do because reaction 94's 16-unit gate means sub-threshold exposures contribute nothing to immunity.
- **Anti-bacterial spray kills the bacterium, but antibodies linger**: after a player uses the anti-bacterial spray to kill an Antigen-3 bacterium, Antigen 3 levels drop, reaction 96 stops firing, Coldness production stops and Coldness fades over ~minutes. But the accumulated Antibody 3 persists for ~1.5 minutes per half-life, providing durable post-infection coverage against immediate reinfection.
- **Community vaccine agents avoid the Coldness collateral**: because every unit of organic Antibody 3 produced comes with 2 units of Coldness, pre-immunising a creature by injecting Antibody 3 directly is dramatically gentler on the creature's comfort than letting a real infection run and build antibody naturally. Injecting 100 units of Antibody 3 saves the creature from producing 200 units of Coldness it would otherwise endure through a comparable organic infection.

### Diagnostic visibility

Antibody 3 is not named in the Medical Pod's toxin panel (it is not a toxin), but it is visible via:

- **Chemistry panel (slot 105)**: direct numeric readout, showing the current antibody level. Values significantly above the 50-unit baseline indicate an active or recent Antigen-3 infection, with the magnitude roughly proportional to cumulative exposure.
- **Debug console**: `CHEM TARG 105` returns the current concentration on demand.
- **Bacterium behaviour inference**: if an Antigen-3 bacterium on a creature suddenly stops producing Coldness and the creature stops feeling cold, the player can infer that Antibody 3 has crossed the bacterium's dormancy threshold.

The clinical signature of a creature mid-infection is: smoothly-rising Antibody 3, slowly-climbing Antigen 3, steadily-elevated Coldness drive, eventual involuntary sleep. The signature of a creature post-infection is: high Antibody 3 (decaying slowly), near-baseline Antigen 3, fading Coldness, no cold-seeking behaviour — and the residual high Antibody 3 is what gives the creature its short-term acquired immunity against re-infection.

### The antibody block as a whole

Antibody 3 sits at the structural mid-point of the antibody block (102-109), which shares the same passive decay (2,481 ticks, Long) and the same "no receptors" design across all eight slots. The block's structure mirrors the antigen block 82-89 one-to-one:

| Antigen 1 (83) | Antibody 1 (103) | Reaction 93 (Short, 52-tick) | 2:12 (6×) | Histamine A (73) |

Antibody 3 is the **only exactly 1:1 antibody response** in the stock genome — every other reaction either amplifies or de-amplifies. Along with its trace-triggered firing (no reactant threshold) and shared Coldness by-product with Antibody 2, this makes Antibody 3 the **continuous-delivery counterpart** to Antigen 2's burst-delivery chills — two thematically similar antigens produced by two mechanically distinct immune responses, giving players subtly different infection experiences across the two Coldness-pairing antigen slots.

## Summary

Antibody 3 is chemical 105 and the dedicated immune response product for [Antigen 3 (085)](085%20-%20Antigen%203.md), the second of two Coldness-producing antigens whose infections elevate the Coldness drive and, at sufficient load, fire the involuntary Sleep reflex. It is produced by **reaction 96** (gene 88, `1× Antigen 3 → 1× Antibody 3 + 2× Coldness`, Short half-life of 64 ticks) — the *only perfectly 1:1 symmetric* antibody reaction in the stock genome, with no reactant threshold, so any trace of Antigen 3 immediately begins building antibody unit-for-unit and emitting Coldness 2-for-1. Every creature starts life with a **50-unit baseline concentration** set by gene 19 (slightly above the antibody-block median), and the chemical clears only via a **Long passive decay** of 2,481 ticks (~1 min 23 s per halving at 30 tps). It has **no receptors** on the creature — no locus reads its concentration, it affects no drive or reflex or metabolic process. Its entire game-mechanical effect is to be read by the external `bacteria.cos` agent: when the host's Antibody 3 rises above an Antigen-3-carrying bacterium's randomly-rolled dormancy threshold, the bacterium goes dormant and stops injecting antigen and toxin; and each tick of elevated antibody drains the bacterium's remaining lifespan by `antibody × 10`, so sustained high antibody eventually kills the bacterium outright. Because reaction 96 is threshold-free and symmetric, Antigen-3 infections produce a **smooth, continuous build-up pattern** in Antibody 3 (in contrast to Antibody 2's staircase of threshold-gated bursts), with Coldness delivered as a steady trickle rather than in spikes — producing the subjectively "persistent chills" symptom profile that distinguishes Antigen 3 from Antigen 2's episodic chills. The Long passive decay preserves accumulated immunity for minutes after an infection clears, giving meaningful short-term protection against reinfection while fading to baseline over ~hours. With no creature-side receptors, no antidote reactions and no direct physiological effect, Antibody 3 exemplifies Creatures 3's modular chemistry design: a pure "signalling" chemical whose meaning lives entirely in the communication between the biology genome (reaction 96, gene 19) and the external agent ecosystem (`bacteria.cos` reading `chem 105`). Player-side, it is the cleanest proportional indicator of Antigen-3 exposure magnitude in the chemistry panel — a continuous biochemical dial rather than the binary "heavy infection experienced" flag of its sibling Antibody 2.
