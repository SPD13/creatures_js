# 104 - Antibody 2

Antibody 2 is chemical slot 104 in the Creatures 3 chemistry and the third entry in the canonical **antibody block** (chemicals 102-109, Antibody 0 through Antibody 7). Each antibody is paired one-to-one with an antigen in the 82-89 block — Antibody 2 is specifically the immune response to [Antigen 2 (084)](084%20-%20Antigen%202.md), the "chills / sleepy-cold" antigen whose infections manifest as elevated Coldness drive and, at high concentration, involuntary sleep. Like all antibodies, Antibody 2 is **cumulative immune memory**: a rising chemical-104 level records that the host has been fighting an Antigen-2 infection, and it is the chemical signal that the attacking bacterium itself reads to decide whether to keep injecting antigen and toxin or to go dormant.

Antibody 2 is produced endogenously by **reaction 94** (gene 87), the Antigen-2 immune response — and this reaction is the **structural outlier** of the antibody block:

```
16× Antigen 2 [84] → 12× Antibody 2 [104] + 2× Coldness [152]
```

Unlike the 2→12 amplifying reactions that produce Antibody 0 and Antibody 1 (6× molecular amplification), reaction 94 is **sub-unity**: it consumes *sixteen* units of Antigen 2 per firing and produces only *twelve* units of Antibody 2, a ratio of 0.75. It is the only *de-amplifying* immune reaction in the stock genome. The reaction runs at a **Short half-life (64 ticks, decay rate 0.989, rate genome value 42)** — fast once it fires — but its 16-unit reactant threshold means it only fires at all when Antigen 2 has accumulated to a substantial reservoir, and when it does fire it **dumps two units of Coldness (152)** as a by-product, double the symptom load of a single Histamine-producing firing for Antibodies 0 or 1. Every Creatures 3 norn, grendel or ettin starts life with a **baseline initial concentration of 46 units** of Antibody 2 (gene 18, concentration 0.1804), about half of Antibody 0's starter dose and in line with the modest starter doses assigned to Antibodies 1, 3-5 and 7. All antibodies in the block receive a genomic starter dose, but Antibody 0 is pre-seeded most aggressively (96 units) because Antigen 0's sneeze symptom is the most player-visible; Antibody 2's starter dose at 46 units gives newborns a modest but non-trivial head start against the chills antigen.

The chemical has **no receptors anywhere in the creature**: no organ locus reads chemical 104, no drive is modulated by its concentration, no metabolic reaction consumes it, no injury wire is triggered. This is the standard "pure signalling" design for the entire antibody block — the antibody does nothing to the creature itself. Its entire game-mechanical footprint is **external**: the bacterium agent (`bacteria.cos`, family/genus/species `2 32 23`) reads the host's chemical slot 104 directly via the CAOS `chem` primitive whenever its rolled `ov15` is 84, using the value to decide whether it is being successfully suppressed by the immune response. When antibody rises above the bacterium's dormancy threshold the bacterium goes dormant and stops injecting Antigen 2 and its paired toxin; when antibody later decays below its wake threshold, the bacterium may resume active infection. Antibody 2 is therefore the **decisive lever** that determines whether an Antigen-2 bacterial infection is winning or losing — from the bacterium's point of view, which in Creatures 3's economy of adaptive immunity is the same thing.

Antibody 2 clears from the bloodstream exclusively through passive decay, at a **Long half-life of 2,481 ticks** (decay rate 0.99972, ~1 minute 23 seconds of real play per halving at 30 tps) — the decay profile shared by every antibody in the block. There is no reaction in the standard genome that consumes Antibody 2 and no antidote that cancels it. Together with reaction 94's reservoir-burn structure, this produces the characteristic Antigen-2 infection arc: short infections never fire reaction 94 at all and therefore never raise Antibody 2 above baseline — the creature retains its starter 46 units and relies on passive antigen decay and mild tissue damage to fade the episode. Sustained infections eventually cross the 16-unit Antigen-2 threshold, triggering bursts of reaction 94 that accumulate Antibody 2 at 12 units per firing and dump Coldness into the bloodstream each time. Once Antibody 2 rises far enough to exceed the bacterium's dormancy threshold, the bacterium goes dormant and the elevated antibody decays slowly over ~minutes, conferring durable short-term immunity against reinfection.

## Role in Game Mechanics

### The de-amplifying immune response: why Antibody 2 is built slowly

Reaction 94's 16:12 stoichiometry makes Antibody 2 the **structural outlier** of the antibody block. Every other endogenous antibody reaction in the stock genome produces at least as much antibody as the antigen it consumes, and reactions 92/93 (Antibody 0 and 1) amplify 6×. Reaction 94 goes the other way — 16 antigen molecules are destroyed to create just 12 antibody molecules. This is not a mistake; it is a deliberate design choice that shapes how Antigen-2 infections play out:

| Property | Reaction 92 (Antibody 0) | Reaction 93 (Antibody 1) | **Reaction 94 (Antibody 2)** | Reaction 96 (Antibody 3) |
|----------|--------------------------|--------------------------|------------------------------|--------------------------|
| Reactant threshold | 2× antigen | 2× antigen | **16× antigen** | 1× antigen |
| Antibody output | 12 | 12 | 12 | 1 |
| Amplification ratio | 6× | 6× | **0.75×** (de-amplifying) | 1× |
| Symptom by-product | 1× Histamine B | 1× Histamine A | **2× Coldness** | 2× Coldness |
| Half-life | Short (52) | Short (52) | Short (64) | (varies) |

The practical consequence for Antigen-2 infections is that the immune response is **threshold-gated and bursty**. A bacterium injects Antigen 2 at 0.02 units/tick, so it takes roughly 800 ticks (~26 seconds of uninterrupted infection) just to build the first 16-unit reservoir — and passive antigen decay (Long, 1,670 ticks, ~0.042%/tick) eats into that build-up continuously. Light, brief infections never cross the threshold and never produce any Antibody 2 at all: the creature accumulates slow tissue damage from the five Antigen-2 injury receptors and the antigen then passively fades, leaving the Antibody 2 level untouched at its 46-unit baseline. Moderate-to-heavy infections eventually push Antigen 2 over 16 units, at which point reaction 94 fires in bursts — each firing consumes 16 antigen, produces 12 antibody, and dumps 2 Coldness. The antigen reservoir empties rapidly (Short 64-tick reaction half-life), Antibody 2 jumps by 12 units per firing, and the creature experiences an intermittent feeling-cold / sudden-sleep symptom pattern while the antibody slowly stairs up toward the bacterium's dormancy threshold.

The de-amplifying ratio serves a clear design goal: it makes Antibody 2 accumulation *slower* than for Antigens 0 or 1, reflecting the different symptom profile. Antigen-0 and Antigen-1 infections produce loud respiratory reflexes (sneeze, cough) that players want to bring under control quickly, so their immune responses amplify aggressively. Antigen-2's symptom is behavioural and thermoregulatory — the creature feels cold and may sleep — which is a slower, more tolerable failure mode, so the genome deliberately responds more gradually and gives the infection a longer lifetime before suppression becomes decisive.

### The Coldness by-product: twice the symptom load

Every time reaction 94 fires, it dumps **2 units of Coldness (chemical 152)** into the bloodstream — twice the per-firing symptom load of the histamine-producing antibodies 0 and 1 (which each emit 1 unit of their paired histamine). Coldness has three downstream chemistry effects:

1. **Coldness drive**: receptor on chemical 152 with gain 204 feeds the creature's Coldness drive. Elevated Coldness makes the creature feel cold and motivates it to seek heat sources.
2. **Involuntary Sleep reflex**: at Coldness ≥128, receptor 72 (LOC_INVOLUNTARY4, DIGITAL flag, gain 255) fires the involuntary Sleep reflex — the creature falls asleep regardless of what they were doing.
3. **Thermoregulatory counterpart to Hotness**: Coldness and Hotness (153) participate in the creature's ambient temperature regulation.

The combination of **gated reaction (16-unit threshold)** and **high-load symptom (2× Coldness per firing)** means that when reaction 94 does fire, it fires *hard*: the creature experiences a sudden burst of Coldness that can push the drive up sharply or even cross the Sleep threshold. Players perceive this as "my norn was fine, then suddenly got really cold and fell asleep" — a bursty, episodic symptom unlike the near-continuous sneezing of an Antigen-0 infection. The Antigen-2 infection profile is consequently one of the *subtlest* to diagnose: the sleep episodes look like normal tiredness, and the cold-seeking behaviour looks like ordinary thermoregulation, so players may not even recognise their creature as "sick" — making Antigen 2 a kind of **stealth infection** that can run for longer than the noisier respiratory antigens before the player intervenes.

### The 46-unit starter dose: modest pre-immunity

Gene 18 sets the initial concentration of chemical 104 to 46 units (0.1804 of the 0-255 scale). This is a modest starter dose — about half of Antibody 0's 96 units, and roughly in the middle of the antibody-block range (23 to 96). The design logic is:

1. **Every antibody block chemical has a starter dose.** The antibody block is unique in the stock genome for having genomic initial concentrations on every slot: Antibody 0 = 96, Antibody 1 = 52, Antibody 2 = 46, Antibody 3 = 50, Antibody 4 = 48, Antibody 5 = 56, Antibody 6 = 23, Antibody 7 = 46. The stock creature is *pre-armed* against every antigen from birth.
2. **The starter dose raises the bacterium's dormancy bar on day one.** The bacterium compares the host's antibody level against its own randomly-rolled dormancy threshold. A 46-unit starter dose is enough to trigger dormancy in any bacterium whose roll landed on the low end of the threshold distribution, meaning newborn creatures have non-trivial odds of silencing a mild-to-moderate Antigen-2 bacterium immediately on contact — without ever firing reaction 94 and therefore without ever experiencing the Coldness symptom.
3. **Antibody 2's starter dose is proportionate to Antigen 2's threat profile.** Antigens 0 and 1 are the loud, player-visible infections, so their antibodies get higher starter doses (96, 52); Antibody 2 at 46 units gives baseline protection against the less-noisy chills antigen without over-engineering it.

Because passive decay runs constantly from birth (Long 2,481 ticks), the starter 46 units halves roughly every 82 seconds of play. After about 5 minutes of newborn life (no Antigen 2 exposure), Antibody 2 has decayed to ~10 units — no longer meaningfully above zero. This is why the starter dose matters specifically for the **first minutes** of a creature's life: it protects against early bacterial exposures that a newborn would otherwise fight from a cold start, but fades away by adolescence unless reinforced by actual exposure events.

### Long decay and the memory of infection

The 2,481-tick passive half-life (~1 min 23 s at 30 tps) is the standard antibody decay profile and balances two competing design goals:

- **Memory of exposure**: antibody levels should persist long enough that a creature suppressing a bacterium actually "remembers" the exposure and can fight off a reinfection more easily. A creature that fought off an Antigen-2 bacterium five minutes ago still carries a meaningful fraction of its peak antibody (~12%) — enough to halt most fresh bacterium dormancy rolls.
- **Long-term reinfection possibility**: on time scales of a full in-game day, antibody must decay back toward baseline so that a creature can realistically be reinfected. The Long half-life achieves this: peak antibody from a heavy infection fades to the starter baseline within an hour or so, so long-term immunity is not permanent.

For Antigen 2 specifically, the interplay between reaction 94's reservoir-burn pattern and the Long passive decay produces an unusual temporal signature: **antibody builds in steps of 12** (one per reaction firing, separated by whatever time it takes the bacterium to refill the 16-unit antigen reservoir), rather than the smooth rise characteristic of the 2→12 amplifying reactions of Antigens 0 and 1. Plotting Antibody 2 over time during a chronic Antigen-2 infection shows a staircase pattern of 12-unit steps separated by long quiet intervals, overlaid on the slow exponential decay of the accumulated pool.

### No creature-side receptors: why the creature doesn't "feel" its own antibodies

Antibody 2 has zero receptors on any creature locus — the creature cannot sense its own antibody level, and no drive, reflex or metabolic process depends on it. This is the standard antibody-block design and is intentional for two reasons:

1. **Separation of concerns**: the chemistry handles the biochemical bookkeeping (antibody accumulation and decay), and the bacterium agent handles the game-state effect (dormancy and lifespan drain). Adding a creature-side receptor would duplicate functionality and risk interference between the two systems.
2. **Prevents overdose side effects**: because there is no receptor, a player can safely dose a creature with arbitrary amounts of Antibody 2 (via a community "immune booster" agent or via CAOS injection) without side effects. The excess simply suppresses Antigen-2 bacteria more decisively and then decays passively. This makes community pharma content safe to compose.

The contrast with histamines and Coldness (which have LOC_INVOLUNTARY reflex receptors, drive receptors, and thermoregulatory roles) is instructive: those are *symptomatic* by-products that need to drive visible creature behaviours, so they have receptors; antibodies are *effective* chemicals that need to drive the bacterium's state machine, so they bypass the creature-side receptor system entirely and talk directly to the external agent.

### Strategic / gameplay implications

- **Reaction 94's threshold creates a "mild infection" regime**: short or isolated Antigen-2 exposures never trigger any antibody response at all, so Antibody 2 stays at its 46-unit starter baseline and the creature experiences only the slow injury-receptor damage of the bacterium before it either detaches or is killed. This is by design — the immune response only engages for infections severe enough to warrant it, saving the metabolic cost of antibody manufacture and the Coldness side effect.
- **Sustained infections produce bursty Coldness symptoms**: once reaction 94 starts firing, each activation pulses 2 Coldness into the bloodstream, producing the characteristic "sudden-cold / sudden-sleep" pattern. Players who notice their norn periodically becoming cold and sleepy when near a visible bacterium are watching reaction 94 fire in bursts.
- **Antibody 2 is the truest "infection severity" indicator for Antigen 2**: because reaction 94 is threshold-gated, any Antibody 2 level meaningfully above the 46-unit baseline is definitive evidence of a heavy-enough Antigen-2 infection to trigger the immune response. Unlike Histamines for Antigens 0 and 1 (which can spike from any trace exposure), Antibody 2 over 46 means the creature really has had a serious exposure.
- **Immune memory against Antigen 2 is real but slow to build**: because reaction 94 de-amplifies (0.75× ratio), a creature must endure a long infection to accumulate a sizeable antibody pool. But once built, the Long passive decay preserves the pool for ~minutes, giving durable protection against near-term reinfection. The protection is less dramatic than for Antigens 0/1 (which can build huge antibody pools quickly) but it is equally durable.
- **Anti-bacterial spray kills the bacterium, but antibodies linger**: after a player uses the anti-bacterial spray to kill an Antigen-2 bacterium, Antigen 2 levels drop, reaction 94 stops firing, and Coldness fades over ~minutes. But the accumulated Antibody 2 persists for ~1.5 minutes per half-life, providing durable post-infection coverage against immediate reinfection.
- **Community vaccine agents bypass the threshold gate**: because reaction 94 requires 16 units of Antigen 2 to fire, a creature can never have Antibody 2 above baseline without a *heavy* Antigen-2 exposure. A community vaccine agent that injects Antibody 2 directly bypasses this requirement, pre-loading the creature with immunity against Antigen-2 bacteria without ever having them experience the exposure. This is especially useful for Antigen 2 compared to Antigens 0 and 1: the threshold-gate makes organic immunity-building for Antigen 2 slow and uncertain, so direct antibody injection provides a proportionally larger protection boost.

### Diagnostic visibility

Antibody 2 is not named in the Medical Pod's toxin panel (it is not a toxin), but it is visible via:

- **Chemistry panel (slot 104)**: direct numeric readout, showing the current antibody level. Values significantly above the 46-unit baseline indicate an active or recent heavy Antigen-2 infection.
- **Debug console**: `CHEM TARG 104` returns the current concentration on demand.
- **Bacterium behaviour inference**: if an Antigen-2 bacterium on a creature suddenly stops producing Coldness bursts and the creature stops feeling cold, the player can infer that Antibody 2 has crossed the bacterium's dormancy threshold.

The clinical signature of a creature mid-infection is: rising Antibody 2 (in 12-unit steps), elevated Antigen 2 (periodically spiking past 16 then dropping), elevated Coldness drive, occasional involuntary sleep. The signature of a creature post-infection is: high Antibody 2, near-baseline Antigen 2, near-baseline Coldness, no cold-seeking behaviour — and the residual high Antibody 2 is what gives the creature its short-term acquired immunity against re-infection.

### The antibody block as a whole

Antibody 2 sits in the middle of the antibody block (102-109), which shares the same passive decay (2,481 ticks, Long) and the same "no receptors" design across all eight slots. The block's structure mirrors the antigen block 82-89 one-to-one:

| Antigen | Paired Antibody | Production Reaction | Amplification | Symptom By-product |
|---------|------------------|---------------------|---------------|---------------------|
| Antigen 0 (82) | Antibody 0 (102) | Reaction 92 (Short, 52-tick) | 2:12 (6×) | Histamine B (74) |
| Antigen 1 (83) | Antibody 1 (103) | Reaction 93 (Short, 52-tick) | 2:12 (6×) | Histamine A (73) |
| Antigen 2 (84) | **Antibody 2 (104)** | **Reaction 94 (Short, 64-tick)** | **16:12 (0.75×)** | **Coldness (152, ×2)** |
| Antigen 3 (85) | Antibody 3 (105) | Reaction 96 | 1:1 | Coldness (152, ×2) |
| Antigen 4 (86) | Antibody 4 (106) | Reaction 95 | 2:3 | Hotness (153) |
| Antigen 5 (87) | Antibody 5 (107) | Reaction 97 | 1:3 | Chemical 90 |
| Antigen 6 (88) | Antibody 6 (108) | Reaction 98 | 1:3 | Hotness (153) |
| Antigen 7 (89) | Antibody 7 (109) | Reaction 99 | 1:3 | Pain (148) |

Antibody 2 is the **only de-amplifying antibody response** in the stock genome — every other reaction produces at least as much antibody as antigen consumed, and most amplify. This makes Antibody 2 uniquely slow to accumulate organically, and the genome compensates by giving it a solid 46-unit starter dose and by pairing it with a relatively high 2×-Coldness per-firing symptom pulse, so when the reaction *does* fire the creature's response is decisive and the bacterium is driven toward dormancy in a handful of firings rather than through dozens.

## Summary

Antibody 2 is chemical 104 and the dedicated immune response product for [Antigen 2 (084)](084%20-%20Antigen%202.md), the "chills / sleepy-cold" antigen whose infections manifest as elevated Coldness drive and, at high concentration, involuntary sleep. It is produced by **reaction 94** (gene 87, `16× Antigen 2 → 12× Antibody 2 + 2× Coldness`, Short half-life of 64 ticks) — the *only de-amplifying* antibody reaction in the stock genome, with a 0.75× molecular ratio and a large 16-unit reactant threshold that gates the immune response behind a substantial antigen reservoir. Every creature starts life with a **46-unit baseline concentration** set by gene 18 (modest, in the middle of the antibody-block range), and the chemical clears only via a **Long passive decay** of 2,481 ticks (~1 min 23 s per halving at 30 tps). It has **no receptors** on the creature — no locus reads its concentration, it affects no drive or reflex or metabolic process. Its entire game-mechanical effect is to be read by the external `bacteria.cos` agent: when the host's Antibody 2 rises above an Antigen-2-carrying bacterium's randomly-rolled dormancy threshold, the bacterium goes dormant and stops injecting antigen and toxin; and each tick of elevated antibody drains the bacterium's remaining lifespan by `antibody × 10`, so sustained high antibody eventually kills the bacterium outright. Because reaction 94's 16-unit reactant threshold makes antibody accumulation stepwise and slow, Antigen-2 infections produce a **staircase build-up pattern** in Antibody 2 (12 units per firing, separated by long quiet intervals) rather than the smooth ramp of Antigens 0 and 1, and each firing dumps **2 units of Coldness** into the bloodstream, producing the characteristic intermittent "suddenly cold / suddenly sleepy" symptom signature. The Long passive decay preserves accumulated immunity for minutes after an infection clears, giving meaningful short-term protection against reinfection while fading to baseline over ~hours. With no creature-side receptors, no antidote reactions and no direct physiological effect, Antibody 2 exemplifies Creatures 3's modular chemistry design: a pure "signalling" chemical whose meaning lives entirely in the communication between the biology genome (reaction 94, gene 18) and the external agent ecosystem (`bacteria.cos` reading `chem 104`). Player-side, it is the truest indicator of whether an Antigen-2 infection has been severe enough to engage the creature's immune system — the numeric heartbeat of adaptive immunity against the stealthiest of the eight canonical antigens.
