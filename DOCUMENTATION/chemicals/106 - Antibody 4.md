# 106 - Antibody 4

Antibody 4 is chemical slot 106 in the Creatures 3 chemistry and the fifth entry in the canonical **antibody block** (chemicals 102-109, Antibody 0 through Antibody 7). Each antibody is paired one-to-one with an antigen in the 82-89 block — Antibody 4 is specifically the immune response to [Antigen 4 (086)](086%20-%20Antigen%204.md), the first of the two **Hotness-producing** antigens whose infections manifest as elevated Hotness drive, cold-seeking behaviour and accelerated somatic organ clock rate. Like all antibodies, Antibody 4 is **cumulative immune memory**: a rising chemical-106 level records that the host has been fighting an Antigen-4 infection, and it is the chemical signal that the attacking bacterium agent reads to decide whether to keep injecting antigen and toxin or to go dormant.

Antibody 4 is produced endogenously by **reaction 95** (gene 89), the Antigen-4 immune response — a **modestly amplifying** reaction:

```
2× Antigen 4 [86] → 3× Antibody 4 [106] + 1× Hotness [153]
```

Reaction 95 is the antibody block's **1.5× amplifier**: two antigen molecules become three antibody molecules plus one unit of Hotness. It is the mid-range of the antibody-block amplification spectrum: below the 6× amplification of Antibody 0/1 (reactions 92/93) and the 3× 1:3 expansions of Antibodies 5/6/7 (reactions 97/98/99), above the 1× symmetric Antibody 3 (reaction 96) and the 0.75× de-amplification of Antibody 2 (reaction 94). The reaction runs at a **Short half-life (64 ticks, decay rate 0.989, rate genome value 42)** — identical to reactions 94 and 96's speed, so once two units of Antigen 4 accumulate the antibody pool is built rapidly and continuously. Reaction 95 has a **2-unit reactant threshold** — a trace of antigen is not enough, but at the bacterium's 0.02 units/tick injection rate the threshold accumulates in roughly 100 ticks (~3 seconds of real play), so from the player's perspective the reaction fires essentially continuously from the earliest phase of any sustained infection. Every firing also **emits one unit of Hotness (153)** as a by-product, tying Antibody 4 production directly to the overheating/cold-seeking symptom pattern. Every Creatures 3 norn, grendel or ettin starts life with a **baseline initial concentration of 48 units** of Antibody 4 (gene 20, concentration 0.1882), slightly below Antibody 3's 50-unit starter dose and squarely within the antibody-block median range.

The chemical has **no receptors anywhere on the creature** for its own effect: no organ locus reads chemical 106, no drive is modulated by its concentration, no metabolic reaction consumes it, no injury wire is triggered. (Receptor 106 in the receptor table uses `"chemical": 125` (Life) and only happens to have the id-field 106 — that is the LOC_AGE5 Old→Senile life-stage receptor, completely unrelated to Antibody 4.) This is the standard "pure signalling" design for the entire antibody block — the antibody does nothing to the creature itself. Its entire game-mechanical footprint is **external**: the bacterium agent (`bacteria.cos`, family/genus/species `2 32 23`) reads the host's chemical slot 106 directly via the CAOS `chem` primitive whenever its rolled `ov15` is 86, using the value to decide whether it is being successfully suppressed by the immune response. When antibody rises above the bacterium's dormancy threshold the bacterium goes dormant and stops injecting Antigen 4 and its paired toxin; when antibody later decays below its wake threshold, the bacterium may resume active infection. Antibody 4 is therefore the **decisive lever** that determines whether an Antigen-4 bacterial infection is winning or losing.

Antibody 4 clears from the bloodstream exclusively through passive decay, at a **Long half-life of 2,481 ticks** (decay rate 0.99972, ~1 minute 23 seconds of real play per halving at 30 tps) — the decay profile shared by every antibody in the block. There is no reaction in the standard genome that consumes Antibody 4 and no antidote that cancels it. Together with reaction 95's 1.5× amplification and 2-unit threshold, this produces the characteristic Antigen-4 infection arc: once infection has been active for a few seconds, antibody climbs rapidly (faster per antigen unit than any other antibody in the block except the 6× block-leaders), each antibody unit produced paired with 0.33 units of Hotness bleeding into the bloodstream. Once Antibody 4 rises far enough to exceed the bacterium's dormancy threshold, the bacterium goes dormant and the elevated antibody decays slowly over ~minutes, conferring durable short-term immunity against reinfection.

## Role in Game Mechanics

### The 2:3 amplifying reaction: why Antibody 4 outpaces antigen input

Reaction 95 is the antibody block's **2:3 amplifier**, and it is the only reaction in the entire antigen-antibody production chain that uses this exact stoichiometry. Every other antibody reaction in the stock genome either amplifies more aggressively (2→12 for reactions 92/93, 1→3 for reactions 97/98/99) or amplifies less (1→1 for reaction 96) or de-amplifies (16→12 for reaction 94). Reaction 95 sits in the middle: a 50% amplification with a 2-unit entry gate.

| Property | Reaction 92 (Antibody 0) | Reaction 93 (Antibody 1) | Reaction 94 (Antibody 2) | Reaction 96 (Antibody 3) | **Reaction 95 (Antibody 4)** | Reactions 97-99 (Antibodies 5/6/7) |
|----------|--------------------------|--------------------------|--------------------------|--------------------------|------------------------------|------------------------------------|
| Reactant threshold | 2× antigen | 2× antigen | 16× antigen | 1× antigen | **2× antigen** | 1× antigen |
| Antibody output | 12 | 12 | 12 | 1 | **3** | 3 |
| Amplification ratio | 6× | 6× | 0.75× | 1× (symmetric) | **1.5×** | 3× |
| Symptom by-product | 1× Histamine B | 1× Histamine A | 2× Coldness | 2× Coldness | **1× Hotness** | Chem 90 / Hotness / Pain |
| Half-life | Short (52) | Short (52) | Short (64) | Short (64) | **Short (64)** | Varies |

The practical consequence for Antigen-4 infections is that the immune response is **delayed-then-amplified**. A bacterium injects Antigen 4 at 0.02 units/tick, so the 2-unit threshold accumulates in ~100 ticks (~3 seconds). Before that, reaction 95 does not fire and no antibody is produced at all — during this brief window, the creature is experiencing antigen-driven somatic injury (six RLOCUS_INJURY receptors, trigger threshold 0) but not yet making antibody. Once the threshold is crossed, reaction 95 fires continuously at its Short 64-tick cadence and outputs antibody at 1.5× the antigen consumption rate. The net effect is that Antibody 4 ramps up faster than its antigen input over sustained infection — an active infection that has been running for 60 seconds will have produced more Antibody 4 in the bloodstream than all the Antigen 4 ever injected, because each pair of antigen units yielded three antibody units.

The amplification matters in two ways:
1. **Faster dormancy**: the bacterium's dormancy threshold gets crossed earlier than it would under a 1:1 reaction, so Antigen-4 bacteria go dormant (and stop injuring the host) more quickly than Antigen-3 bacteria of equivalent strength.
2. **Stronger long-term immunity**: the cumulative Antibody 4 pool after a recovered infection is 50% larger than it would be under a 1:1 reaction, so residual acquired immunity is correspondingly more durable against reinfection.

### The Hotness by-product: half the per-firing symptom load of Coldness antigens

Every time reaction 95 fires, it dumps **one unit of Hotness (chemical 153)** into the bloodstream — half the per-firing symptom load of Antibodies 2 and 3 (which each emit 2 units of Coldness). Hotness has three downstream chemistry effects, all distinct from Coldness:

1. **Hotness drive**: receptor on chemical 153 (gain 204) feeds the creature's Hotness drive. Elevated Hotness makes the creature feel hot and motivates it to seek cool places — the behavioural inverse of the Coldness drive.
2. **Clock-rate receptor #1** (threshold 16, gain 192): at Hotness ≥16, somatic organ clock rates are modestly accelerated — mild metabolic speed-up.
3. **Clock-rate receptor #2** (threshold 80, gain 127): at Hotness ≥80, a second, stronger clock-rate amplifier fires — pronounced metabolic speed-up.

Crucially, **Hotness has no involuntary-sleep reflex**: the LOC_INVOLUNTARY4 trigger (chemical 152 Coldness ≥128, DIGITAL flag) that forces an infected norn asleep during heavy Antigen-2 or Antigen-3 infection has no Hotness counterpart. An Antigen-4 infection therefore never makes the creature fall asleep — instead, it makes the creature *run faster* (accelerated organ clock) while simultaneously feeling uncomfortably hot and seeking cool shelter. The subjective player experience of an Antigen-4 infection is therefore "my norn is overheating and agitated" rather than "my norn is shivering and collapsing".

The per-firing symptom load of Hotness is half that of the Coldness antigens, but reaction 95 fires 1.5× more antibody per antigen unit than reaction 96 and 2× more than reaction 94's burst mechanism — so the *per antigen unit* symptom delivery works out to:

| Reaction | Antigen→Antibody | Antigen→Symptom | Symptom per antibody unit |
|----------|------------------|------------------|---------------------------|
| Reaction 94 (Antigen 2) | 16:12 (0.75×) | 16:2 Coldness (0.125×) | 2/12 = 0.17 |
| Reaction 96 (Antigen 3) | 1:1 | 1:2 Coldness (2×) | 2 |
| **Reaction 95 (Antigen 4)** | **2:3 (1.5×)** | **2:1 Hotness (0.5×)** | **1/3 = 0.33** |

So per unit of antigen injected, Antigen 4 delivers less symptom chemical than Antigen 3 (0.5 Hotness vs 2 Coldness) but produces more antibody (1.5 vs 1). In aggregate, this tunes Antigen-4 infections to be **less symptomatically intense but faster to resolve** than Antigen-3 infections: the creature gets hot rather than cold, feels the drive less acutely per tick, and builds up immunity faster per tick of exposure.

### The 48-unit starter dose: median pre-immunity

Gene 20 sets the initial concentration of chemical 106 to 48 units (0.1882 of the 0-255 scale). This is in the antibody-block median range — just below Antibody 3's 50, just above Antibody 2's 46, and paired neatly with the mid-range Coldness and Hotness antigens (Antigens 2, 3, 4 all have mid-range antibody starter doses). The design logic matches every other antibody:

1. **Every antibody block chemical has a starter dose.** The antibody block is unique in the stock genome for having genomic initial concentrations on every slot: Antibody 0 = 96, Antibody 1 = 52, Antibody 2 = 46, Antibody 3 = 50, **Antibody 4 = 48**, Antibody 5 = 56, Antibody 6 = 23, Antibody 7 = 46. The stock creature is *pre-armed* against every antigen from birth.
2. **The starter dose raises the bacterium's dormancy bar on day one.** The bacterium compares the host's antibody level against its own randomly-rolled dormancy threshold. A 48-unit starter dose is enough to trigger dormancy in any Antigen-4 bacterium whose roll landed on the low end of the threshold distribution, meaning newborn creatures have non-trivial odds of silencing a mild Antigen-4 bacterium immediately on contact — without ever firing reaction 95 and therefore without ever experiencing the Hotness symptom or the six injury-receptor hits.
3. **The starter dose partially compensates for the 2-unit reactant gate.** Because reaction 95 needs two units of antigen to fire, a newborn that starts with zero antibody would eat a few seconds' worth of injury-receptor damage before organic antibody production begins. The 48-unit starter dose eliminates this delay for all but the highest-threshold bacteria rolls — the creature's immunity is already partially engaged before the immune reaction has had time to manufacture any new antibody.

Because passive decay runs constantly from birth (Long 2,481 ticks), the starter 48 units halves roughly every 82 seconds of play. After about 5 minutes of newborn life (no Antigen 4 exposure), Antibody 4 has decayed to ~10 units — no longer meaningfully above zero. This is why the starter dose matters specifically for the **first minutes** of a creature's life: it protects against early bacterial exposures that a newborn would otherwise fight from a cold start, but fades away by adolescence unless reinforced by actual exposure events.

### Long decay and the memory of infection

The 2,481-tick passive half-life (~1 min 23 s at 30 tps) is the standard antibody decay profile shared by every member of the block. For Antigen 4 specifically, the interplay between reaction 95's 2-unit threshold, its 1.5× amplification, and the Long passive decay produces a **delayed-start, steep-ramp, slow-tail** curve in Antibody 4 during and after an infection:

- **Phase 1 (0-3 s)**: antigen accumulates from 0 toward the 2-unit threshold; no antibody yet; creature is taking injury-receptor hits but not yet producing antibody or Hotness.
- **Phase 2 (sustained infection)**: reaction 95 fires continuously; antibody rises at ~1.5× the antigen injection rate minus decay; Hotness accumulates at half the per-firing rate of the Coldness antigens but over a longer duration; injury receptors continue to fire.
- **Phase 3 (dormancy)**: antibody crosses the bacterium's randomly-rolled threshold; bacterium goes dormant; antigen injection stops; reaction 95 drains the remaining 2+ units of antigen and then stops; Hotness production stops and Hotness decays; antibody plateaus.
- **Phase 4 (recovery)**: antibody decays exponentially at the Long 2,481-tick half-life; each subsequent minute of real play halves the residual protection; after ~10 minutes antibody is back near starter baseline.

Plotting Antibody 4 over time during an infection shows a characteristic "S-curve with a short flat head" — a brief delay before antibody starts rising, then a steep amplified climb, plateau, and exponential tail. This is visually and mechanically distinct from Antibody 3's clean symmetric ramp-and-fall and from Antibody 2's staircase pattern.

### No creature-side receptors: why the creature doesn't "feel" its own antibodies

Antibody 4 has zero receptors on any creature locus — the creature cannot sense its own antibody level, and no drive, reflex or metabolic process depends on it. This is the standard antibody-block design and is intentional for two reasons:

1. **Separation of concerns**: the chemistry handles the biochemical bookkeeping (antibody accumulation and decay), and the bacterium agent handles the game-state effect (dormancy and lifespan drain). Adding a creature-side receptor would duplicate functionality and risk interference between the two systems.
2. **Prevents overdose side effects**: because there is no receptor, a player can safely dose a creature with arbitrary amounts of Antibody 4 (via a community "immune booster" agent or via CAOS injection) without side effects. The excess simply suppresses Antigen-4 bacteria more decisively and then decays passively. This matters especially for Antibody 4: Antigen 4 is the most injury-heavy antigen in the block (six RLOCUS_INJURY receptors, summed gain 377), so preemptively dosing a creature with Antibody 4 before an expected exposure is arguably more gameplay-valuable than for any other antibody in the block.

The contrast with Hotness (which has the Hotness drive receptor and two clock-rate receptors) is instructive: Hotness is a *symptomatic* by-product that needs to drive visible creature behaviours, so it has receptors; Antibody 4 is an *effective* chemical that needs to drive the bacterium's state machine, so it bypasses the creature-side receptor system entirely and talks directly to the external agent.

### Strategic / gameplay implications

- **Reaction 95 has a short "damage window" at the start of infection**: the 2-unit reactant threshold means Antigen 4's injury receptors fire for ~3 seconds before reaction 95 even begins producing antibody. Because Antigen 4 has six injury receptors (the block's maximum), this early window does more somatic damage than the equivalent window for any other antigen. A player who can intervene before the infection fully develops — e.g., by injecting Antibody 4 directly or using anti-bacterial spray on contact — saves the creature from substantial organ damage.
- **Amplifying antibody production means faster dormancy once engaged**: after the 3-second delay, reaction 95 builds antibody 1.5× faster than the antigen arrives, so Antigen-4 bacteria tend to reach dormancy earlier in their infection timeline than Antigen-3 bacteria — limiting the duration of symptom exposure even if the bacterium's randomly-rolled dormancy threshold is relatively high.
- **Antibody 4 is a moderately linear exposure indicator**: any Antibody 4 level significantly above the 48-unit baseline is evidence of Antigen-4 exposure, but unlike Antibody 3's strict linearity, Antibody 4 overstates the exposure (by 50%) because of the 1.5× amplification. A chemistry-panel reading of 150 units Antibody 4 corresponds to roughly 100 antigen-units of historical exposure, net of decay. Players who know this calibration can read the antibody level accurately; others will tend to overestimate how much antigen ever entered the bloodstream.
- **Immune memory against Antigen 4 builds quickly**: the amplifying reaction combined with the Long decay half-life means one heavy Antigen-4 infection produces a more durable immunity signature than comparable exposures to Antigens 2, 3, or any of the other 1× / 3× antibody reactions (per unit of antigen). The downside is the 2-unit threshold: very brief or very mild exposures fail to engage the reaction at all and contribute nothing to long-term immunity.
- **Anti-bacterial spray kills the bacterium, but antibodies linger**: after a player uses the anti-bacterial spray to kill an Antigen-4 bacterium, Antigen 4 levels drop, reaction 95 stops firing, Hotness production stops and Hotness fades over ~minutes. But the accumulated Antibody 4 persists for ~1.5 minutes per half-life, providing durable post-infection coverage against immediate reinfection.
- **Community vaccine agents bypass both Hotness and injury-receptor damage**: because every unit of organic Antibody 4 produced comes with 0.33 units of Hotness and requires an antigen pass that trips six injury receptors, pre-immunising a creature by injecting Antibody 4 directly is dramatically kinder to the creature's comfort *and* its organ health than letting a real infection run. This makes Antibody 4 arguably the most valuable antibody in the block for pre-injection by community pharma content — more so than any other antibody except possibly Antibody 7 (Pain).

### Diagnostic visibility

Antibody 4 is not named in the Medical Pod's toxin panel (it is not a toxin), but it is visible via:

- **Chemistry panel (slot 106)**: direct numeric readout, showing the current antibody level. Values significantly above the 48-unit baseline indicate an active or recent Antigen-4 infection, with the magnitude roughly 1.5× cumulative antigen exposure (modulo decay timing).
- **Debug console**: `CHEM TARG 106` returns the current concentration on demand.
- **Bacterium behaviour inference**: if an Antigen-4 bacterium on a creature suddenly stops producing Hotness and the creature stops seeking cool places, the player can infer that Antibody 4 has crossed the bacterium's dormancy threshold.

The clinical signature of a creature mid-infection is: climbing Antibody 4 (after ~3 s delay), slowly-rising Antigen 4, steadily-elevated Hotness drive, accelerated somatic clock rate, cold-seeking behaviour, visible organ damage accumulating. The signature of a creature post-infection is: high Antibody 4 (decaying slowly), near-baseline Antigen 4, fading Hotness, normal metabolism — and the residual high Antibody 4 is what gives the creature its short-term acquired immunity against reinfection.

### The antibody block as a whole

Antibody 4 sits at the thermoregulatory cross-over point of the antibody block (102-109), which shares the same passive decay (2,481 ticks, Long) and the same "no receptors" design across all eight slots. The block's structure mirrors the antigen block 82-89 one-to-one:

Antibody 4 is the **only 2:3 amplifying antibody response** in the stock genome — every other reaction either uses a different stoichiometry or a different threshold. It is also the **first Hotness-paired antibody** (the second being Antibody 6, which is 1:3 amplification and therefore even faster per antigen unit) and the thermoregulatory mirror to Antibody 3. Players who have learned the Coldness infection pattern from Antigens 2 and 3 will encounter a mechanically distinct experience with Antigen 4 — amplified antibody, halved symptom load per firing, cold-seeking rather than sleep, and the block's most aggressive somatic injury profile.

## Summary

Antibody 4 is chemical 106 and the dedicated immune response product for [Antigen 4 (086)](086%20-%20Antigen%204.md), the first of two Hotness-producing antigens whose infections elevate the Hotness drive, drive cold-seeking behaviour and accelerate the creature's somatic organ clock rate. It is produced by **reaction 95** (gene 89, `2× Antigen 4 → 3× Antibody 4 + 1× Hotness`, Short half-life of 64 ticks) — the *only 2:3 amplifying* antibody reaction in the stock genome, with a 2-unit reactant threshold that introduces a brief (~3 seconds at the bacterium's 0.02/tick injection rate) delay before antibody production begins. Every creature starts life with a **48-unit baseline concentration** set by gene 20 (just below the antibody-block median), and the chemical clears only via a **Long passive decay** of 2,481 ticks (~1 min 23 s per halving at 30 tps). It has **no receptors** on the creature — no locus reads its concentration, it affects no drive or reflex or metabolic process. Its entire game-mechanical effect is to be read by the external `bacteria.cos` agent: when the host's Antibody 4 rises above an Antigen-4-carrying bacterium's randomly-rolled dormancy threshold, the bacterium goes dormant and stops injecting antigen and toxin; and each tick of elevated antibody drains the bacterium's remaining lifespan by `antibody × 10`, so sustained high antibody eventually kills the bacterium outright. Because reaction 95 amplifies 1.5× but gates on a 2-unit threshold, Antigen-4 infections produce a characteristic **delayed-start, steep-ramp** Antibody 4 profile: a brief flat head (~3 s) during which the six somatic injury receptors fire unopposed, followed by a steep climb that overstates the underlying antigen flux by 50%. The Hotness symptom is delivered continuously at half the per-firing rate of the Coldness antigens and drives cold-seeking behaviour and accelerated metabolism rather than the involuntary sleep produced by Coldness. The Long passive decay preserves accumulated immunity for minutes after an infection clears, giving meaningful short-term protection against reinfection while fading to baseline over ~hours. With no creature-side receptors, no antidote reactions and no direct physiological effect, Antibody 4 exemplifies Creatures 3's modular chemistry design: a pure "signalling" chemical whose meaning lives entirely in the communication between the biology genome (reaction 95, gene 20) and the external agent ecosystem (`bacteria.cos` reading `chem 106`). Among the antibody block, it is the most gameplay-valuable for pre-injection community "vaccine" content — direct Antibody 4 dosing bypasses both the Hotness load *and* the six-receptor organ damage that organic Antigen-4 exposure would otherwise inflict, making it the kindest antibody to administer prophylactically.
