# 108 - Antibody 6

Antibody 6 is chemical slot 108 in the Creatures 3 chemistry and the seventh entry in the canonical **antibody block** (chemicals 102-109, Antibody 0 through Antibody 7). Each antibody is paired one-to-one with an antigen in the 82-89 block — Antibody 6 is specifically the immune response to [Antigen 6 (088)](088%20-%20Antigen%206.md), the **milder of the two Hotness-producing antigens** whose infection manifests as elevated Hotness drive, cold-seeking behaviour and accelerated somatic organ clock rate (but no involuntary-sleep reflex). Like all antibodies, Antibody 6 is **cumulative immune memory**: a rising chemical-108 level records that the host has been fighting an Antigen-6 infection, and it is the chemical signal that the attacking bacterium agent reads to decide whether to keep injecting antigen and toxin or to go dormant.

Antibody 6 is produced endogenously by **reaction 98** (gene 91), the Antigen-6 immune response — a **3× amplifying** reaction:

```
1× Antigen 6 [88] → 3× Antibody 6 [108] + 1× Hotness [153]
```

Reaction 98 is the second member of the antibody block's **"Medium-cadence triplet"** of 1:3 amplifiers (reactions 97, 98, 99 for Antibodies 5, 6, 7), sitting structurally between the block's 6× block-leaders (reactions 92/93 for Antibodies 0/1, 2:12 stoichiometry) and the weaker 2:3 amplifier of Antibody 4 (reaction 95, 1.5×). The reaction uses a **1-unit reactant threshold** — the lowest possible firing threshold — so a trace of Antigen 6 is enough to engage the immune response, and reaction 98 fires from the very first tick of any bacterial contact. It runs at a **Medium half-life (116 ticks, decay rate 0.994, rate genome value 48)** — slower than the Short antibody reactions (reactions 92-96 at 52 or 64 ticks) but identical to reaction 97's Medium cadence. Every firing also **emits one unit of Hotness (153)** as a by-product — the same thermoregulatory symptom chemical produced by reaction 95 for Antibody 4, tying Antibody 6 production directly to the overheating/cold-seeking symptom pattern. Every Creatures 3 norn, grendel or ettin starts life with a **baseline initial concentration of 23 units** of Antibody 6 (gene 22, concentration 0.0902) — **the lowest starter dose in the entire antibody block**, less than a quarter of Antibody 0's 96 units and less than half of most other antibodies' starter doses.

The chemical has **no receptors anywhere on the creature** for its own effect: no organ locus reads chemical 108, no drive is modulated by its concentration, no metabolic reaction consumes it, no injury wire is triggered. (Receptor 108 in the receptor table uses `"chemical": 35` (ATP) on Immune tissue and only happens to have the id-field 108 — that is a LOC_DIE receptor firing when Immune-tissue ATP drops below 19 to trigger immune-cell apoptosis from metabolic starvation, completely unrelated to Antibody 6.) This is the standard "pure signalling" design for the entire antibody block — the antibody does nothing to the creature itself. Its entire game-mechanical footprint is **external**: the bacterium agent (`bacteria.cos`, family/genus/species `2 32 23`) reads the host's chemical slot 108 directly via the CAOS `chem` primitive whenever its rolled `ov15` is 88, using the value to decide whether it is being successfully suppressed by the immune response. When antibody rises above the bacterium's dormancy threshold the bacterium goes dormant and stops injecting Antigen 6 and its paired toxin; when antibody later decays below its wake threshold, the bacterium may resume active infection. Antibody 6 is therefore the **decisive lever** that determines whether an Antigen-6 bacterial infection is winning or losing.

Antibody 6 clears from the bloodstream exclusively through passive decay, at a **Long half-life of 2,481 ticks** (decay rate 0.99972, ~1 minute 23 seconds of real play per halving at 30 tps) — the decay profile shared by every antibody in the block. There is no reaction in the standard genome that consumes Antibody 6 and no antidote that cancels it. Together with reaction 98's 3× amplification and 1-unit threshold, this produces the characteristic Antigen-6 infection arc: from the very first tick of bacterial contact, antibody rises at three times the antigen flux, each antibody unit produced paired with 0.33 units of Hotness bleeding into the bloodstream. Once Antibody 6 rises far enough to exceed the bacterium's dormancy threshold, the bacterium goes dormant and the elevated antibody decays slowly over ~minutes, conferring durable short-term immunity against reinfection.

## Role in Game Mechanics

### The 1:3 amplifying reaction: the fastest-to-engage antibody response in the block

Reaction 98 belongs to the antibody block's **"Medium-cadence triplet"** (reactions 97, 98, 99 for Antibodies 5, 6, 7), and shares with its triplet-siblings the combination of a **1-unit reactant threshold** and **3× amplification**. This combination makes reaction 98 the fastest-to-engage antibody response in the block — the moment any Antigen-6-carrying bacterium injects its first 0.02 units of antigen, the reaction begins producing antibody on its next cadence tick, ~50 ticks (~1.7 seconds) from exposure onset.

| Property | Reaction 92 (Antibody 0) | Reaction 93 (Antibody 1) | Reaction 94 (Antibody 2) | Reaction 96 (Antibody 3) | Reaction 95 (Antibody 4) | Reaction 97 (Antibody 5) | **Reaction 98 (Antibody 6)** | Reaction 99 (Antibody 7) |
|----------|--------------------------|--------------------------|--------------------------|--------------------------|--------------------------|--------------------------|------------------------------|--------------------------|
| Reactant threshold | 2× antigen | 2× antigen | 16× antigen | 1× antigen | 2× antigen | 1× antigen | **1× antigen** | 1× antigen |
| Antibody output | 12 | 12 | 12 | 1 | 3 | 3 | **3** | 3 |
| Amplification ratio | 6× | 6× | 0.75× | 1× (symmetric) | 1.5× | 3× | **3×** | 3× |
| Symptom by-product | 1× Histamine B | 1× Histamine A | 2× Coldness | 2× Coldness | 1× Hotness | 1× Chemical 90 | **1× Hotness** | 1× Pain |
| Reaction half-life | Short (52) | Short (52) | Short (64) | Short (64) | Short (64) | Medium (116) | **Medium (116)** | Medium (105) |

The practical consequence for Antigen-6 infections is that the immune response is **immediate-start, amplified, and paced**. Where Antibody 4's reaction needs ~3 seconds to build up its 2-unit reactant pool before firing at all, reaction 98 fires at the first cadence tick after bacterial contact. The Medium 116-tick cadence is slower than the Short reactions — at 30 tps the reaction fires roughly every 3.9 seconds rather than every 1.7-2.1 seconds — but the 1-unit threshold and 3× amplification together mean that antibody begins accumulating from tick one of the infection and does so at three times the antigen input rate.

Relative to reaction 95 (Antibody 4, the other Hotness antibody), reaction 98 trades Short cadence for a lower firing threshold and higher amplification:

- **Reaction 95 (Antibody 4)**: 2-unit threshold, 2:3 stoichiometry (1.5×), Short 64-tick cadence, 1× Hotness by-product → *delayed* start, fast per-tick build-up, 0.5× Hotness per unit of antigen consumed.
- **Reaction 98 (Antibody 6)**: 1-unit threshold, 1:3 stoichiometry (3×), Medium 116-tick cadence, 1× Hotness by-product → *immediate* start, slower per-tick firings but 2× antibody-per-antigen, 1.0× Hotness per unit of antigen consumed.

So per unit of antigen injected, Antigen 6 produces **twice as much antibody** and **twice as much Hotness** as Antigen 4. The fast engagement and high amplification together mean that Antigen-6 bacteria are driven to dormancy faster than Antigen-4 bacteria in per-tick terms — part of why Antigen 6 is the "milder twin" despite sharing the same symptom chemical. The elevated per-antigen Hotness output does not translate to harsher symptoms overall because Antigen 6's bacterium injection rate and low injury receptor count (only four, summed gain 247) keep net antigen flux and direct damage low.

### The Hotness by-product: shared symptom footprint with Antibody 4

Every time reaction 98 fires, it dumps **one unit of Hotness (chemical 153)** into the bloodstream — the same by-product released by reaction 95 (Antibody 4). Hotness has three downstream chemistry effects:

1. **Hotness drive**: receptor on chemical 153 (gain 204) feeds the creature's Hotness drive. Elevated Hotness makes the creature feel hot and motivates it to seek cool places — the behavioural inverse of the Coldness drive.
2. **Clock-rate receptor #1** (threshold 16, gain 192): at Hotness ≥16, somatic organ clock rates are modestly accelerated — mild metabolic speed-up.
3. **Clock-rate receptor #2** (threshold 80, gain 127): at Hotness ≥80, a second, stronger clock-rate amplifier fires — pronounced metabolic speed-up.

Crucially, **Hotness has no involuntary-sleep reflex**: the LOC_INVOLUNTARY4 trigger (chemical 152 Coldness ≥128, DIGITAL flag) that forces an infected norn asleep during heavy Antigen-2 or Antigen-3 infection has no Hotness counterpart. An Antigen-6 infection therefore never makes the creature fall asleep — instead, it makes the creature *run faster* (accelerated organ clock) while simultaneously feeling uncomfortably hot and seeking cool shelter.

Because Antigens 4 and 6 share the same Hotness symptom chemical, they are **clinically indistinguishable at the behavioural level**: both produce cold-seeking creatures with accelerated metabolism. The only way to tell them apart without chemistry-panel inspection is through indirect cues (Antigen 4 inflicts heavier tissue damage via its six injury receptors, so an Antigen-4 infection tends to show more visible organ damage) or by watching which antibody slot rises — Antibody 4 or Antibody 6.

The per-firing symptom load of reaction 98 is 1× Hotness per 1 Antigen 6 consumed (ratio 1.0 Hotness per unit antigen), compared to reaction 95's 1× Hotness per 2 Antigen 4 consumed (ratio 0.5 Hotness per unit antigen). So per unit of antigen, Antigen 6 delivers twice the Hotness of Antigen 4. However, Antigen 6 bacteria inject antigen at the same per-tick rate as Antigen 4 bacteria (0.02/tick), so the *absolute* Hotness production rate during an active infection depends entirely on how aggressively reaction 98 is firing — which it does continuously once engaged, making Antigen-6 infections symptomatically comparable to (or slightly hotter than) Antigen-4 infections, counterbalanced by their lower direct tissue-damage profile.

### The 23-unit starter dose: lowest pre-immunity in the block

Gene 22 sets the initial concentration of chemical 108 to 23 units (0.0902 of the 0-255 scale). This is the **lowest starter dose in the entire antibody block**, substantially below every other antibody:

- **Antibody 0 starter: 96** (highest) — aggressive Histamine B response.
- **Antibody 5 starter: 56** — compensates for Medium cadence.
- **Antibody 1 starter: 52**, Antibody 3 starter: 50, Antibody 4 starter: 48, Antibody 2/7 starter: 46.
- **Antibody 6 starter: 23** (lowest).

The low starter dose is a deliberate design choice that reflects Antibody 6's unique combination of **low cost** and **high engagement speed**:

1. **Low cost**: Antigen 6 is "the mild overheating antigen" — it has the smallest injury-receptor set in the antigen block (summed gain 247, ~35% less than Antigen 4's 377) and the fastest passive decay (1,018 ticks). A newborn that catches an Antigen-6 bacterium with no pre-armed antibody suffers relatively little direct damage while reaction 98 ramps up.
2. **High engagement speed**: reaction 98's 1-unit threshold means antibody begins being manufactured at the very first antigen unit, and the 3× amplification means the manufactured pool rises quickly. A creature that starts with only 23 units of Antibody 6 can still reach meaningful dormancy thresholds within ~30-60 seconds of sustained infection, thanks to the amplification.

Because passive decay runs constantly from birth (Long 2,481 ticks), the starter 23 units halves roughly every 82 seconds of play. After about 2-3 minutes of newborn life (no Antigen 6 exposure), Antibody 6 has decayed to near zero. The low starter dose therefore has **relatively little protective value** beyond the first minute or two of the creature's life — newborn immunity against Antigen 6 is essentially a "get acquired immunity quickly" design rather than a "start with pre-immunity" design, consistent with the fast-engagement reaction profile.

The 23-unit starter is enough to trigger dormancy in any Antigen-6 bacterium whose randomly-rolled dormancy threshold landed on the very low end of the distribution, but it gives much weaker first-contact protection than Antibody 0's 96 units or Antibody 5's 56 units. In practice, almost every Antigen-6 infection will actually engage reaction 98 and build antibody organically, as opposed to being silenced immediately by starter-dose baseline.

### Long decay and the memory of infection

The 2,481-tick passive half-life (~1 min 23 s at 30 tps) is the standard antibody decay profile shared by every member of the block. For Antigen 6 specifically, the interplay between reaction 98's 1-unit threshold, its 3× amplification, and the Long passive decay produces a **no-delay, steep-ramp, slow-tail** curve in Antibody 6 during and after an infection:

- **Phase 1 (0-2 s)**: antigen accumulates from 0 toward 1 unit; the reaction fires on the next Medium-cadence tick as soon as that 1-unit threshold is met; antibody begins accumulating at 3× the antigen input rate; four somatic injury receptors fire on Antigen 6 but at the block's lowest summed gain (247), and Hotness begins trickling into the bloodstream at the reaction's firing rate.
- **Phase 2 (sustained infection)**: reaction 98 fires every ~3.9 seconds; antibody rises steadily at 3× antigen injection rate minus decay; Hotness accumulates and begins driving the Hotness drive; at Hotness ≥16 somatic organ clock rate accelerates; at Hotness ≥80 the second clock-rate receptor fires and metabolism speeds up significantly; injury receptors continue to fire but at the block's lowest damage rate.
- **Phase 3 (dormancy)**: antibody crosses the bacterium's randomly-rolled threshold; bacterium goes dormant; antigen injection stops; reaction 98 drains remaining antigen and stops firing; Hotness production stops and Hotness decays; clock-rate receptors disengage; antibody plateaus.
- **Phase 4 (recovery)**: antibody decays exponentially at the Long 2,481-tick half-life; each subsequent minute of real play halves the residual protection; after ~10 minutes antibody is back near (the low) starter baseline.

Plotting Antibody 6 over time during an infection shows the same "steep-from-start S-curve" shape as Antibody 5 — no flat head, an immediate steep climb, plateau, and exponential tail. The steep climb is particularly pronounced for Antibody 6 because the starter dose is so low: the ratio of peak antibody to starting antibody is the largest in the block, giving Antigen-6 infections a characteristic dramatic-looking chemistry panel signature.

### No creature-side receptors: why the creature doesn't "feel" its own antibodies

Antibody 6 has zero receptors on any creature locus — the creature cannot sense its own antibody level, and no drive, reflex or metabolic process depends on it. This is the standard antibody-block design and is intentional for two reasons:

1. **Separation of concerns**: the chemistry handles the biochemical bookkeeping (antibody accumulation and decay), and the bacterium agent handles the game-state effect (dormancy and lifespan drain). Adding a creature-side receptor would duplicate functionality and risk interference between the two systems.
2. **Prevents overdose side effects**: because there is no receptor, a player can safely dose a creature with arbitrary amounts of Antibody 6 (via a community "immune booster" agent or via CAOS injection) without side effects. The excess simply suppresses Antigen-6 bacteria more decisively and then decays passively.

### Strategic / gameplay implications

- **No damage window at infection start**: the 1-unit threshold means reaction 98 fires from the very first antigen unit injected, so there is no unopposed-injury phase like Antigen 4's ~3-second delay. This, combined with Antigen 6's low summed injury gain of 247, makes Antigen-6 infections the **least-damaging bacterial infections** in the antigen block per unit of active infection time.
- **3× amplification means rapid dormancy once engaged**: reaction 98 builds antibody 3× faster than the antigen arrives, so Antigen-6 bacteria tend to reach dormancy faster than Antigen-4 bacteria (1.5×) in per-tick terms, further limiting the duration of injury exposure.
- **Medium cadence smooths out the response**: with a 116-tick reaction half-life rather than the 52-64-tick Short cadence used by most antibodies, Antibody 6 builds in somewhat larger per-firing increments at slightly longer intervals. In aggregate per second the build-up rate is similar to or faster than the Short-cadence 1× reactions, but the pattern is slightly "lumpier".
- **Shared Hotness symptom with Antigen 4 but milder overall**: Antigen-6 infections produce the same cold-seeking and accelerated-metabolism symptoms as Antigen-4 infections, but with less direct organ damage. The net player experience is "my norn is overheating a bit but not suffering much" — the mildest of the Hotness infections.
- **Low starter dose makes first-contact immunity weak**: unlike Antibody 0 or 5 (starter doses 96 and 56), a newborn has effectively no pre-armed defence against Antigen 6. However, reaction 98's fast engagement and 3× amplification compensate, so acquired immunity builds quickly.
- **Community "pre-immunisation" agents are particularly attractive**: the combination of low starter dose, no receptors, and a symptom by-product that drives behavioural disruption makes Antibody 6 a good candidate for prophylactic injection. A community agent that injects 100 units of Antibody 6 gives the creature immediate, receptor-free protection that decays over ~10 minutes without side effects.

### Diagnostic visibility

Antibody 6 is not named in the Medical Pod's toxin panel (it is not a toxin), but it is visible via:

- **Chemistry panel (slot 108)**: direct numeric readout, showing the current antibody level. Values significantly above the 23-unit baseline indicate an active or recent Antigen-6 infection, with the magnitude roughly 3× cumulative antigen exposure (modulo decay timing). Because the baseline is so low, even modest accumulations are clearly visible.
- **Debug console**: `CHEM TARG 108` returns the current concentration on demand.
- **Bacterium behaviour inference**: if an Antigen-6 bacterium on a creature suddenly stops producing Hotness (visible as Hotness's chemistry-panel reading plateauing and starting to decay), the player can infer that Antibody 6 has crossed the bacterium's dormancy threshold.
- **Behavioural symptoms via Hotness**: unlike Antibody 5, Antibody 6 production is symptomatically *visible* through its Hotness by-product — cold-seeking behaviour, elevated Hotness drive, and accelerated organ clock rate. However, these symptoms are shared with Antigen 4, so they cannot be used to distinguish Antigen 6 from Antigen 4 without chemistry-panel inspection.

The clinical signature of a creature mid-infection is: immediately-climbing Antibody 6, slowly-rising Antigen 6, steadily-rising Hotness, cold-seeking behaviour, accelerated metabolism, mild visible organ damage (less than any other infection). The signature of a creature post-infection is: high Antibody 6 (decaying slowly), near-baseline Antigen 6, decayed Hotness, normal metabolism, and residual acquired immunity against reinfection for ~10 minutes.

### The antibody block as a whole

Antibody 6 sits in the middle of the antibody block's "Medium-cadence triplet" (chemicals 107, 108, 109 for Antibodies 5, 6, 7), which shares the same passive decay (2,481 ticks, Long) and the same "no receptors" design across all eight slots. ## Summary

Antibody 6 is chemical 108 and the dedicated immune response product for [Antigen 6 (088)](088%20-%20Antigen%206.md), the **milder of the two Hotness-producing antigens**. It is produced by **reaction 98** (gene 91, `1× Antigen 6 → 3× Antibody 6 + 1× Hotness`, Medium half-life of 116 ticks) — one of the block's three 1:3 amplifying antibody reactions, with a **1-unit reactant threshold** that engages immune production from the very first unit of antigen injected (no delay phase). Every creature starts life with a **23-unit baseline concentration** set by gene 22 — **the lowest starter dose in the antibody block** — and the chemical clears only via a **Long passive decay** of 2,481 ticks (~1 min 23 s per halving at 30 tps). It has **no receptors** on the creature — no locus reads its concentration, it affects no drive or reflex or metabolic process. Its entire game-mechanical effect is to be read by the external `bacteria.cos` agent: when the host's Antibody 6 rises above an Antigen-6-carrying bacterium's randomly-rolled dormancy threshold, the bacterium goes dormant and stops injecting antigen and toxin; and each tick of elevated antibody drains the bacterium's remaining lifespan by `antibody × 10`, so sustained high antibody eventually kills the bacterium outright. Because reaction 98 amplifies 3× and fires immediately on contact, Antigen-6 infections produce a characteristic **no-delay, steep-ramp** Antibody 6 profile from tick one of the infection.
