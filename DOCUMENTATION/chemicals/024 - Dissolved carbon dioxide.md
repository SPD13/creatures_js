# 024 - Dissolved carbon dioxide

Dissolved carbon dioxide is the creature's **respiratory waste chemical**. It is the by-product of aerobic respiration — every time a cell burns Pyruvate with Oxygen to release Energy, CO₂ is generated. Unlike signalling chemicals such as Pain or Adrenalin, Dissolved carbon dioxide has no receptors of its own: nothing in the standard genome reads its concentration to produce a behavioural, drive, or injury response. Instead, it exists as a *metabolic intermediate* that ties together two of the creature's most important biochemical loops — the **respiration / energy pipeline** and the **nitrogen (urea) excretion pipeline**.

Because CO₂ has an effectively infinite natural half-life (~9 × 10¹⁰ ticks, decay rate 1), any CO₂ the body produces stays in the bloodstream until it is actively consumed by one of two reactions: a slow reaction that turns it back into Water, or the urea-synthesis reaction that combines it with Ammonia. This gives CO₂ a dual role: it is both a disposable respiration waste product *and* a required reactant for nitrogen excretion, so the creature's protein-metabolism loop depends on a constant supply of CO₂ being generated by normal energy metabolism.

## Sources

|---|-----------|------|----------------|-------------------|------|
| 1 | Chemical reaction (id 19) — aerobic respiration | Gene 39, Baby onwards | Standard (genome-wide) | `1× Pyruvate [2] + 3× Oxygen [30] → 6× Energy [34] + 3× Dissolved carbon dioxide [24]` | Very short half-life (~5 ticks, decay 0.879) — fires almost as soon as both reactants are present, making CO₂ production tightly coupled to energy production |

Dissolved carbon dioxide has no dedicated emitter in the standard Norn/Grendel/Ettin genome. Every molecule of CO₂ in a healthy creature's body comes from reaction 19 (cellular respiration). External sources are possible (injected via CAOS `CHEM` / `INJR`, or placed in a creature by food/drug agents) but are not part of the normal metabolic design.

Dissolved carbon dioxide has **no receptors** in the standard genome. It is a pure metabolic intermediate: nothing reads its concentration to drive a locus, alter a drive, or raise an injury signal. Its only effects are indirect, via the downstream products of the reactions that consume it.

## Role in Game Mechanics

### The respiration pipeline

Dissolved carbon dioxide is the waste terminal of the creature's aerobic energy pathway:

```
 (food → digestion → Glucose/Starch → Pyruvate)
                                         │
                                         ▼
                             Pyruvate [2]  +  Oxygen [30]
                                         │  reaction 19 (very short half-life)
                                         ▼
                         Energy [34]  +  Dissolved CO₂ [24]
```

Every tick in which both Pyruvate and Oxygen are present, reaction 19 fires very aggressively (half-life ~5 ticks) and produces CO₂ in a 3:1 ratio relative to the Pyruvate consumed. This means the creature is effectively *always* generating CO₂ as long as it is alive and breathing — the amount scales directly with how active its metabolism is. A resting, well-fed creature produces a modest baseline of CO₂; a creature burning a lot of Pyruvate (e.g. after eating, fighting, or being dosed with activity-boosting chemicals) produces a burst of it.

Because CO₂ itself is inert to the nervous system, this does not cause any drive or brain effect — its significance comes from what happens to it next.

### The two disposal pathways

CO₂'s "infinite" half-life means the body never simply lets it decay; it must be routed through a reaction. Two reactions consume it, and between them they create the CO₂ steady-state in a healthy creature:

**1. Passive clearance (reaction 25, `3 CO₂ → 1 Water`).** This is the creature's default "exhalation" mechanism. It runs continuously whenever any CO₂ is present and simply destroys CO₂ three units at a time while topping up Water. With a short half-life (~19 ticks) it provides a reliable background drain that keeps CO₂ from building up indefinitely, even if no Ammonia is available for reaction 30.

**2. Urea synthesis (reaction 30, `2 Ammonia + 1 CO₂ → 1 Urea + 1 Water`).** This reaction is the creature's **nitrogen-disposal loop**, biologically modelled on the real urea cycle. Protein metabolism (reaction 21: `2 Amino Acid → 1 Glucose + 1 Ammonia`) produces Ammonia as a toxic by-product; reaction 30 combines that Ammonia with CO₂ to form Urea, which is then expelled / stored as a safe waste. **CO₂ is therefore a required co-substrate for the creature to detoxify Ammonia** — without a supply of CO₂, reaction 30 stalls and Ammonia accumulates unchecked, poisoning the creature via its own receptors on Ammonia.

This second pathway is the reason CO₂ is not a "pure" waste: the respiration pipeline is effectively *feeding* the excretion pipeline. A creature that stops metabolising Pyruvate (starvation, sleeping with low food reserves, etc.) stops producing CO₂, which in turn throttles its ability to process any new Ammonia through urea synthesis, leaving only the much slower / less direct pathways to handle it.

### Steady-state behaviour

Because generation (reaction 19) is very fast and consumption (reactions 25 and 30) is short but not as fast, CO₂ typically reaches a **low, positive equilibrium** rather than spiking or vanishing:

- While the creature is actively metabolising, reaction 19 tops up CO₂ every few ticks.
- Reactions 25 and 30 continually drain it, with 30 prioritised whenever Ammonia is around (its presence consumes CO₂ alongside Ammonia at comparable speed).
- Reaction 25 acts as the floor: even with no Ammonia to react with, CO₂ will slowly be converted to Water and cleared out.

The net effect is that CO₂ is a short-lived *transient* pool in gameplay terms, despite its near-infinite raw half-life. Players almost never see a creature "die of CO₂ poisoning" because there is no CO₂ receptor to cause such a thing — problems only arise if a scripter or genetic engineer adds one.

### Interaction with the water / coolant loop

Both CO₂-consuming reactions produce Water as a by-product. Water in the standard genome is not biochemically inert — it participates in cooling reactions (26 and 31: `Water + Hotness → nothing`) and other chemistry. So the respiration pipeline quietly provides a slow, constant trickle of Water into the creature's body even if it never drinks, which among other things contributes to the creature's ability to handle Hotness build-up. CO₂ is the intermediary that links these: respiration → CO₂ → Water → heat regulation.

### Balancing and scripter notes

Because Dissolved CO₂ has no receptor and no player-facing effect, it is rarely touched directly by scripted agents. Typical interactions are:

- **Genetic engineers / modders** may add a CO₂ receptor (for instance, to make "suffocation" meaningful, or to pressure creatures into ventilated areas) or tune reaction 25's rate to simulate holding one's breath.
- **Food / drug agents** that inject Oxygen or Pyruvate will indirectly raise CO₂; agents injecting CO₂ directly (via `CHEM`) are mostly used for debugging or to stress-test the urea pathway.
- **Reaction speed mutations** on genes 50 (reaction 25) or 46 (reaction 30) have outsized effects: slowing either one backs CO₂ up and, if reaction 30 is the one affected, causes Ammonia to accumulate because its disposal requires CO₂ to be consumed at a reasonable rate.

### Summary of the CO₂ loop

```
   Pyruvate [2] + Oxygen [30]
            │  reaction 19 (very short half-life, always firing when fed)
            ▼
   Energy [34]  +  Dissolved CO₂ [24]
                           │
            ┌──────────────┴──────────────┐
            │                             │
  reaction 25 (short)            reaction 30 (short, needs Ammonia)
  3 CO₂ → 1 Water                2 Ammonia + 1 CO₂ → 1 Urea + 1 Water
            │                             │
            ▼                             ▼
       Water [33]                   Urea [25] + Water [33]
                                   (nitrogen excretion)
```

Dissolved carbon dioxide therefore sits at the crossroads of two of the creature's core metabolic cycles: it is the waste output of burning food for energy, and the required co-substrate that makes nitrogen excretion possible. It has no direct behavioural effect, but shutting off either of its disposal reactions has serious downstream consequences for water balance and ammonia toxicity.
