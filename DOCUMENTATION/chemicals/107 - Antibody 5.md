# 107 - Antibody 5

Antibody 5 is chemical slot 107 in the Creatures 3 chemistry and the sixth entry in the canonical **antibody block** (chemicals 102-109, Antibody 0 through Antibody 7). Each antibody is paired one-to-one with an antigen in the 82-89 block — Antibody 5 is specifically the immune response to [Antigen 5 (087)](087%20-%20Antigen%205.md), the block's "mystery" antigen whose infection does not produce one of the common thermoregulatory or allergic symptom chemicals (Histamine, Coldness, Hotness, Pain) but instead releases **Chemical 90**, an unnamed signalling chemical whose sole known effect is to kill Immune tissue at high concentrations. Like all antibodies, Antibody 5 is **cumulative immune memory**: a rising chemical-107 level records that the host has been fighting an Antigen-5 infection, and it is the chemical signal that the attacking bacterium agent reads to decide whether to keep injecting antigen and toxin or to go dormant.

Antibody 5 is produced endogenously by **reaction 97** (gene 90), the Antigen-5 immune response — a **3× amplifying** reaction:

```
1× Antigen 5 [87] → 3× Antibody 5 [107] + 1× Chemical 90 [90]
```

Reaction 97 is one of the antibody block's three **1:3 amplifiers** (reactions 97, 98, 99 for Antibodies 5, 6, 7), sitting structurally between the block's 6× block-leaders (reactions 92/93 for Antibodies 0/1, 2:12 stoichiometry) and the weaker 2:3 amplifier of Antibody 4 (reaction 95, 1.5×). The reaction uses a **1-unit reactant threshold** — the lowest possible firing threshold — so a trace of Antigen 5 is enough to engage the immune response, and reaction 97 fires from the very first tick of any bacterial contact. It runs at a **Medium half-life (116 ticks, decay rate 0.994, rate genome value 48)** — slower than the Short antibody reactions (reactions 92-96 at 52 or 64 ticks) but faster than the Long decay of antibody passive clearance. The Medium reaction cadence is distinctive: among the antibody block, reactions 97 and 99 both use Medium cadence while the rest are Short, giving Antibody 5 a slightly more "measured" build-up than Antibodies 0-3. Every firing also **emits one unit of Chemical 90 (90)** as a by-product, tying Antibody 5 production directly to the block's mystery signalling pathway. Every Creatures 3 norn, grendel or ettin starts life with a **baseline initial concentration of 56 units** of Antibody 5 (gene 21, concentration 0.2196), modestly above the antibody-block median — only Antibody 0's 96-unit starter dose is higher among the eight antibodies.

The chemical has **no receptors anywhere on the creature** for its own effect: no organ locus reads chemical 107, no drive is modulated by its concentration, no metabolic reaction consumes it, no injury wire is triggered. (Receptor 107 in the receptor table uses `"chemical": 125` (Life) and only happens to have the id-field 107 — that is the LOC_AGE6 "die of old age" receptor, completely unrelated to Antibody 5.) This is the standard "pure signalling" design for the entire antibody block — the antibody does nothing to the creature itself. Its entire game-mechanical footprint is **external**: the bacterium agent (`bacteria.cos`, family/genus/species `2 32 23`) reads the host's chemical slot 107 directly via the CAOS `chem` primitive whenever its rolled `ov15` is 87, using the value to decide whether it is being successfully suppressed by the immune response. When antibody rises above the bacterium's dormancy threshold the bacterium goes dormant and stops injecting Antigen 5 and its paired toxin; when antibody later decays below its wake threshold, the bacterium may resume active infection. Antibody 5 is therefore the **decisive lever** that determines whether an Antigen-5 bacterial infection is winning or losing.

Antibody 5 clears from the bloodstream exclusively through passive decay, at a **Long half-life of 2,481 ticks** (decay rate 0.99972, ~1 minute 23 seconds of real play per halving at 30 tps) — the decay profile shared by every antibody in the block. There is no reaction in the standard genome that consumes Antibody 5 and no antidote that cancels it. Together with reaction 97's 3× amplification and 1-unit threshold, this produces the characteristic Antigen-5 infection arc: from the very first tick of bacterial contact, antibody rises at three times the antigen flux, each antibody unit produced paired with 0.33 units of Chemical 90 bleeding into the bloodstream. Once Antibody 5 rises far enough to exceed the bacterium's dormancy threshold, the bacterium goes dormant and the elevated antibody decays slowly over ~minutes, conferring durable short-term immunity against reinfection.

## Role in Game Mechanics

### The 1:3 amplifying reaction: why Antibody 5 scales faster than Antibody 4

Reaction 97 is the antibody block's **lowest-threshold amplifier**, and together with reactions 98 and 99 (for Antibodies 6 and 7) forms the block's "Medium-cadence triplet" of 1:3 amplifiers. Unlike reactions 92-96, reaction 97 requires only a single unit of Antigen 5 to fire — so the moment any Antigen-5-carrying bacterium injects its first 0.02 units of antigen, the reaction starts producing antibody on its next cadence tick.

| Property | Reaction 92 (Antibody 0) | Reaction 93 (Antibody 1) | Reaction 94 (Antibody 2) | Reaction 96 (Antibody 3) | Reaction 95 (Antibody 4) | **Reaction 97 (Antibody 5)** | Reaction 98 (Antibody 6) | Reaction 99 (Antibody 7) |
|----------|--------------------------|--------------------------|--------------------------|--------------------------|--------------------------|------------------------------|--------------------------|--------------------------|
| Reactant threshold | 2× antigen | 2× antigen | 16× antigen | 1× antigen | 2× antigen | **1× antigen** | 1× antigen | 1× antigen |
| Antibody output | 12 | 12 | 12 | 1 | 3 | **3** | 3 | 3 |
| Amplification ratio | 6× | 6× | 0.75× | 1× (symmetric) | 1.5× | **3×** | 3× | 3× |
| Symptom by-product | 1× Histamine B | 1× Histamine A | 2× Coldness | 2× Coldness | 1× Hotness | **1× Chemical 90** | 1× Hotness | 1× Pain |
| Reaction half-life | Short (52) | Short (52) | Short (64) | Short (64) | Short (64) | **Medium (116)** | Medium | Medium |

The practical consequence for Antigen-5 infections is that the immune response is **immediate-start, amplified, and paced**. Where Antibody 4's reaction needs ~3 seconds to build up its 2-unit reactant pool before firing at all, reaction 97 fires at the first cadence tick after bacterial contact. The Medium 116-tick cadence is slower than the Short reactions — at 30 tps the reaction fires roughly every 3.9 seconds rather than every 1.7-2.1 seconds — but the 1-unit threshold and 3× amplification together mean that antibody begins accumulating from tick one of the infection and does so at three times the antigen input rate.

The amplification matters in two ways:
1. **No damage window**: unlike Antigen 4's 3-second unopposed injury phase, Antigen 5's five somatic injury receptors are met by immediate antibody production from the very first unit of antigen injected.
2. **Stronger long-term immunity**: the cumulative Antibody 5 pool after a recovered infection is 3× the antigen input (vs 1.5× for Antibody 4), so residual acquired immunity is proportionally more durable against reinfection.

### Chemical 90: the mystery by-product

Every time reaction 97 fires, it dumps **one unit of Chemical 90** into the bloodstream. Chemical 90 is one of the rare "unnamed" chemicals in the Creatures 3 chemistry — its slot in the chemical dictionary has no meaningful name string, just the numeric label "90". Its properties in the stock genome are:

- **Half-life**: Medium (462 ticks, decay rate 0.998, genome value 62). Decays moderately quickly — faster than the Long antibody passive decay but slower than the Short drive decays.
- **One receptor in the genome** (receptor 110, gene 158): LOC_DIE on **Immune tissue**, threshold 232, gain 255, DIGITAL flag. That is: when Chemical 90 on Immune tissue exceeds 232 units, the immune tissue undergoes `LOC_DIE` — the "kill this tissue" trigger. This is the mechanism by which sustained heavy Chemical 90 accumulation can damage the creature's immune system.
- **No other receptors, no reactions that consume it**.

The threshold of 232 is high (close to the 0-255 maximum), so Chemical 90 rarely accumulates to dangerous levels during a single infection — reaction 97 would have to fire continuously for many minutes against a bacterium whose dormancy threshold never gets reached. The Medium 462-tick decay provides additional buffering against accumulation. In practice, the LOC_DIE mechanism is a *rare edge case*: only an Antigen-5 infection that never resolves (e.g., because the bacterium has an exceptionally high randomly-rolled dormancy threshold that Antibody 5 never crosses) can push Chemical 90 to its kill threshold. The more common experience is that Chemical 90 sits well below 232, rising during infection and decaying afterwards without ever triggering its receptor.

Compared to other antibody by-products, Chemical 90 is **unique**:
- Antibodies 0/1 produce Histamine A/B (allergic symptoms, affect drives).
- Antibodies 2/3 produce Coldness (thermoregulatory drive, organ clock rate, involuntary sleep).
- Antibody 4 produces Hotness (thermoregulatory drive, organ clock rate, no sleep).
- Antibody 6 produces Hotness (shared with Antibody 4).
- Antibody 7 produces Pain (visceral pain drive).
- **Antibody 5 produces Chemical 90 (mystery "immune self-damage" signal with no drive effect).**

This makes Antigen-5 infections *symptomatically silent* from the player's perspective: there is no visible Hotness/Coldness/Pain/Histamine drive pinned high, no hot-seeking or cold-seeking or sleeping behaviour, no scratching or vomiting. The only signals that an Antigen-5 infection is active are the direct symptoms of Antigen 5 itself (the five injury receptors firing) and the chemistry-panel rise of Chemical 90. Under sustained heavy infection, the eventual LOC_DIE firing on Immune tissue would manifest as a dying immune system — but this is a rare outcome.

### The 56-unit starter dose: second-highest pre-immunity in the block

Gene 21 sets the initial concentration of chemical 107 to 56 units (0.2196 of the 0-255 scale). This is the **second-highest starter dose** in the antibody block, beaten only by Antibody 0's 96 units. The design logic is a deliberate compensation for reaction 97's Medium cadence: because the immune response builds antibody more slowly per tick than the Short-cadence reactions, the newborn needs a larger pre-armed baseline to bridge the gap before organic antibody production catches up.

- **Antibody 0 starter: 96** (Short cadence, 2-unit threshold, 6× amplification) — highest, because Antigen 0's Histamine B is an aggressive symptom.
- **Antibody 5 starter: 56** (Medium cadence, 1-unit threshold, 3× amplification) — second-highest, compensating for the Medium cadence.
- **Antibody 1 starter: 52**, Antibody 3 starter: 50, Antibody 4 starter: 48, Antibody 2/7 starter: 46, Antibody 6 starter: 23 (lowest).

The 56-unit starter dose is enough to trigger dormancy in any Antigen-5 bacterium whose randomly-rolled dormancy threshold landed on the low end of the distribution, meaning newborn creatures have meaningful odds of silencing a mild Antigen-5 bacterium immediately on contact — without ever firing reaction 97 and therefore without ever dumping Chemical 90 into the bloodstream or taking injury from the five somatic injury receptors.

Because passive decay runs constantly from birth (Long 2,481 ticks), the starter 56 units halves roughly every 82 seconds of play. After about 5 minutes of newborn life (no Antigen 5 exposure), Antibody 5 has decayed to ~12 units — no longer meaningfully above zero. The elevated starter dose therefore matters specifically for the **first several minutes** of a creature's life: it protects against early bacterial exposures during the vulnerable newborn phase.

### Long decay and the memory of infection

The 2,481-tick passive half-life (~1 min 23 s at 30 tps) is the standard antibody decay profile shared by every member of the block. For Antigen 5 specifically, the interplay between reaction 97's 1-unit threshold, its 3× amplification, and the Long passive decay produces a **no-delay, steep-ramp, slow-tail** curve in Antibody 5 during and after an infection:

- **Phase 1 (0-4 s)**: antigen accumulates from 0 toward 1 unit; the reaction fires on the next Medium-cadence tick as soon as that 1-unit threshold is met; antibody begins accumulating at 3× the antigen input rate; five somatic injury receptors fire on Antigen 5 but are immediately being countered by rising antibody.
- **Phase 2 (sustained infection)**: reaction 97 fires every ~3.9 seconds; antibody rises steadily at 3× antigen injection rate minus decay; Chemical 90 accumulates at 1× antigen injection rate and decays at Medium cadence (well below the 232 LOC_DIE threshold under normal infection loads); injury receptors continue to fire.
- **Phase 3 (dormancy)**: antibody crosses the bacterium's randomly-rolled threshold; bacterium goes dormant; antigen injection stops; reaction 97 drains remaining antigen and stops firing; Chemical 90 production stops and Chemical 90 decays at Medium cadence; antibody plateaus.
- **Phase 4 (recovery)**: antibody decays exponentially at the Long 2,481-tick half-life; each subsequent minute of real play halves the residual protection; after ~10 minutes antibody is back near starter baseline.

Plotting Antibody 5 over time during an infection shows a characteristic "steep-from-start S-curve" — no flat head, an immediate steep climb, plateau, and exponential tail. This is visually and mechanically distinct from Antibody 4's delayed ramp.

### No creature-side receptors: why the creature doesn't "feel" its own antibodies

Antibody 5 has zero receptors on any creature locus — the creature cannot sense its own antibody level, and no drive, reflex or metabolic process depends on it. This is the standard antibody-block design and is intentional for two reasons:

1. **Separation of concerns**: the chemistry handles the biochemical bookkeeping (antibody accumulation and decay), and the bacterium agent handles the game-state effect (dormancy and lifespan drain). Adding a creature-side receptor would duplicate functionality and risk interference between the two systems.
2. **Prevents overdose side effects**: because there is no receptor, a player can safely dose a creature with arbitrary amounts of Antibody 5 (via a community "immune booster" agent or via CAOS injection) without side effects. The excess simply suppresses Antigen-5 bacteria more decisively and then decays passively.

The contrast with Chemical 90 (which has the Immune-tissue LOC_DIE receptor) is instructive: Chemical 90 is a *symptomatic* by-product that can eventually damage immune tissue under sustained heavy exposure, so it has a receptor; Antibody 5 is an *effective* chemical that needs to drive the bacterium's state machine, so it bypasses the creature-side receptor system entirely and talks directly to the external agent.

### Strategic / gameplay implications

- **No damage window at infection start**: the 1-unit threshold means reaction 97 fires from the very first antigen unit injected, so there is no unopposed-injury phase like Antigen 4's. This partially offsets Antigen 5's five somatic injury receptors (gains 64, 65, 64, 64, 55 — summed gain 312).
- **3× amplification means rapid dormancy once engaged**: reaction 97 builds antibody 3× faster than the antigen arrives, so Antigen-5 bacteria tend to reach dormancy earlier in their infection timeline than Antigen-4 bacteria (1.5×) or Antigen-3 bacteria (1×) — limiting the duration of injury exposure.
- **Medium cadence smooths out the response**: with a 116-tick reaction half-life rather than the 52-64-tick Short cadence used by most antibodies, Antibody 5 builds in somewhat larger per-firing increments at slightly longer intervals. In aggregate per second the build-up rate is similar to or faster than the Short-cadence 1× reactions, but the pattern is slightly "lumpier".
- **Symptomatic silence is a two-edged sword**: Chemical 90 does not drive any visible creature behaviour, so a player may miss an ongoing Antigen-5 infection unless they check the chemistry panel. On the upside, infected creatures are not miserable in the way Antigen-3 or Antigen-6 infections make them (no hot-seeking, no cold-seeking, no pain, no sleep); on the downside, diagnosing an Antigen-5 infection requires explicit inspection rather than behavioural cues.
- **Immune-tissue LOC_DIE risk is real but rare**: sustained heavy Antigen-5 exposure (a bacterium whose dormancy threshold Antibody 5 cannot reach) will eventually accumulate Chemical 90 above 232 and trigger Immune tissue death. This is a long-term risk — Chemical 90's Medium decay provides strong buffering — but it is the one way in which an Antigen-5 infection can cause lasting damage beyond the direct injury receptors on Antigen 5 itself.
- **Community vaccine agents bypass both Chemical 90 and injury-receptor damage**: because every unit of organic Antibody 5 produced comes with 0.33 units of Chemical 90 and requires antigen passes that trip five injury receptors, pre-immunising a creature by injecting Antibody 5 directly is materially kinder to the creature's organ health than letting a real infection run. The low 23-232 risk of LOC_DIE-on-Immune makes this prophylactic strategy particularly valuable against Antigen 5.

### Diagnostic visibility

Antibody 5 is not named in the Medical Pod's toxin panel (it is not a toxin), but it is visible via:

- **Chemistry panel (slot 107)**: direct numeric readout, showing the current antibody level. Values significantly above the 56-unit baseline indicate an active or recent Antigen-5 infection, with the magnitude roughly 3× cumulative antigen exposure (modulo decay timing).
- **Debug console**: `CHEM TARG 107` returns the current concentration on demand.
- **Bacterium behaviour inference**: if an Antigen-5 bacterium on a creature suddenly stops producing Chemical 90 (visible as Chemical 90's chemistry-panel reading plateauing and starting to decay), the player can infer that Antibody 5 has crossed the bacterium's dormancy threshold.
- **Chemical 90 chemistry panel (slot 90)**: indirect inference of Antibody 5 production activity, since every antibody-production firing also releases Chemical 90.

The clinical signature of a creature mid-infection is: immediately-climbing Antibody 5, slowly-rising Antigen 5, steadily-rising Chemical 90 (well below the 232 kill threshold under normal loads), visible organ damage accumulating, but **no visible behavioural symptoms** — no drive pinned high, no thermoregulatory seeking, no pain. The signature of a creature post-infection is: high Antibody 5 (decaying slowly), near-baseline Antigen 5, decayed Chemical 90, normal metabolism — and the residual high Antibody 5 is what gives the creature its short-term acquired immunity against reinfection.

### The antibody block as a whole

Antibody 5 sits at the start of the antibody block's "Medium-cadence triplet" (chemicals 107, 108, 109 for Antibodies 5, 6, 7), which shares the same passive decay (2,481 ticks, Long) and the same "no receptors" design across all eight slots. The block's structure mirrors the antigen block 82-89 one-to-one:

Antibody 5 is the **first of the 1:3 amplifying antibody responses** in the stock genome and the only antibody whose by-product is the unnamed Chemical 90 rather than a drive-carrying symptom chemical. It is distinguished from its triplet-siblings (Antibodies 6 and 7) by the character of its by-product: where Antibody 6 produces thermoregulatory Hotness and Antibody 7 produces visceral Pain, Antibody 5 produces a symptomatically silent chemical whose only long-term effect is a rare LOC_DIE trigger on Immune tissue.

## Summary

Antibody 5 is chemical 107 and the dedicated immune response product for [Antigen 5 (087)](087%20-%20Antigen%205.md), the antibody block's "mystery" antigen whose infection does not manifest as one of the common drive-carrying symptoms (Histamine, Coldness, Hotness, Pain) but instead produces the unnamed **Chemical 90**. It is produced by **reaction 97** (gene 90, `1× Antigen 5 → 3× Antibody 5 + 1× Chemical 90`, Medium half-life of 116 ticks) — one of the block's three 1:3 amplifying antibody reactions, with a **1-unit reactant threshold** that engages immune production from the very first unit of antigen injected (no delay phase). Every creature starts life with a **56-unit baseline concentration** set by gene 21 (the second-highest starter dose in the block), and the chemical clears only via a **Long passive decay** of 2,481 ticks (~1 min 23 s per halving at 30 tps). It has **no receptors** on the creature — no locus reads its concentration, it affects no drive or reflex or metabolic process. Its entire game-mechanical effect is to be read by the external `bacteria.cos` agent: when the host's Antibody 5 rises above an Antigen-5-carrying bacterium's randomly-rolled dormancy threshold, the bacterium goes dormant and stops injecting antigen and toxin; and each tick of elevated antibody drains the bacterium's remaining lifespan by `antibody × 10`, so sustained high antibody eventually kills the bacterium outright. Because reaction 97 amplifies 3× and fires immediately on contact, Antigen-5 infections produce a characteristic **no-delay, steep-ramp** Antibody 5 profile from tick one of the infection. The Chemical 90 by-product is symptomatically silent — creating no visible behavioural symptoms — but has a rare LOC_DIE receptor on Immune tissue at threshold 232, so sustained unresolved infection can eventually damage the creature's immune system. The Long passive decay preserves accumulated immunity for minutes after an infection clears, giving meaningful short-term protection against reinfection while fading to baseline over ~hours. With no creature-side receptors, no antidote reactions and no direct physiological effect, Antibody 5 exemplifies Creatures 3's modular chemistry design: a pure "signalling" chemical whose meaning lives entirely in the communication between the biology genome (reaction 97, gene 21) and the external agent ecosystem (`bacteria.cos` reading `chem 107`). It is the **symptomatically silent** antibody of the block — an infection neither hot nor cold nor itchy nor painful, detectable only by chemistry-panel inspection and by the accumulating damage from Antigen 5's five somatic injury receptors.
