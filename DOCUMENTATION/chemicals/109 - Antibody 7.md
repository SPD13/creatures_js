# 109 - Antibody 7

Antibody 7 is chemical slot 109 in the Creatures 3 chemistry and the **eighth and final entry in the canonical antibody block** (chemicals 102-109, Antibody 0 through Antibody 7). Each antibody is paired one-to-one with an antigen in the 82-89 block — Antibody 7 is specifically the immune response to [Antigen 7 (089)](089%20-%20Antigen%207.md), the **distress antigen** whose infection manifests as the most behaviourally-disruptive symptom profile in the entire antigen block: a limping gait, involuntary egg-laying, accelerated metabolism, elevated Pain drive and (in youth onwards) a circulatory stress reflex. Like all antibodies, Antibody 7 is **cumulative immune memory**: a rising chemical-109 level records that the host has been fighting an Antigen-7 infection, and it is the chemical signal that the attacking bacterium agent reads to decide whether to keep injecting antigen and toxin or to go dormant.

Antibody 7 is produced endogenously by **reaction 99** (gene 92), the Antigen-7 immune response — a **3× amplifying** reaction:

```
1× Antigen 7 [89] → 3× Antibody 7 [109] + 1× Pain [148]
```

Reaction 99 is the third member of the antibody block's **"Medium-cadence triplet"** of 1:3 amplifiers (reactions 97, 98, 99 for Antibodies 5, 6, 7) and structurally mirrors its triplet-siblings: a **1-unit reactant threshold** (the lowest possible firing threshold, so a trace of Antigen 7 is enough to engage the immune response from the very first tick of bacterial contact), a 3× amplification, and a Medium-speed reaction cadence. Where reaction 99 differs from its siblings is in its half-life: **105 ticks** (decay rate 0.9934, genome rate value 47) — **the fastest Medium-speed reaction in the entire antigen/antibody matrix**, ~10% quicker than reactions 97 and 98 (both 116 ticks). That extra speed means reaction 99 fires marginally more often than the other Medium-cadence antibody reactions, so per unit of time Antibody 7 accumulates slightly faster than Antibody 5 or Antibody 6. Every firing also **emits one unit of Pain (148)** as a by-product — the symptom chemical whose broad receptor network makes Antigen-7 infections symptomatically the most disruptive of all bacterial infections, even though Antigen 7's direct tissue-damage load (four injury receptors, summed gain 252) sits near the bottom of the antigen block. Every Creatures 3 norn, grendel or ettin starts life with a **baseline initial concentration of 46 units** of Antibody 7 (gene 23, concentration 0.1804) — a mid-range starter dose in the antibody block, roughly double Antibody 6's 23 and matching the starter doses of Antibodies 2 and 4.

The chemical has **no receptors anywhere on the creature** for its own effect: no organ locus reads chemical 109, no drive is modulated by its concentration, no metabolic reaction consumes it, no injury wire is triggered. This is the standard "pure signalling" design for the entire antibody block — the antibody does nothing to the creature itself. Its entire game-mechanical footprint is **external**: the bacterium agent (`bacteria.cos`, family/genus/species `2 32 23`) reads the host's chemical slot 109 directly via the CAOS `chem` primitive whenever its rolled `ov15` is 89, using the value to decide whether it is being successfully suppressed by the immune response. When antibody rises above the bacterium's dormancy threshold the bacterium goes dormant and stops injecting Antigen 7 and its paired toxin; when antibody later decays below its wake threshold, the bacterium may resume active infection. Antibody 7 is therefore the **decisive lever** that determines whether an Antigen-7 bacterial infection — and its cascade of Pain-driven behavioural symptoms — is winning or losing.

Antibody 7 clears from the bloodstream exclusively through passive decay, at a **Long half-life of 2,481 ticks** (decay rate 0.99972, ~1 minute 23 seconds of real play per halving at 30 tps) — the decay profile shared by every antibody in the block. There is no reaction in the standard genome that consumes Antibody 7 and no antidote that cancels it. Together with reaction 99's 3× amplification and 1-unit threshold, this produces the characteristic Antigen-7 infection arc: from the very first tick of bacterial contact, antibody rises at three times the antigen flux, each antibody unit produced paired with 0.33 units of Pain bleeding into the bloodstream. Because Pain drives the broadest receptor network in the game, those 0.33 units per antibody unit are what the player *sees* — a distressed, limping, erratic creature — even though the antigen itself is doing relatively little direct organ damage. Once Antibody 7 rises far enough to exceed the bacterium's dormancy threshold, the bacterium goes dormant and the elevated antibody decays slowly over ~minutes, conferring durable short-term immunity against reinfection.

## Role in Game Mechanics

### The fastest 1:3 amplifier: reaction 99 at the block's quickest Medium cadence

Reaction 99 belongs to the antibody block's **"Medium-cadence triplet"** (reactions 97, 98, 99 for Antibodies 5, 6, 7) and shares with its triplet-siblings the combination of a **1-unit reactant threshold** and **3× amplification**. Where reaction 99 pulls ahead is its half-life: **105 ticks**, versus 116 ticks for reactions 97 and 98. In a 30 tps simulation, reaction 99 fires roughly every 3.5 seconds rather than every 3.9 seconds — a modest ~10% speed-up that makes Antibody 7 accumulate marginally faster per second than any other Medium-cadence antibody.

| Property | Reaction 92 (Antibody 0) | Reaction 93 (Antibody 1) | Reaction 94 (Antibody 2) | Reaction 96 (Antibody 3) | Reaction 95 (Antibody 4) | Reaction 97 (Antibody 5) | Reaction 98 (Antibody 6) | **Reaction 99 (Antibody 7)** |
|----------|--------------------------|--------------------------|--------------------------|--------------------------|--------------------------|--------------------------|--------------------------|------------------------------|
| Reactant threshold | 2× antigen | 2× antigen | 16× antigen | 1× antigen | 2× antigen | 1× antigen | 1× antigen | **1× antigen** |
| Antibody output | 12 | 12 | 12 | 1 | 3 | 3 | 3 | **3** |
| Amplification ratio | 6× | 6× | 0.75× | 1× (symmetric) | 1.5× | 3× | 3× | **3×** |
| Symptom by-product | 1× Histamine B | 1× Histamine A | 2× Coldness | 2× Coldness | 1× Hotness | 1× Chemical 90 | 1× Hotness | **1× Pain** |
| Reaction half-life | Short (52) | Short (52) | Short (64) | Short (64) | Short (64) | Medium (116) | Medium (116) | **Medium (105)** |

The practical consequence for Antigen-7 infections is that the immune response is **immediate-start, amplified, and paced slightly faster than its Medium-triplet siblings**. Where Antibody 4's reaction needs ~3 seconds to build up its 2-unit reactant pool before firing at all, reaction 99 fires at the first cadence tick after bacterial contact. The combination of 1-unit threshold, 3× amplification and the block's fastest Medium cadence makes reaction 99 the **single most efficient antigen-to-antibody conversion reaction in the entire standard genome** in per-second terms. In terms of antibody output per unit of antigen consumed, reactions 92 and 93 are still ahead (6× amplification), but per unit of time at sustained infection pressure, reaction 99 is the top performer.

### The Pain by-product: the broadest symptom footprint in the game

Every time reaction 99 fires, it dumps **one unit of Pain (chemical 148)** into the bloodstream. Pain has the **broadest receptor network of any symptom chemical in the standard genome** — five distinct receptors across five different body systems:

1. **Pain drive** (`Creature / Drives / Locus 0`, gain 207): Pain directly feeds the creature's Pain drive at a very high gain — a dominating motivation to escape whatever the creature blames for the hurt. The Pain drive is tied into the decision-making process and weighs heavily on action selection.
2. **LOC_INVOLUNTARY0 "Lay egg"** (`Creature / Sensorimotor / Locus 0`, threshold 48): at Pain ≥48, the creature involuntarily triggers the egg-laying reflex. During an Antigen-7 infection this produces unfertilised eggs being laid spontaneously by fertile females — a uniquely disturbing visible symptom. (The reflex has no effect on males or infertile individuals, but the script still fires.)
3. **RLOCUS_CLOCKRATE** (`Organ / Somatic / Locus 0`, threshold 30): at Pain ≥30, somatic organ clock rates are accelerated — metabolic speed-up, increased energy consumption, and faster chemistry turnover. The creature literally runs "hotter" (metabolically) under Pain.
4. **LOC_GAIT1** (`Creature / Sensorimotor / Locus 9`, threshold 33): at Pain ≥33, the creature switches to an alternate gait — typically the "limping" or "injured" walk animation. This is the most visually obvious Antigen-7 symptom: the player sees the creature moving differently.
5. **Circulatory stress receptor** (`Creature / Circulatory / Locus 12`, threshold 191, Youth+): at Pain ≥191 and age ≥3 (Youth), a circulatory locus activates — a late-game, high-threshold cardiac/circulatory stress reflex that fires only at extreme Pain levels in post-childhood creatures.

This receptor set is **qualitatively broader than any other symptom chemical's**. Hotness has three receptors (drive + two clock-rate thresholds); Coldness has four (including involuntary-sleep); Histamines A and B each drive only sneezing/coughing reflexes. Pain uniquely touches the drive system, the reflex system, the metabolic system, the gait/motor system, **and** the circulatory system all at once — a cross-cutting cascade that makes Antigen-7 infections unmistakeable and hard to ignore.

Because reaction 99 produces 1× Pain per 1× Antigen 7 consumed (ratio 1.0), and Antigen-7 bacteria inject antigen at 0.02/tick just like every other antigen, the Pain production rate during active infection is directly proportional to how many Antigen-7 bacteria are attached. A single bacterium produces modest Pain; a colony of multiple Antigen-7 bacteria can drive Pain past the 191-threshold circulatory receptor in a fully-grown creature.

### The 46-unit starter dose: mid-range pre-immunity

Gene 23 sets the initial concentration of chemical 109 to 46 units (0.1804 of the 0-255 scale). This is a **mid-range starter dose**, matching Antibodies 2 and 4 and sitting well above Antibody 6 (23) but below the block leaders (Antibody 0 at 96 and Antibody 5 at 56):

- **Antibody 0 starter: 96** (highest) — aggressive Histamine B response.
- **Antibody 5 starter: 56** — compensates for Medium cadence.
- **Antibody 1 starter: 52**, Antibody 3 starter: 50, Antibody 4 starter: 48, Antibody 2/**7 starter: 46**.
- **Antibody 6 starter: 23** (lowest).

The medium starter dose is consistent with Antigen 7's design profile:

1. **High behavioural cost if left unchecked**: Pain's broad receptor network means that even a small sustained Antigen-7 infection is extremely disruptive. A creature with *no* pre-immunity would spend its first bacterial encounter limping, laying, running faster and crying — a notably harsh introduction. Some starter loading is therefore warranted.
2. **Fast engagement compensates for modest starter**: reaction 99's 1-unit threshold and 3× amplification at the block's fastest Medium cadence mean that even a modest starter dose is enough to carry the creature through the ~few seconds it takes for acquired immunity to ramp up to dormancy-threshold levels against a typical bacterium.

At the Long 2,481-tick half-life, the starter 46 halves roughly every 82 seconds of play. After ~3 minutes of newborn life with no Antigen-7 exposure, Antibody 7 has decayed to roughly 12 units; after ~5 minutes, ~6 units. So the starter dose provides meaningful first-contact protection for the first minute or two of life, and then the creature effectively depends on acquired immunity.

### Long decay and the memory of infection

The 2,481-tick passive half-life (~1 min 23 s at 30 tps) is the standard antibody decay profile shared by every member of the block. For Antigen 7 specifically, the interplay between reaction 99's 1-unit threshold, its 3× amplification at the fastest Medium cadence, and the Long passive decay produces a **no-delay, steep-ramp, slow-tail** curve in Antibody 7 during and after an infection:

- **Phase 1 (0-2 s)**: antigen accumulates from 0 toward 1 unit; the reaction fires on the next Medium-cadence tick as soon as that 1-unit threshold is met (slightly sooner than for Antibodies 5 and 6 owing to the 105-tick cadence); antibody begins accumulating at 3× the antigen input rate; four somatic injury receptors fire on Antigen 7 at the block's second-lowest summed gain (252), and Pain begins trickling into the bloodstream at the reaction's firing rate.
- **Phase 2 (sustained infection)**: reaction 99 fires every ~3.5 seconds; antibody rises steadily at 3× antigen injection rate minus decay; Pain accumulates and crosses receptor thresholds in quick succession — the clock-rate receptor at Pain ≥30 accelerates metabolism, the limp-gait receptor at Pain ≥33 switches the motor model, the Pain drive gain of 207 feeds distress-motivated action selection, and at Pain ≥48 the involuntary egg-laying reflex begins firing. In youth+ creatures, sustained Pain may eventually cross the Locus 12 threshold at 191.
- **Phase 3 (dormancy)**: antibody crosses the bacterium's randomly-rolled threshold; bacterium goes dormant; antigen injection stops; reaction 99 drains remaining antigen and stops firing; Pain production stops and Pain decays at its own 172-tick (Short) half-life — so the Pain symptoms fade substantially faster than the antibody does; clock-rate, gait and involuntary-egg reflexes disengage in reverse threshold order as Pain falls; antibody plateaus.
- **Phase 4 (recovery)**: antibody decays exponentially at the Long 2,481-tick half-life; each subsequent minute of real play halves the residual protection; after ~10 minutes antibody is back near starter baseline.

The asymmetry between Pain's Short (172-tick) decay and Antibody 7's Long (2,481-tick) decay is important: after a bacterium goes dormant, the **behavioural symptoms clear rapidly** (within tens of seconds), while the **immune memory persists for minutes**. The player sees a clear recovery trajectory — the creature stops limping, starts acting normally, stops laying — while chemistry-panel-visible antibody remains elevated as a latent re-infection defence.

### No creature-side receptors: why the creature doesn't "feel" its own antibodies

Antibody 7 has zero receptors on any creature locus — the creature cannot sense its own antibody level, and no drive, reflex or metabolic process depends on it. This is the standard antibody-block design and is intentional for two reasons:

1. **Separation of concerns**: the chemistry handles the biochemical bookkeeping (antibody accumulation and decay), and the bacterium agent handles the game-state effect (dormancy and lifespan drain). Adding a creature-side receptor would duplicate functionality and risk interference between the two systems.
2. **Prevents overdose side effects**: because there is no receptor, a player can safely dose a creature with arbitrary amounts of Antibody 7 (via a community "immune booster" agent or via CAOS injection) without side effects. The excess simply suppresses Antigen-7 bacteria more decisively and then decays passively.

This is particularly valuable for Antibody 7 relative to the rest of the block: because the Pain symptom cascade of an Antigen-7 infection is so disruptive, a "cheat the infection" injection of antibody is especially appealing, and the receptor-free design means there is no mechanical penalty for doing so.

### Strategic / gameplay implications

- **No damage window at infection start**: the 1-unit threshold means reaction 99 fires from the very first antigen unit injected, so there is no unopposed-injury phase like Antigen 4's ~3-second delay. This, combined with Antigen 7's low summed injury gain of 252, makes Antigen-7 infections among the **least directly-damaging bacterial infections** in the antigen block per unit of active infection time — even though they *feel* like the harshest ones because of the Pain cascade.
- **Fastest per-second antibody build-up in the block**: reaction 99's 1-unit threshold, 3× amplification, and 105-tick Medium cadence combine to give the highest sustained antibody-per-second output at any given antigen injection rate. Antigen-7 bacteria tend to reach dormancy faster than Antigen-5 or Antigen-6 bacteria in per-tick terms, further limiting the duration of exposure.
- **Pain is the real punishment**: Antigen 7's direct tissue-damage load is second-lowest in the block, but the Pain cascade — limping, laying, accelerated metabolism, high Pain drive, and at extreme concentrations a circulatory stress response — makes Antigen-7 infections **the most visible and behaviourally disruptive** of all bacterial infections. A chronic Antigen-7 infection (a multi-bacterium colony) is the closest thing in Creatures 3 to a "my norn is suffering" scenario.
- **Involuntary egg-laying is a unique signature**: no other antigen in the block triggers involuntary egg-laying. A fertile female laying spontaneous eggs is a near-definitive indicator of an Antigen-7 infection (the alternative being other sources of high Pain, such as direct environmental injury).
- **Community "analgesic" agents are particularly attractive**: because direct Pain injection is a common community hazard mechanic and reaction 99 produces Pain from antigen, a community "vaccine" or "painkiller" agent that injects Antibody 7 directly bypasses the entire Pain cascade without side effects — the cleanest mechanical counter to a bacterial Pain source. Modders sometimes build Antibody 7 into "distress relief" items for this reason.
- **Mid-range starter dose with fast-ramp reaction**: the 46-unit starter gives newborns a buffer of a minute or so of protection before starter decay drops them below meaningful dormancy thresholds. After that, the creature is fully dependent on acquired immunity — which reaction 99 builds faster than any other antibody reaction in the block per second of infection time.

### Diagnostic visibility

Antibody 7 is not named in the Medical Pod's toxin panel (it is not a toxin), but it is visible via:

- **Chemistry panel (slot 109)**: direct numeric readout, showing the current antibody level. Values significantly above the 46-unit baseline indicate an active or recent Antigen-7 infection, with the magnitude roughly 3× cumulative antigen exposure (modulo decay timing).
- **Debug console**: `CHEM TARG 109` returns the current concentration on demand.
- **Bacterium behaviour inference**: if an Antigen-7 bacterium on a creature suddenly stops producing Pain (visible as Pain's chemistry-panel reading plateauing and beginning to decay at its Short half-life), the player can infer that Antibody 7 has crossed the bacterium's dormancy threshold.
- **Behavioural symptoms via Pain**: unlike Antibody 5, Antibody 7 production is symptomatically *highly* visible through its Pain by-product — limping gait, involuntary egg-laying in fertile females, accelerated metabolism, elevated Pain drive and (in youth+) circulatory stress. These symptoms are unique to Pain within the antigen block, so the behavioural signature is specific to Antigen 7 and cannot be confused with any other bacterial infection.

The clinical signature of a creature mid-infection is: immediately-climbing Antibody 7, slowly-rising Antigen 7, steadily-rising Pain, limping gait, possible involuntary eggs being laid, accelerated metabolism, elevated Pain drive and distress behaviour. The signature of a creature post-infection is: high Antibody 7 (decaying slowly), near-baseline Antigen 7, decayed Pain (decaying faster than antibody), normal gait, normal metabolism, and residual acquired immunity against reinfection for ~10 minutes.

Antibody 7 closes the antibody block's "Medium-cadence triplet" (chemicals 107, 108, 109 for Antibodies 5, 6, 7), which shares the same passive decay (2,481 ticks, Long) and the same "no receptors" design across all eight slots. The block's structure mirrors the antigen block 82-89 one-to-one:

## Summary

Antibody 7 is chemical 109 and the dedicated immune response product for [Antigen 7 (089)](089%20-%20Antigen%207.md), the **distress antigen**. It is produced by **reaction 99** (gene 92, `1× Antigen 7 → 3× Antibody 7 + 1× Pain`, Medium half-life of **105 ticks** — the fastest Medium-speed reaction in the entire antigen/antibody matrix) — one of the block's three 1:3 amplifying antibody reactions, with a **1-unit reactant threshold** that engages immune production from the very first unit of antigen injected (no delay phase). Every creature starts life with a **46-unit baseline concentration** set by gene 23 — a mid-range starter dose in the antibody block — and the chemical clears only via a **Long passive decay** of 2,481 ticks (~1 min 23 s per halving at 30 tps). It has **no receptors** on the creature — no locus reads its concentration, it affects no drive or reflex or metabolic process. Its entire game-mechanical effect is to be read by the external `bacteria.cos` agent: when the host's Antibody 7 rises above an Antigen-7-carrying bacterium's randomly-rolled dormancy threshold, the bacterium goes dormant and stops injecting antigen and toxin; and each tick of elevated antibody drains the bacterium's remaining lifespan by `antibody × 10`, so sustained high antibody eventually kills the bacterium outright. Because reaction 99 amplifies 3× at the block's fastest cadence and fires immediately on contact, Antigen-7 infections produce a characteristic **no-delay, steep-ramp** Antibody 7 profile from tick one of the infection. The Pain by-product drives **the broadest receptor cascade of any symptom chemical in the standard genome** — Pain drive (gain 207), involuntary egg-laying at Pain ≥48, limping gait at Pain ≥33, accelerated somatic metabolism at Pain ≥30, and circulatory stress at Pain ≥191 in youth+ — making Antigen-7 infections symptomatically the most behaviourally disruptive of all bacterial infections, even though the antigen's direct tissue damage is among the mildest in the block (four somatic injury receptors summing to 252). With no creature-side receptors, no antidote reactions and no direct physiological effect, Antibody 7 exemplifies Creatures 3's modular chemistry design: a pure "signalling" chemical whose meaning lives entirely in the communication between the biology genome (reaction 99, gene 23) and the external agent ecosystem (`bacteria.cos` reading `chem 109`). It is **the distress antibody** — paired with the block's most behaviourally punishing antigen, powered by the block's fastest per-second antibody production, and the closing entry of the eight-slot immune system that defines Creatures 3's bacterial-infection gameplay loop.
