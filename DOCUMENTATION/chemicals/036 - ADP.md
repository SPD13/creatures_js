# 036 - ADP

ADP (adenosine diphosphate) is the **discharged partner of ATP [35]** in the creature's energy economy. Biologically it mirrors real-world ADP: every time a carbon-metabolism reaction (glycolysis, β-oxidation, or aerobic respiration) phosphorylates ADP into ATP, a unit of ADP is "spent" as the input side of the charge; every time a downstream biosynthetic or ATP-consuming reaction spends ATP, a unit of ADP is handed back as the output. Together ATP and ADP form a closed pair: the total mass `[ATP] + [ADP]` in a healthy creature stays roughly constant, only the *ratio* between them moves depending on whether the creature is making or spending energy.

ADP is the cleanest example in the whole genome of a **pure substrate chemical**: it has **no emitter**, **no neuroemitter**, and **no receptor of any kind**. Nothing in the brain, the somatic tissue, or the immune tissue reads the ADP concentration — it is invisible to every behavioural and physiological feedback loop. Its only role is as a **reactant and product** in five chemical reactions, where it and ATP swap places to encode the charge/discharge state of the creature's energy pool. All the actual decision-making about that pool (clockrate throttling, death-from-energy-failure, hunger drives) is read off ATP instead.

Like the other metabolic substrates, ADP does not decay on its own — its half-life is "Very long" (decay rate 1, ≈9.07 × 10¹⁰ ticks). Unlike ATP, the genome does **not** seed a newborn creature with any ADP at all: the baby spawns with `ATP = 255/255` and `ADP = 0/255`, so the entire adenine-nucleotide pool starts fully charged. From that moment on, every ATP that is spent by a downstream reaction produces one matching unit of ADP, and every ATP that is re-synthesised by respiration, glycolysis or β-oxidation consumes one matching unit of ADP back. The total pool therefore self-assembles during the first few seconds of life as the creature's metabolism begins to tick.

## Role in Game Mechanics

### The ATP/ADP pair as a closed currency

Creatures 3 biochemistry deliberately treats ATP and ADP as a matched charge/discharge pair, exactly like a rechargeable battery. Every reaction that produces ATP consumes the same amount of ADP, and every reaction that consumes ATP produces the same amount of ADP:

```
    Energy    + 6× ADP ──(r20)──► 6× ATP                 (charge)
    Fatty Acid + 6× ADP ──(r17)──► Pyruvate + 6× ATP      (charge)
    Glucose   + 2× ADP ──(r18)──► Pyruvate + 2× ATP      (charge)

    Pyruvate  + 6× ATP ──(r7) ──► Fatty Acid + 6× ADP    (discharge)
    Pyruvate  + 2× ATP ──(r8) ──► Glucose    + 2× ADP    (discharge)
    ATP       + Decoupler ──(r84)──► ADP + Decoupler      (discharge, poison)
```

Neither ADP nor ATP decays on its own (both have decay rate 1.0), so the **sum** `[ATP] + [ADP]` in a healthy creature stays roughly constant near 1.0. What moves up and down is the ratio:

- A well-fed, well-oxygenated creature sits near `ATP ≈ 1.0, ADP ≈ 0`. This is the state the baby starts in — ATP seeded to 255, ADP seeded to 0.
- A starving or suffocating creature drifts towards `ATP ≈ 0, ADP ≈ 1.0`. This is the state that triggers the ATP LOC_DIE receptor (id 108).

### Why ADP has no receptors (and ATP has three)

It would be equivalent, mathematically, to build the "energy starvation" death trigger off either chemical: `ATP < 19` and `ADP > 236` describe essentially the same condition. Cyberlife chose to put every receptor on the ATP side:

- Receptor 52 reads ATP to throttle organ clockrate (REDUCE — slows everything when ATP is *low*).
- Receptor 60 reads ATP to nudge a specific reaction rate up when ATP is low.
- Receptor 108 reads ATP as the LOC_DIE trigger — kills the creature when ATP drops below threshold 19.

Putting the feedback all on one side of the pair is cleaner: the designers only had to tune three ATP receptors and could leave ADP as pure plumbing. It also makes biochemical sense — real-world cells likewise sense the ATP/ADP ratio mostly through ATP-binding proteins. The consequence for the player and for any modder is that **ADP is never "read" by the creature**. Changing the ADP concentration with `CHEM` has no direct effect on behaviour, drives, or lifespan; it only affects the rates of reactions 17, 18, 20 (which need ADP as substrate), and reactions 7, 8 (which produce it).

### Why ADP is needed as a substrate (and not just "free")

A naive design could have each ATP-producing reaction simply generate ATP from Energy/Glucose/Fatty Acid without requiring ADP on the input side. Requiring ADP does three useful things:

1. **It conserves the adenine nucleotide pool.** Because every production of ATP consumes the matching ADP, the creature cannot accidentally accumulate more total ATP+ADP than it started with (255 initial ATP + 0 ADP + whatever the designer injects later). This prevents runaway biochemistry where, for example, a creature with permanently abundant food would slowly build up a 5× or 10× ATP pool.
2. **It gates recharge on how depleted the creature is.** If ATP is already near 1.0, ADP is near 0, and so reactions 17/18/20 run slowly no matter how much food or oxygen is available. This is a clean biochemical way to implement "a full battery won't accept more charge" without any explicit cap.
3. **It couples ATP production to ATP consumption.** The only way to refill ADP is for another reaction to spend ATP first. In a well-fed, idle creature that spends almost no ATP, respiration naturally slows down to match, and the creature sits at a calm, low-activity metabolic setpoint. In an active, hungry creature that spends lots of ATP, ADP builds up and respiration races to refill it.

### Starting conditions and the first few ticks of life

Because the baby spawns at `ATP = 255, ADP = 0`, the very first ATP-spending reaction of the creature's life is what actually introduces ADP into the body. In practice this happens on tick one or two: any of the genome's many ATP-dependent biosynthetic reactions (protein synthesis, fat storage, etc.) fires, consumes some ATP, and releases the matching ADP. From there the creature's respiration can begin genuinely cycling the pool. The "full battery at birth" design means no matter how metabolically active the newborn is at tick zero, it has a full energy reserve to draw from while the ADP/ATP pool balances itself.

### ATP Decoupler and the poison path

Reaction 84 (`ATP + ATP Decoupler → ADP + ATP Decoupler`) is the one pathway in the base genome that converts ATP directly into ADP *without* doing any biological work. ATP Decoupler appears on both sides of the reaction, so it is a catalyst — it is not consumed, and so a single dose continues to bleed ATP into ADP until the decoupler itself decays (it has its own half-life) or is neutralised by Medicine one [92] via reaction 81. Because ADP is the *product* of this reaction, ATP Decoupler is also the fastest non-injected way to raise ADP in the body. The gameplay effect is on the ATP side (eventually ATP drops below the LOC_DIE threshold of 19 and the creature dies); the rising ADP is just the mirror image of that process.

### Why ADP does not decay

Like ATP, Energy [34] and Pyruvate [2], ADP has a "Very long" half-life (decay rate 1.0). This is deliberate: ADP is a *substrate* for the recharge reactions, not a *signal* that needs to time out. If ADP decayed on its own, the total adenine-nucleotide pool `[ATP] + [ADP]` would slowly shrink, and eventually the recharge reactions would run out of substrate even when the creature was fed and breathing — effectively a hidden, untreatable metabolic decline. Pinning the decay rate at 1.0 guarantees that every unit of ADP produced by an ATP-spending reaction is either cashed back in by a recharge reaction (the expected path) or stays parked in the pool waiting for one (the idle path), but cannot simply evaporate.

### Practical consequences for gameplay

- **Normal operation:** ADP stays pinned near 0 most of the time — ATP is the dominant side of the pair, so the ADP trace on the biochemistry viewer is a near-flat line that twitches upward only briefly during bursts of ATP-heavy activity.
- **Suffocation / low Oxygen:** reaction 19 stalls (no Oxygen means no Energy), which starves reaction 20 (the dominant ATP recharge) of its substrate. ATP is still being spent by the rest of the genome, so ADP builds up steadily. The ATP LOC_DIE receptor fires when ATP drops below 19 — which corresponds to ADP having climbed to roughly 236.
- **Starvation:** with no Glucose / Fatty Acid to feed reactions 17 and 18, and no Pyruvate to feed reaction 19 (and therefore reaction 20), none of the three recharge reactions can run. ADP climbs as ATP-spending reactions continue. Same LOC_DIE endpoint as suffocation.
- **ATP Decoupler poisoning:** the decoupler reaction directly converts ATP → ADP even while respiration is otherwise healthy, so ADP rises faster than in pure starvation / suffocation. Again the gameplay signal is the ATP drop, not the ADP climb.
- **Direct `CHEM` manipulation:** injecting ADP has no direct effect on the creature (no receptors read it) but it *does* top up the substrate pool for reactions 17/18/20 — so if a creature has somehow been losing ADP (through external injection of ATP, say, or via modded reactions), re-injecting ADP restores the recharge capacity. Conversely, draining ADP to 0 with `CHEM` stalls all three ATP-producing reactions until the normal ATP-spending reactions refill it, giving a brief metabolic pause.
- **Modding notes:** any new reaction that spends ATP should normally produce an equal amount of ADP on the output side (and vice-versa) to keep the conservation property intact. Reactions that break this rule (e.g. produce ATP from nothing, or destroy ADP) will gradually skew the pool and may interfere with the normal balance that receptors 52, 60 and 108 were tuned against.

### Summary of the ADP loop

```
  ATP-consuming reactions (r7, r8, r84, and every ATP-dependent gene via Energy)
                                    │
                                    ▼
                                  ADP pool
                                    │
                                    ├─ r17 (+Fatty Acid)  ─► 8 Pyruvate + 6 ATP
                                    ├─ r18 (+Glucose)     ─► 2 Pyruvate + 2 ATP
                                    └─ r20 (+Energy)      ─► 6 ATP       (dominant recharge)
                                    │
                                    ▼
                                  ATP pool  (read by receptors 52, 60, 108)
```

ADP is therefore the **silent half of the energy currency**: produced as a by-product every time ATP is spent, consumed as a substrate every time ATP is recharged, never read by any receptor, never emitted by any event, and never decayed by the passage of time. It exists solely to close the loop and make the ATP/ADP pair conserve mass — and in doing so it enforces the design invariant that every unit of metabolic work the creature does is paid for out of a finite, rechargeable pool.
