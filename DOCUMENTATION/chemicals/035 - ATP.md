# 035 - ATP

ATP (adenosine triphosphate) is the **universal chemical currency** of the creature's metabolism — the chemical that every energy-consuming gene actually spends. Biologically it mirrors real-world ATP: high-energy carbon metabolism (glycolysis, β-oxidation and aerobic respiration) phosphorylates ADP into ATP, and downstream biosynthetic pathways (fat synthesis, gluconeogenesis, and, via Energy, every other ATP-dependent reaction) spend it again, regenerating ADP. Together ATP and ADP [36] form a closed redox-like pair: the total mass of adenine nucleotide in the creature stays roughly constant, only the ATP/ADP *ratio* moves up or down depending on whether the creature is currently making or spending energy.

ATP is the second of the two "starvation" chemicals (alongside Energy [34]) whose depletion causes outright death. Like Energy it has **no emitter**, **no neuroemitter**, and **no behavioural-signal receptor** — its role is purely biochemical plumbing. But three receptors *do* monitor ATP: one that drives the global reaction clock-rate, one that throttles a specific reaction, and, critically, an immune-system **LOC_DIE receptor** (id 108) that kills the creature outright when ATP falls below ~7% of full. That last receptor makes ATP a load-bearing chemical: the creature's continued existence literally depends on keeping the ATP pool topped up.

Like the other metabolic substrates, ATP does not decay on its own — its half-life is "Very long" (decay rate 1, ≈9.07 × 10¹⁰ ticks). The genome seeds a newborn creature with a full 255/255 pool so that every ATP-dependent gene can fire from tick 0, and from that moment on the ATP pool is driven exclusively by the balance between the ATP-producing reactions (17, 18, 20) and the ATP-consuming reactions (7, 8, 84, and indirectly every other metabolic process via Energy).

## Role in Game Mechanics

### ATP/ADP as a closed currency pair

The Creatures 3 biochemistry is deliberately built around an **ATP ↔ ADP** pair that together act like a rechargeable battery. No reaction in the base genome creates or destroys adenine nucleotides — every ATP-producing reaction consumes a matching amount of ADP, and every ATP-consuming reaction produces the same amount of ADP back:

```
    Energy  + 6× ADP ──(r20)──► 6× ATP               (charge)
    Fatty   + 6× ADP ──(r17)──► Pyruvate + 6× ATP    (charge)
    Glucose + 2× ADP ──(r18)──► Pyruvate + 2× ATP    (charge)

    Pyruvate + 6× ATP ──(r7) ──► Fatty Acid + 6× ADP (discharge)
    Pyruvate + 2× ATP ──(r8) ──► Glucose    + 2× ADP (discharge)
    ATP       + Decoupler ──(r84)──► ADP + Decoupler (discharge)
```

The *sum* `[ATP] + [ADP]` in a healthy creature therefore stays roughly constant near 1.0; what moves is the **ratio** between the two. A well-fed, well-oxygenated creature sits near `ATP ≈ 1.0, ADP ≈ 0`; a starving or suffocating one drifts towards `ATP ≈ 0, ADP ≈ 1.0`, which is precisely the state that triggers the LOC_DIE receptor.

### Death by ATP failure (LOC_DIE)

Receptor 108 is the receptor that makes ATP a *load-bearing* chemical rather than just a metabolic bookkeeping chemical. It is a **REDUCE + DIGITAL** receptor targeting the Immune-tissue `LOC_DIE` locus, threshold 19 (≈0.07 concentration). The REDUCE flag inverts its response, so:

- While ATP ≥ 19, the receptor output stays low → LOC_DIE is *not* triggered → creature remains alive.
- When ATP drops below 19 (inverted output goes high, digital flag then snaps it to full), LOC_DIE fires → the creature dies.

An identical receptor (id 109) exists for Energy [34] (threshold 13). Together they define the base genome's two "energy-starvation" death conditions: run out of ATP directly, or run out of the upstream Energy that is used to make ATP. Either way the creature dies. This is why low-ATP states are an emergency, not a mild penalty: there is no gradual debuff, only a sudden death threshold.

### Global clock-rate throttle (receptor 52)

Receptor 52 is the most architecturally interesting ATP receptor in the genome. It writes to **RLOCUS_CLOCKRATE** — the special receptor locus that controls how fast a whole organ's reactions are ticked. With the REDUCE flag inverting its output, the receptor behaves like a *metabolic brown-out protector*:

- **High ATP (≥ nominal 0.75):** organ runs at its full speed.
- **Low ATP:** inverted output rises, pushing clockrate *down*, and every reaction in that organ slows — including the very reactions that consume ATP.

In other words, as the creature runs out of ATP the entire organ *automatically slows down to conserve it*. This is a key survival mechanism: instead of burning through the last scraps of ATP at full speed and crashing into the LOC_DIE threshold, the creature enters a kind of low-power state first, buying time for respiration or feeding to refill the pool.

### Reaction-rate feedback (receptor 60)

Receptor 60 watches ATP and, via its REDUCE flag, drives a specific reaction's rate *upward* as ATP falls. The nominal is 248 (near saturation) and the gain is relatively small (37), so this receptor produces a mild nudge rather than a strong swing — a "try harder to make ATP when it's scarce" signal layered on top of the much more dramatic clockrate throttle from receptor 52. In combination the two receptors form a classic dual-loop feedback: the global clockrate slows everything down when ATP is low, while this reaction-specific loop keeps the ATP-producing pathway relatively faster than the average reaction, biasing the creature towards recharging.

### ATP Decoupler: the uncoupling poison

Reaction 84 (`ATP + ATP Decoupler → ADP + ATP Decoupler`) mirrors the biology of real-world *uncoupling agents* (such as 2,4-dinitrophenol) which collapse the mitochondrial proton gradient and cause ATP to be wastefully dephosphorylated back to ADP without doing any biological work. In the game ATP Decoupler appears on both sides of the reaction, so it is not consumed — it only *catalyses* the destruction of ATP. Because ATP does not decay on its own, ATP Decoupler is one of the very few ways ATP can fall while respiration is otherwise healthy. Persistent exposure will therefore eventually push ATP below the LOC_DIE threshold and kill the creature outright.

Two medicinal reactions elsewhere in the genome neutralise ATP Decoupler once enough has been administered (Medicine one [92] removes ATP Decoupler via reaction 81), giving the player a way to rescue a poisoned creature before the ATP pool collapses.

### Why ATP does not decay

Like Energy [34], Pyruvate [2] and ADP [36], ATP has a "Very long" half-life (decay rate 1.0): nothing is lost to natural background decay. This is deliberate — ATP is a *substrate* for many reactions, not a *signal* that needs to time out. If it decayed on its own, the creature would slowly lose the ability to run any ATP-dependent reaction even when fed and breathing, and the closed ATP/ADP pair property would break. By pinning the decay rate at 1.0 the designers guarantee that every unit of ATP produced by respiration is either cashed in by a downstream reaction (the expected path) or destroyed by ATP Decoupler (the poison path) — it cannot simply evaporate.

### Practical consequences for gameplay

- **Normal operation:** ATP stays pinned near 1.0 most of the time — reactions 17/18/20 continuously top it up while reactions 7/8 (and every ATP-dependent biosynthesis elsewhere in the genome) drain it. The measurable ATP reading on a healthy creature is therefore a near-full bar that barely moves.
- **Suffocation / low Oxygen:** reaction 19 stalls (no Oxygen means no Energy), which starves reaction 20 (the dominant ATP producer) of its substrate. ATP begins to fall; receptor 52 throttles the organ clockrate down to slow the bleed, but once ATP drops below 19 the LOC_DIE receptor fires and the creature dies of asphyxiation.
- **Starvation:** with no Glucose / Fatty Acid to feed reactions 17, 18 and (through Pyruvate) 19/20, ATP production collapses from all three sides. The clockrate throttle buys a little time, but the endpoint is the same LOC_DIE death — biochemically equivalent to suffocation, just reached from the carbon side rather than the oxygen side.
- **ATP Decoupler poisoning:** even if Pyruvate, O₂ and food are abundant, ATP Decoupler (reaction 84) converts ATP to ADP continuously. Because the decoupler is not consumed, a single dose keeps bleeding ATP until the decoupler itself decays or is neutralised by Medicine one. This is one of the game's few fast-acting toxins against a well-fed creature.
- **Hunger-for-starch and hunger-for-protein drives** are ultimately gated on the ATP/ADP pair: eating food replenishes Glucose / Fatty Acid / Amino Acid, which rebuilds Pyruvate, which feeds respiration, which recharges ATP via reaction 20. Any failure mode that breaks this chain shows up first as a falling ATP trace in the biochemistry viewer.
- **CAOS-level tweaks:** injecting ATP directly with `CHEM` effectively bypasses the entire respiration pipeline and refills the "universal currency" in one step — useful for debugging metabolic genomes or for "revival" potions in mods. Conversely, draining ATP to 0 with `CHEM` is the fastest way to trigger the LOC_DIE ATP-failure death; injecting ATP Decoupler is a slower, poison-flavoured way to reach the same endpoint.

### Summary of the ATP loop

```
  Fatty Acid ──(r17)──► Pyruvate + ATP    ◄─── (r7)  Pyruvate → Fatty Acid (ATP-spent)
  Glucose    ──(r18)──► Pyruvate + ATP    ◄─── (r8)  Pyruvate → Glucose    (ATP-spent)
  Energy     ──(r20)──►            ATP    ◄─── (every other ATP-dependent gene via Energy)
                                    │
                                    ├─ (r84) +ATP Decoupler ─► ADP (Decoupler unchanged)
                                    │
                                    ├─ Receptor 52 ─► organ RLOCUS_CLOCKRATE (REDUCE)
                                    ├─ Receptor 60 ─► linked reaction rate (REDUCE, mild)
                                    └─ Receptor 108 ─► LOC_DIE (REDUCE + DIGITAL)
                                                       ↳ ATP < 19 ⇒ creature dies
```

ATP is therefore the **universal currency** of the creature's biochemistry: produced by three parallel pathways (glycolysis, β-oxidation, and respiration via Energy), spent by every ATP-dependent reaction elsewhere in the genome, monitored by a death-trigger receptor, throttled by a global clockrate receptor, and uniquely vulnerable to the ATP Decoupler poison. Together with its partner ADP it forms a closed charge/discharge pair, and together with Energy it forms the two-chemical "energy-starvation" failure mode that terminates a creature whose metabolism can no longer keep up with its needs.
