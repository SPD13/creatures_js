# 001 - Lactate

Lactate is a long-lived **somatic damage signal** in the creature's biochemistry. It is not a metabolite the body uses, nor a neurotransmitter — it is a waste product whose sole function is to accumulate inside the creature's tissues and report that accumulation to the injury system. Biologically, it mirrors real-world lactic-acid buildup: a by-product generated by sustained muscular effort that, if not cleared, contributes to tissue damage and fatigue.

In the standard Norn/Grendel/Ettin genome, Lactate is produced indirectly: physical activity (and other stressors) raise **Muscle toxin [81]** levels, and a fast chemical reaction continually converts that toxin into Lactate. A somatic **injury receptor** then reads the Lactate concentration and feeds it into the creature's injury locus, meaning a creature that has built up a lot of Lactate is treated by the body as "damaged". Because Lactate's natural half-life is effectively infinite (~9 × 10¹⁰ ticks, i.e. it never decays on its own), its role is specifically that of a **permanent injury marker** rather than a transient metabolic signal — once laid down, it can only be removed by a chemical reaction, a healing emitter, or an external dose from the environment/CAOS.

## Sources

| # | Mechanism | Gene | Organ / Tissue | Formula / Trigger | Rate |
|---|-----------|------|----------------|-------------------|------|
| 1 | Chemical reaction (id 81) | Gene 94, Baby onwards | Standard (genome-wide) | `1× Muscle toxin [81] → 1× Lactate [1]` | Short half-life (~24 ticks, decay 0.971) — converts muscle-toxin buildup into Lactate very quickly |

Lactate has no dedicated emitter in the standard genome. Any Lactate in a creature's body either comes from this reaction (via upstream Muscle toxin) or is injected externally (CAOS `CHEM`/`INJR` on agents, food containing Lactate, etc.). Muscle toxin itself has no emitter in the genome either; it is introduced by gameplay mechanisms (agent scripts, drugs, environmental hazards) and then steadily routed into Lactate by reaction 81.

## Role in Game Mechanics

### Somatic injury signalling

Lactate is the canonical **somatic injury chemical** on the Organ / Somatic tissue. The `RLOCUS_INJURY` receptor (locus 2) is read by the body's organ/tissue damage system: the higher the locus, the more the tissue is treated as injured. With threshold 0 and nominal 0, the receptor fires as soon as there is *any* Lactate in the creature's bloodstream, scaled by its gain (34). In practice this means:

- A brand-new creature starts at Lactate = 0 and is therefore uninjured.
- As Muscle toxin accumulates (from physical stress, combat, drugs, environment), the reaction converts it into Lactate, and the injury level rises.
- Because Lactate does not decay, the injury signal it produces is **monotonically non-decreasing** — it can only go down when some other gene, emitter, reaction, or player-driven CAOS removes the Lactate.

This makes Lactate a "scar chemical": it records cumulative damage rather than an instantaneous pain signal. This is distinct from chemicals like Pain or short-lived injury signals, which report transient events.

### Interaction with Muscle toxin

Reaction 81 consumes Muscle toxin at roughly 3% per tick (half-life ~24 ticks). Muscle toxin itself has a long half-life (~3024 ticks), so without the reaction it would linger harmlessly; with the reaction, most Muscle toxin is funnelled into permanent Lactate before it can decay away on its own. The functional result is that **any Muscle toxin the creature is exposed to leaves a permanent Lactate trail** — a clean way for the designers to turn a transient toxin into a lasting injury record.

Parallel receptor 183 also reads Muscle toxin directly on the same Organ/Somatic `RLOCUS_INJURY` locus (gain 26), so Muscle toxin causes both *immediate* injury signalling and *persistent* injury signalling (via its conversion to Lactate). Lactate is the "memory" of this exposure.

### Balancing and healing

Because Lactate's natural decay is essentially zero, designers and players have a few ways to reduce it:

- **Healing drugs / consumables** whose chemistry includes a Lactate-consuming reaction or an injector that pushes a negative amount (in practice, a reaction that pairs Lactate with another reactant and produces none).
- **CAOS scripts** on agents (medical kits, potions, cheats) that directly zero or reduce the creature's Lactate with `CHEM` / `INJR`-style commands.
- **Genetic engineering** — mutating the creature's genome to add a Lactate decay reaction, shorten its half-life, or remove the injury receptor.

Without any of these, Lactate acts as a hard, cumulative cost for being exposed to Muscle toxin over a creature's lifetime, contributing to the way older or more "battle-worn" creatures carry a higher baseline injury signal than young, untouched ones.

### Summary of the Lactate loop

```
 (physical stress / drugs / environment)
              │
              ▼
       Muscle toxin [81]
              │  reaction 81 (fast: half-life ~24 ticks)
              ▼
         Lactate [1]  ──────► RLOCUS_INJURY (Organ/Somatic), gain 34
              │
              │ natural decay: ~0 (permanent)
              ▼
          persists until externally cleared
```

Lactate therefore sits at the end of a deliberately one-way pipeline: stress flows in, permanent injury signal stays behind.
